HALACHA
When Moshiach comes, every Bais Chabad and shul will be trans-
ported to Eretz Yisrael and become an extension of the Beis Hamikdash.1
This is because, as mikdashei me’at, their ultimate function is identical,
namely, to illuminate their respective areas of influence with Elokus
and achieve a dirah betachtonim.
It is self-understood that in order for a Bais Chabad or shul to fulfill
its purpose and draw down the shechinah, fulfilling the mission the
Rebbe has placed upon us, the davening in that Bais Chabad or shul
must operate within the laws of Shulchan Aruch and the directives of
the Rebbe. It is therefore critical that we know what is expected of us
in that regard.
The purpose of this article is to offer guidelines with regard to issues
that may arise in a contemporary Chabad House or shul in relation to
davening.2

IMPORTANT POINTS
☞
It is essential that every Chabad House director and rabbi of a shul have a rov
moreh hora’ah b’poel who can answer the difficult shaylos that often arise. Very often
the answers may be counterintuitive and require a highly balanced approach, harmo-
nizing the needs of the Chabad House and hafatzah with the requirements of halachah.
☞
Furthermore, consideration must always be given to the type of shul in question.
A synagogue that bears the name Chabad or Chabad House is beholden by a certain
standard, as per the views and minhagim of our Rebbeim. A non-Chabad shul may have
different minhagim that must also be upheld.
☞
One must also bear in mind the direction the Rebbe constantly gave in relation
to shuls and nusach, namely, to avoid machlokes (see sidebar on p. 47). Machlokes is
an issur de’oraysa,3 and to avoid it by sometimes choosing to ignore a breach of a min-
hag, or even acting in accordance with another’s minhag, is the preferred option to an
argument.
☞
A shliach must also be careful with regard to setting precedents. You might insti-
tute some leniency that you are not really happy with, and then find it very difficult to
change in the future.
☞
Please note that some of the rulings and suggestions made here are a matter of
nuance and approach; other rabbonim may take slightly different approaches. That is
the way of Torah. When in doubt, please consult the rov from whom you usually take
advice.

must have been at least six men davening Shemoneh

six davened, Shemoneh Esrei may still be repeated
due to the din of pores al shema. According to this din,

to give them a chance to say Kedushah. Therefore,
provided six men davened Shemoneh Esrei together,
even if the rest of the ten arrived only after the six had
finished, any of the six may go to the amud and repeat
Shemoneh Esrei. Alternatively, if necessary, any of
the remaining four who have not yet davened may go

loud, and his loud Shemoneh Esrei is counted also as

IS THERE ANY PROBLEM WITH FOLLOWING
THE OLOM’ISHE MINHAG OF SINGING ADON

In a Chabad House, which follows, of course, Nusach Chabad,
one must be careful not to tamper with the nusach by adding
or subtracting anything from the nusach or seder hatefillah.
This includes Anim Zemiros, Eitz Chaim He, or the like, not-
withstanding the extraordinary kedushah of these tefillos.
Indeed, there is a Chabad tune for Anim Zemiros, a Chabad
tune for Adon Olam, and a Chabad tune for Ani Maamin. But
they do not form part of our nusach or seder hatefillah, and it
would not be right to insert them.
In one unforgettable farbrengen, the Rebbe encouraged

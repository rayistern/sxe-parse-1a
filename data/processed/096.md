## For Guests: Primary Obligation

### Where to Light

**The Rule:**
> "The menorah needs to be lit in your place of 'kvi'us' i.e. your home, and after shkia."

**Kvi'us = Fixed location**
- Your home
- Where you're staying
- Not wedding hall

**Timing:**
- After shkia (sunset)
- Not at hall during day

---

### Timing Question for Guests

**Specific Psak Received:**

**Scenario:**
- Going to wedding after plag (hamincha)
- But before shkia
- Not coming home till very late
- Close to Alos (dawn)

**Psak:**
> "It is better to do it then [close to Alos] (and get some b'nai bayis to be present) rather than after plag."

**Principle:**
- Better late at night at home
- With household members present
- Than early not at home
- Kvi'us (fixed location) matters

---

## For Chosson: Special Status

### Yichud Room as Kvi'us

**What the Chosson Was Told:**

**Psak from 2 Rabbonim:**
> "He should light in the yichud room as that is his 'kvius' for that evening."

**Reasoning:**
- Yichud room = his "place" that evening
- First moments of married life
- Establishes location
- Fulfills obligation properly

**Uncertainty:**
> "I am not sure if he will be in his apartment or not that night, or even if that makes a difference regarding the chosson."

**Questions Remain:**
- If going to apartment after: still yichud room?
- Does chosson's status change the rule?
- Need more clarification

---

### Proper Timing for Chosson

**The Rule:**
> "The proper time to do it is right after shkia, or anytime thereafter, like everyone else."

**No Special Time:**
- Same as everyone
- After sunset
- Standard halacha applies
- No wedding-specific exemption

---

## Lighting in the Wedding Hall

### The "Public Menorah" Question

**Parallel to Shul:**
> "To light a menorah in the hall, is probably also an inyan [worthy thing], I guess it is no worse than a shul where we light l'pirsumai nissa [to publicize the miracle]"

**Especially If:**
> "Certainly if some present will not light in their homes"

**Purpose:**
- Pirsumei nisa (publicizing miracle)
- For those who won't light at home
- Educational moment
- Communal celebration

---

### Can Guests Be Yotzei?

**The Critical Question:**
> "But you should ask a rov if one can be yotze from such a lighting."

**Rabbi's Doubt:**
> "It is very difficult for me to believe that it has more strength then a shul lighting or public lighting."

**Comparison:**
- Shul lighting: don't fulfill obligation
- Public lighting: don't fulfill obligation
- Wedding hall: likely same status

**Request:**
> "If someone got a psak otherwise, I would like to hear that."

**Implication:** Probably NOT yotzei, but confirm with Rov

## Practical Recommendations

### Based on This Discussion

**For the Chosson:**
1. Light in yichud room
2. After shkia (sunset)
3. Get psak for your specific situation
4. If going to apartment: clarify with Rov

**For Guests:**
1. Light at home (where staying)
2. After shkia
3. Better very late at home than early elsewhere
4. Don't rely on hall lighting

**In the Wedding Hall:**
1. Can light for pirsumei nisa
2. Good for those not lighting at home
3. Educational moment
4. But guests NOT yotzei
5. Make announcement: must light at home

## Timing Scenarios

### Wedding Schedule Options

**Scenario 1: Evening Wedding (After Shkia)**

**Works Well:**
- Chosson lights in yichud room (after shkia)
- Hall menorah during reception (pirsumei nisa)
- Guests go home, light there
- Timing aligns naturally

---

**Scenario 2: Afternoon Wedding (Before Shkia)**

**Challenges:**
- Chupah during day
- Can't light then (before shkia)
- Chosson lights in yichud room later
- Hall lighting during reception (if after shkia)
- Guests must light when get home

---

**Scenario 3: Very Late Wedding**

**Special Case:**
- Wedding goes very late
- Guests staying locally
- Light at accommodation after wedding
- Even if very late (better than not at home)

## Under Chupah: Analysis

### Why Probably Not Ideal

**Timing Issues:**
- Chupah often before shkia
- Can't light menorah before shkia
- Would need to wait
- Disrupts flow

**Focus Issues:**
- Each mitzvah deserves focus
- Mixing could diminish both
- Wedding is main simcha
- Menorah separate moment

**Logistical Issues:**
- Chupah already has elements
- Adding more complexity
- Fire safety under chupah?
- Practical concerns

---

### Better Alternatives

**Option 1: Yichud Room**
- Private moment
- Chosson & Kallah together
- First mitzvah as married couple
- Intimate and meaningful
- After shkia timing works

**Option 2: During Reception**
- After shkia
- During dancing or meal
- Brief ceremony
- Emcee announcement
- Educational moment
- Pirsumei nisa for all

**Option 3: Pre-Reception (Cocktail Hour)**
- If after shkia
- Before seated meal
- Guests gathered
- Still casual
- Good timing

## Announcements to Make

### If Lighting at Wedding Hall

**Critical Communication:**

> "This menorah lighting is for pirsumei nisa - publicizing the miracle of Chanukah. It does NOT fulfill each person's individual obligation to light menorah at home. Everyone must still light menorah at their place of lodging tonight after sunset."

**Why Important:**
- People might think they're yotzei
- Could skip lighting at home
- Educational responsibility
- Halachic clarity

## Bottom Line Summary

### The Essentials

**Chosson's Obligation:**
- Light in yichud room
- After shkia
- That's his kvi'us for the evening
- Get specific psak

**Guests' Obligation:**
- Light at their lodging
- After shkia
- NOT yotzei from hall lighting
- Even if late is better than wrong place

**Hall Lighting:**
- Permitted for pirsumei nisa
- Especially for non-observers
- Must announce NOT fulfilling obligation
- Educational moment

**Under Chupah:**
- Not addressed directly

- Focus issues
- Better alternatives exist

**When to Do Hall Lighting:**
- During reception
- After shkia
- Brief ceremony
- Announce clearly
- Then continue simcha

---

**Bottom Line:**
Chanukah wedding menorah lighting requires careful halachic consideration: chosson should light in yichud room (his "kvi'us" that evening per psak from 2 rabbonim) after shkia like everyone else; guests must light at their lodging after shkia—better very late at home than early elsewhere—and are NOT yotzei from hall lighting even though hall lighting permitted for pirsumei nisa especially for those who won't light at home. Lighting under chupah not directly addressed but timing problematic (chupah usually before shkia, can't light menorah before sunset) plus "mixing mitzvos" concern. Best practice: chosson lights privately in yichud room fulfilling obligation, then optional hall lighting during reception after shkia for pirsumei nisa with critical announcement that guests must still light at home. Hall lighting serves educational purpose for not-yet-religious guests ("some present will not light in their homes") but Rabbi Greisman doubts it "has more strength than shul lighting or public lighting" for fulfilling obligation—get specific psak if relying on it. Coordinate with venue for fire safety, vendors for timing, photographer for moment, and prepare announcement script emphasizing individual home lighting obligation. Consider enhancing entire wedding with Chanukah theme (dreidels, doughnut station, blue/silver decor, gelt favors) to inspire guests and create double simcha atmosphere. Consult local Rov for specific situation as "there were other solutions that rabbonim offered...complicated somewhat" requiring case-by-case guidance.

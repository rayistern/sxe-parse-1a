# Shluchim Forum: Water-to-Flour Ratio for Model Matzah Bakery

**Type**: Shluchim Exchange Forum Discussion
**Topic**: Correct flour and water proportions for model matzah bakery
**Date**: March 17, 2017
**Source**: http://www.shluchimexchange.com/en/forum/viewtopic.php?t=515274
**Key Question**: "Do you know the ratio?"

## Original Question - Avrohom Rimler, Plattsburgh, NY

**Question**: "ratio water to flour for model matzah bakery? Do you know the ratio?"

**Context**: Shliach preparing for model matzah bakery needs correct proportions

**Contact**: Rabbi Avrohom Rimler, Chabad of Plattsburgh, www.PSJews.com, 518-565-0677

## Response #1: Yitzchok Bergstein, Oak Park, IL

**Ratio Provided**: "just less than 50% water than you are of flour"

**Example**: 4 cups flour to 1.75 cups water

**Mathematical Breakdown**:
- 1.75 ÷ 4 = 0.4375 (43.75% water relative to flour)
- Slightly less than 50% as stated

## Response #2: Eli Dovid Strasberg, Media, PA

**Detailed Recipe**:
- 4 cups flour (approx. 2/3 white flour and 1/3 whole wheat)
- 1.5 cups water
- Yields: "enough for about 30 smaller-medium sized matzos"

**Ratio Calculation**:
- 1.5 ÷ 4 = 0.375 (37.5% water relative to flour)

**Important Caveat**: "More water than that, and it sticks"

**Critical Skill Note**: "(only catch is you need to know how to mix well)"

**Flour Composition**:
- 2/3 white = 2.67 cups white
- 1/3 whole wheat = 1.33 cups whole wheat

## Response #3: Yisroel Hecht, Sunnyvale, CA

**Practical System for Traveling Programs**:

"I would add that for those who do doing ongoing, traveling programs, what has worked well for me was to work it out backwards."

**Method**:
1. **Start with water**: Get small bottle of bottled water (~15oz)
2. **Calculate flour**: Figure out how much flour is needed "in comparison"
3. **Benefits**:
   - "Saves spills"
   - "Time refilling water"
   - "Problems if the water spills after you measure (there are bottles at hand)"

**Pre-Prep Strategy**:
- "Get cheap plastic containers and prefill the flour for as many as 10 programs in advance"
- "Beats making a mess measuring before programs"
- "Beats carrying around open flour bags"

## For Shluchim: Ratio Analysis and Implementation

### Comparing the Three Approaches

**Bergstein Method** (4 cups : 1.75 cups):
- Ratio: 43.75% water to flour
- Wetter dough
- Potentially easier to work with (more pliable)
- Risk: May be too sticky if not mixed well

**Strasberg Method** (4 cups : 1.5 cups):
- Ratio: 37.5% water to flour
- Dryer dough
- "More water than that, and it sticks"
- Requires good mixing technique
- Proven yield: 30 smaller-medium matzos

**Consensus Range**: 37.5% - 43.75% water relative to flour

### Recommended Starting Point

**For First-Time Programs**:
- Start with Strasberg's ratio (4:1.5 = 37.5%)
- Safer (less sticky)
- Can add water if too dry
- Harder to fix if too wet

**As You Gain Experience**:
- Can experiment toward 40-44% water
- More pliable dough may be easier for kids
- But requires better mixing technique

### Flour Type Matters

**Strasberg's Composition** (2/3 white, 1/3 whole wheat):
- White flour: More gluten, can handle more water
- Whole wheat: Absorbs more water, dryer result
- Balance provides workability plus authentic appearance
- Whole wheat gives brownish color (more "authentic" looking)

**If Using All White**:
- May need slightly less water
- Will be lighter colored

**If Using All Whole Wheat**:
- May need slightly more water
- Will be darker, denser

### The "Know How to Mix Well" Factor

Strasberg's caveat is critical: achieving proper consistency requires mixing technique.

**Proper Mixing**:
1. Create well in center of flour
2. Add water gradually to well
3. Mix from center outward
4. Knead briefly until cohesive (but under 18 minutes per matzah custom)
5. Dough should be smooth, not sticky, not cracking

**Signs of Problems**:
- Too sticky: Add flour tablespoon at a time
- Too crumbly: Add water teaspoon at a time
- Proper: Forms ball, slight tackle but doesn't stick to hands

### Hecht's Traveling Program Innovation

**Brilliant for Regular Programs**:

**Water Strategy**:
- Buy cases of small bottled water (16.9 oz standard, or find 15oz)
- One bottle = one batch's water
- No measuring, no spilling during transport
- Always fresh water
- Bottles stack/store easily

**Flour Pre-Prep**:
- Buy cheap plastic containers with lids (dollar store)
- Pre-measure flour for each program
- Label: "Program 1," "Program 2," etc.
- Stack in storage
- Grab and go

**Benefits**:
- Setup time at venue: ~2 minutes (open container, open water bottle)
- No mess at venue
- No transporting open flour bags
- No forgotten measuring cups
- Professional, organized appearance

**Calculation for Hecht Method**:
If using 15 oz water bottle:
- 15 oz = 1.875 cups
- At Strasberg ratio (37.5%): 1.875 ÷ 0.375 = 5 cups flour
- At Bergstein ratio (43.75%): 1.875 ÷ 0.4375 = 4.29 cups flour

**Recommendation**: Round to 5 cups flour per 15oz water bottle (37.5% ratio)

### Scaling for Different Group Sizes

**For 30 kids** (Strasberg's yield):
- 4 cups flour + 1.5 cups water
- Or: 5 cups flour + 15oz water bottle

**For 60 kids**:
- 8 cups flour + 3 cups water
- Or: 10 cups flour + two 15oz water bottles

**For 100 kids**:
- 13.3 cups flour + 5 cups water
- Or: 16-17 cups flour + three 15oz water bottles

### Testing Before Event

**Critical**: Test your specific flour with your chosen ratio AT LEAST one week before event

**Testing Process**:
1. Make small batch (1 cup flour, 0.375 cups water)
2. Mix, knead, roll
3. Note consistency
4. Adjust ratio if needed
5. Document for future years

**Variables Affecting Results**:
- Flour brand (protein content varies)
- Humidity (affects flour moisture)
- Altitude (affects dough texture)
- Water temperature (room temp works best)

### Storage of Pre-Made Dough

**Generally Not Recommended**:
- Dough should be made fresh
- Can't refrigerate (condensation = water contact over time)
- Matzah must be made from fresh-mixed dough

**Exception**:
- Can pre-mix for demo portion (show how it's done)
- Then make fresh batch for kids to use

## The Jokes

### 1. Dam Tzefardeyah (Unintelligent Frog)

**Q:** What do you call an unintelligent frog?

**A:** A Dam tzefardeyah!

**Type:** Wordplay on the plague
- Dam = blood (first plague)
- Tzefardeyah = frogs (second plague)
- Dam (also) = damn (curse/unintelligent)

---

### 2. Haggadah Wordplay

**Q:** Why do we have an Haggadah at Passover?

**A:** So we can Seder right words.

**Type:** Pun
- Seder = order of service
- Say-der = say the
- "Say the right words"

---

### 3. The Matzah Sandwich Exchange

**Setup:**
> "It was Passover and two Jewish attorneys, Saul and David, who worked downtown met at a food court to have lunch. Saul and David proceeded to produce matzah sandwiches from their briefcases and began to eat."

**Conflict:**
> "One of the waiters in the food court marched over and told them, 'You can't eat your own food in here!'"

**Punchline:**
> "Saul and David looked at each other, shrugged their shoulders, and then exchanged matzah sandwiches."

**Type:** Clever solution
- Rule: Can't eat your own food
- Loophole: Exchange, so it's not "your own"
- Lawyer humor
- Following letter not spirit of rule

---

### 4. The Blind Man and the Matzah

**Setup:**
> "A blind man is sitting on a park bench. A Rabbi sits down next to him. The Rabbi is chomping on a piece of matzoh. Taking pity on the blind man, he breaks off a piece and gives it to the blind man."

**Punchline:**
> "Several minutes later, the blind man turns, taps the Rabbi on the shoulder and asks, 'Who the heck wrote this?!!'"

**Type:** Misunderstanding
- Blind man reads by touch (Braille)
- Matzah has holes/texture
- Thinks it's written text
- Funny confusion

---

### 5. Abe and the Boss

**Setup:**
> "Abe goes to his boss and says 'we're doing some heavy house cleaning at home tomorrow for Pesach and my wife needs me to help with the attic and garage, moving and hauling stuff.'"

**Boss's Response:**
> "'We're shorthanded, Abe' the boss replies, 'I just can't give you the day off.'"

**Punchline:**
> "'Thanks, boss,' says Abe, 'I knew I could count on you.'"

**Type:** Reversal
- Expected: Abe disappointed
- Reality: Abe happy to be refused
- Wants to avoid cleaning
- Got excuse

---

### 6. Good Sermon Like Matzah (Version 1)

**Q:** How is a good sermon like a piece of matza?

**A:** They both should take less than 18 minutes!

**Type:** Self-deprecating rabbi humor
- 18 minutes = time for matzah to rise into chometz
- Long sermons = like chometz (inflated)
- Keep it brief
- Practical advice

**Note:** This joke appears twice in the collection (repeated)

---

### 7. Chopped Liver and Charoses Warning

**Setup:**
> "Just in time for this year, a group of leading medical people has published data indicating that seder participants should NOT partake of both chopped liver and charoses."

**Punchline:**
> "It is indicated that this combination can lead to Charoses of the Liver!"

**Type:** Pun
- Cirrhosis = liver disease
- Charoses = Passover food (apple/nut mixture)
- "Charoses of the liver"
- Medical humor

**Note:** This joke also appears twice in the collection (repeated)

---

### 8. Morris and the Divorce Threat

**Long Story Summary:**

**Setup:** Morris calls son Benny in NY:
> "Benny, I have something to tell you. However, I don't want to discuss it. I'm merely telling you because you're my oldest child, and I thought you ought to know. I've made up my mind, I'm divorcing Mama."

**Son's Reaction:** Shocked, tries to get information

**Morris's Response:** Won't discuss, too painful, 54 years together, has appointment with lawyer

**Son's Plea:**
> "Dad, don't do anything rash. I'm going to take the first flight down. Promise me that you won't do anything until I get there."

**Morris Agrees:** "Well, all right, I promise. Next week is Passover. I'll hold off seeing the lawyer until after the Seder."

**Daughter Calls:** Gets same news, also promises to fly down with her family

**The Reveal:**
> "After hanging up from his daughter, Morris turns to his wife and says, 'Well, it worked this time, but what are we going to do, to get them to come down next year?'"

**Type:** Elaborate scheme
- Manipulative parents
- Want kids to visit for Passover
- Fake divorce threat
- Gets results
- Already planning next year
- Relatable family dynamics

---

### 9. Moses and the Exodus Story

**Setup:**
> "A little boy once returned home from Hebrew school and his father asked, 'What did you learn today?'"

**Boy's Answer:**
> "The Rabbi told us how Moses led the children of Israel out of Egypt."

**Father:** "How?"

**Boy's Creative Version:**
> "Moses was a big strong man and he beat Pharoah up. Then while he was down, he got all the people together and ran towards the sea. When he got there, he has the Corps of Engineers build a huge pontoon bridge. Once they got on the other side, they blew up the bridge while the Egyptians were trying to cross."

**Father's Shock:**
> "Is that what the Rabbi taught you?"

**Punchline:**
> "The boy replied, 'No. But you'd never believe the story he DID tell us!'"

**Type:** Children's perspective
- Real story too fantastical
- Kid "improves" it with modern military
- Even made-up version more believable than truth
- Miracles hard to explain
- Kid's honesty

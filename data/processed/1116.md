## 1) To Charge or Not to Charge

### Strategy A: "Suggested Donation"

**Approach:**
> "We offer a 'suggested donation.' Almost everyone chooses the paying option! People want to feel comfortable eating as much as they want and drinking as much as they want - and they do!"

**Benefits:**
- Optional but encouraged
- Removes guilt about consumption
- Most choose to pay
- Dignified approach

---

### Strategy B: Deposit System

**Experience Shared:**
> "When we didn't charge we had a load of RSVPs and half didn't show up. We are on a college campus."

**Solution:**
> "The next year we charged with the option to click 'I can't afford it now'. (we only charge about $25, it's really just a deposit to make sure we get cancellations or don't waste food)."

**Result:**
> "Anyway, that first year we began 'charging' we got more people than before."

**Benefits:**
- Reduces no-shows
- Prevents food waste
- "Can't afford" option preserves dignity
- $25 = deposit not full cost
- Actually increases attendance

---

### The Rebbe's Guidance: Yechidus to Chief Rabbis

**Source:**
> "In the yechidus to the chief rabbis Shapira and Eliyahu of Israel."

**Caution:**
> "Not so simple what lessons one can derive for our public Seders in the states."

**Access:**
> "Read the transcript of the talk Hisvadyos 5746 vol 2 page 851 available on otzar770"

> "Or listen here at about 1:42: https://www.chabad.org/therebbe/article_cdo/aid/554285/jewish/26-Adar-II-5746-Yechidus-Rabonim-Haroshim.htm"

---

### The Rebbe's Six Points

**1) Not Associated with Specific Group:**
- Don't make Sedarim associated with any specific group or party
- Broad community appeal

**2) Avoid Embarrassment:**
- Don't make communal Seder where some will be embarrassed to come
- This would imply they don't have their own Seder
- Rather: Rabbi doing his Seder in public space inviting everyone

**3) Hosted by Local Rabbis:**
- Should be hosted by Rabbis of each city and town and neighborhood
- Local leadership essential

**4) Rabbis Should Be Reimbursed:**
- Rabbis should be reimbursed for expenses
- Don't expect them to cover costs

**5) Rebbe Will Be Mistatef:**
- Rebbe will be mistatef (participate/contribute)
- Get some others to
- Philanthropists in America will be happy to sponsor if organized by chief rabbinate

**6) Comfortable Flexibility:**
- People should feel comfortable staying as much or as little as they like
- No pressure to remain entire time

## 2) When to Start

### The Dilemma

> "There is always a question when to start after nacht [nightfall] or before."

**Important:**
> "If starting early the Rabbanim gave guidelines how to do it (like what should go first)."

---

### Strategy A: Start Early

**Rationale:**
> "For most ppl w/ young children, to hope that they'll even do that after 8:20 is overly wishful thinking."

**Additional Benefits:**
- Starting early also allows you to put on tefillin with the men before Shabbos
- Helps curb Chilul Shabbos v'yom tov
- Makes for a calm, attentive audience at the Seder
- A shorter Shulchan Oreich (meal section)
- Which in turn helps more people stay for the whole seder

---

### Strategy B: Start by Nacht (After Nightfall)

**Approach:**
> "The minhag is to start by nacht and to show how its supposed to be."

**Compromise Solution:**
> "You could announce at the beginning that we are going to do the kiddush at the earliest time appropriate, but will precede it with songs divrei torah etc. We will move quickly after the kiddush and not spend too much time in discussion and song, as we have already covered it."

**Benefits:**
- Halachically ideal
- Educational (showing proper way)
- Songs/divrei Torah fill time before kiddush
- Move quickly after since already covered material
- Demonstrates flexibility within halacha

## 3) Rush Through the Seder or Not

### The Reality

**Diversity of Opinion:**
> "אין דעותיהן שוות [Their views are not equal/people have different opinions] and you may hear complains from both opposites."

**Key Factor:**
> "It depends on the crowd coming."

---

### Strategy A: Different Nights, Different Paces

**Suggestion:**
> "Can do what night fast another night slow."

---

### Strategy B: Start Slow, End Fast

**Approach:**
> "Start slow, rush by end (in a way that everyone can see that it's rushed) and announce in the beginning that due to families with young children, we can't do it too long. I invite everyone to our 2nd night seder."

**Benefits:**
- Quality beginning (most memorable)
- Visible rushing (not hidden)
- Announce reason (transparency)
- Invitation to second night (deeper option)

---

### Strategy C: Authentic and Alive

**Philosophy:**
> "People who are coming to a Seder that only starts at 8:45 or 9:00 at night, aren't coming for brisket.... They're coming to experience a Seder at my table, which is authentic, alive and real."

**Approach:**
> "They want to receive my warm - full of life - experience, the way I received it from my parents, grandparents and great grandparents. So I don't feel a need to rush."

**Result:**
> "Our experience has been that people don't eat very much by the time we get to Shulchan Oirech."

**Conclusion:**
> "But ultimately, this differs from place to place."

---

### Strategy D: Two Different Sedarim

**Approach:**
> "If doing 2 nights of public sedarim, advertising one Seder more geared for families with young children and the other for adults."

**Benefits:**
> "Much easier time adapting style when the crowd is more similar, plus people know what to expect."

## 4) Washing Hands

### The Challenge

**Logistics:** Large group washing hands twice during Seder

---

### Efficient System

**Setup:**
> "We arrange as many washing stations as possible, and appoint a designated 'WASHER' at each station that pours the water on everyone's hands."

**Benefits:**
> "This reduces the time very significantly, especially if the 'washer' is the right person."

**Tznius Consideration:**
> "(Mitzad darkay tznius, you should consider having separate male/female 'washers', or ask a rov if a child could do the washing...)"

---

### Halachic Note

**Important Distinction:**
> "There is a deference between rochtzo and urchatz, rochtzo is not a minhag, it is a din that you make a berocho asher kidshonu bemitzvosov."

**Meaning:**
- Urchatz (first washing): Custom, no blessing
- Rochtzah (second washing): Law, with blessing
- Don't confuse the two

## 5) Matzah and Wine Quality

### Matzah Quality Matters

**Experience:**
> "We bought better matzah this year, Montreal for our families and Landau for Seders, both were fresh and people did eat a lot more matzah and honestly many more people fulfilled the mitzvah because the matzah was enjoyable..."

**Lesson:**
- Fresh matzah significantly impacts compliance
- People eat more when quality is better
- Actually fulfilling mitzvah (not just nibbling)
- Worth the investment

**Recommended:**
- Montreal matzah (for families)
- Landau (for Seders)
- Fresh is key

---

### Wine Quality and Variety

**Observation:**
> "On a similar note, I noticed having a variety of good quality wines (nothing too crazy expensive) causes people to actually drink four cups..."

**Lesson:**
- Variety encourages drinking
- Good quality (not necessarily expensive)
- Result: People fulfill four cups mitzvah
- Not just token sips

## 6) Maggid - Making It Interesting

### A) Tell the Story

**The Basic Approach:**

> "Tell the story of Yetzias Mitzraim from Yosef to Mattan Torah. Believe it or not, most Jews today don't know the story. And you can make it very exciting if you prepare."

**Why This Works:**
- Most attendees don't know full story
- Naturally engaging narrative
- Can be dramatic
- Preparation key

---

### C) Cup Pyramid Building

**Activity:**
> "We put a bunch of plastic cups on each table. When it comes to a part in hagada about yiddin working, each table has to make a cup pyramid. First table wins."

**Benefits:**
- Physical activity
- Team building per table
- Competition element
- Relates to slavery/building theme

---

### D) Jokes Every 18 Minutes

**Planning:**
> "Planning this year (from a chat) print pesach jokes. Put face down on each table. Every 18 minutes another table reads their joke."

**Variation:**
> "Could do the same with inspirational words."

**Why 18 Minutes:**
- Connects to matzah (18 minutes before chametz)
- Regular intervals
- Keeps energy up
- Multiple tables participate

---

### E) Frogs in the Pond Game

**Activity:**
> "Dollar tree sells frogs in the pond game. At makkos, see with table flicks most frogs in the pond."

**Details:**
> "1. Bought this game for every table: https://www.dollartree.com/Jumpin-Frogs-Games/p397187/index.pro"

**Implementation:**
> "We sang the Ma nishtana and every table had 3 players trying to flick as many frogs into the tub before we finished the ma nishtana."

**Educational Connection:**
> "We did a similar game while that same thought 'why focus on maakos' came to mind.. We therefore explain that the focus is Hashems miracles.."

---

### F) JLI "How Jew You Do" Card Game

**Resource:**
> "We used the JLI 'How Jew You Do' card game and spread them out to the 'captains' of every table. Throughout the evening - at random times - we had different people stand up and read from them. Worked out very well."

**Ordering:**
- JLI game Amazon with Prime Shipping: http://amzn.to/2DCjvq4
- Bulk discounts available at: www.myjli.com/game

**Implementation:**
- One card set to each "table captain"
- Random times throughout evening
- Different people read
- Keeps everyone engaged

---

### G) Makos Masks (Headbanz Game)

**Activity:**
> "1. We purchased these Makos masks: https://www.amazon.com/gp/product/B01NC0XCHM/ref=oh_aui_detailpage_o01_s00?ie=UTF8&psc=1"

**Game:**
> "When we got to the Makos we called on the children and played the 'headbanz' game. One by one we put a Mask on the children and they had to ask the crowd questions with a yes/no answer until they figured out what Makoh they were wearing."

**Benefits:**
- Children actively involved
- Audience participation
- Educational (learning plagues)
- Interactive and fun

---

### H) Numbered Reading Cards

**System:**
> "#2. I have photocopied maggid and cut it into portions for people to read. I have them pasted onto numbered index cards with Hebrew on one side and English on the other."

**Pre-Seder:**
> "Before the Seder while people are milling about, I have someone walk around offering people if they would like to participate by selecting a reading to recite during the Seder in the language of their choice."

**During Maggid:**
> "During maggid I call out number 1, they read and I might offer a short vort, then I call out number 2."

---

### Benefits of Numbered Card System

**A. Random Movement:**
> "The reading is randomly moving around the room. It keeps it interesting."

**B. No Declining:**
> "No one has to decline to read."
- Voluntary selection beforehand

**C. Practice Time:**
> "They can practice."
- Cards given in advance

**D. Choice of Length:**
> "They can choose a length they are comfortable with, or something on which they have an insight to offer."

**E. Control Important Readings:**
> "You can make sure that shodayim nochonu goes to someone who will read it in Hebrew."

---

### I) Around the Room Reading

**Simple Approach:**
> "Go around and everyone read a paragraph."

**Cultural Variation:**
> "Ask people to read in their cultural language, Spanish, Russian, farsi (everyone else can read on their own quietly)."

**Educational Moment:**
> "You can take a minute to imagine a seder in that respective country, 20 years ago, 100 years ago, etc"

**Benefits:**
- Everyone participates
- Celebrates diversity
- Educational context
- Historical perspective

**Posted:** Sat Mar 04, 2017 23:11 pm ET

**Subject:** A Purim Thought based on Likutei Diburim

**Source:** Likutei Diburim 371b and other sources

**Theme:** Assimilation, forgetfulness, and return

---

## Section 1: Inadvertent Slide

### Core Message

**Central Teaching:**
> "Purim celebrates the reversal of assimilation."

**Not Sudden Fall:**
- Not deliberate abandonment
- Inadvertent process
- Gradual slide
- Little by little

---

### Historical Context: Jews in Persia

**Before Haman:**
- Talmud teaches slide began well before Haman
- Jews in highest enclaves
- Commerce and government
- Most exclusive social clubs
- When king gave party, Jews on guest list

**Accommodations:**
- Royal caterers served kosher food
- Jews free to observe dietary laws
- Surface-level observance maintained
- Appeared integrated successfully

---

### Mordechai's Warning

**His Reasoning:**
> "Natural social boundaries are important to foster pride in distinct cultural identities and these would erode at a one-hundred-eighty day party."

**Philosophy:**
- Minority populations must preserve identity
- Lest swallowed by majority
- Must interface with majority
- But never integrate fully

**Timeline:**
- Might take a year
- Might take decades
- Might take generations
- But unchecked long-term integration
- Leads inexorably to assimilation

---

### No One Intends It

**Reality:**
> "No one sets out to erode religious or cultural pride. No Jew seeks to deliberately stamp out our traditions. It occurs inadvertently. Little by little, boundaries fade, divisions disappear and identity is lost."

**Persian Jews' State:**
- By no means assimilated (yet)
- But process had begun
- But for Mordechai and handful of followers
- No one took notice

---

## Section 2: Forget

### Gematria Analysis of "Shushan"

**Exploration Method:**
- Explored name Shushan for message
- Tried numeric value of Hebrew letters
- Failed to decipher message
- Divided number by two
- Hit pay dirt

**Calculation:**
- Four letters of Shushan = 656
- 656 ÷ 2 = 328
- 328 in Hebrew letters: Shin, Chaf, Chet
- Spells: **Shachach** = "to forget"

**Message:**
> "In other words, if you forget two or more times, you reach the *Shushan state*."

---

### The Process of Forgetfulness

**How It Begins:**
- Set out to broaden horizons
- Establish contacts, friendships, familiarities
- With differing religions and cultures
- Don't intend to erode own heritage

**Immersed in Discovery:**
- Thrill of newness
- Might on occasion neglect single Shabbat
- Or Passover
- First time: feel guilty
- Second or third time: becomes habit

**Result:**
> "If we engage in the process of forgetfulness, we reach the state that our ancestors reached in Shushan."

---

### Responsibility for Forgetfulness

**Never Set Out to Reach That Stage:**
- Yet responsible for having reached it

**Talmudic Teaching:**
- We are responsible for Torah knowledge we forget
- Because forgetfulness results from idleness
- When choose to sit idly rather than study
- Responsible for consequences of choice

**Same Applies to Assimilation:**
> "When we choose to pursue foreign cultures, rather than our own, we are responsible for our resultant assimilation."

---

## Section 3: Return

### Meaning of "Responsible"

**Word Play:**
> "Responsible means response-able."

**Implication:**
- We are able to respond
- To reality of assimilation
- By reversing it
- Which is precisely what Mordechai set out to do

---

### Mordechai's Response to Haman

**After Haman's Announcement:**
- Rallied nation to renew commitment
- Throughout year of impending doom
- Despite continued contacts with Persian community
- No Jew, even inadvertently, slipped away
- From Jewish faith and practice

**Source:** Torah Ohr, Megilas Esther 91b

---

### When You Really Care, You Remember

**Analogy: Forgotten Birthday**
> "When a husband forgets his wife's birthday, she is justifiably upset. Though he didn't deliberately set out to forget, if he had really cared, he would have remembered."

**Application to Faith:**
- When strive to be true to faith
- Become intensely aware of behavior
- Don't trip up even by mistake
- Care creates vigilance

---

### Story: Rabbi Nochum from Chernobyl

**Decided to Allow Evil Inclination One Victory:**
- Set out to commit a sin
- Heard people gossiping
- Joined the group
- As approached, fellow shouted: "Don't gossip, it's a sin"

**Group's Response:**
- Someone retorted: speaking ill of this individual is actually a Mitzvah
- When Rabbi heard this
- Stepped away
- "He was in the business of committing a sin that day, not a Mitzvah"

**His Day:**
- From one sin opportunity to next
- Every time set out to commit sin
- Somehow recast in guise of Mitzvah
- Was spared each time

**Lesson:**
> "When one is truly attached, heart and soul, to G-d, one does not trip up even deliberately. How much more so inadvertently."

---

## Section 4: Response

### G-d Responds Measure for Measure

**Teaching:**
> "Our sages taught that G-d responds to our behavior measure for measure. He treats us precisely as we treat Him."

**In Case of Purim:**
> "This is astoundingly accurate."

**Source:** Babylonian Talmud, Megillah 12b

---

### Haman's Lottery

**The Process:**
- Chose date for annihilation by throwing lots
- Didn't set out to kill in month of Adar
- Lot just happened to fall on that month

**Haman's Joy:**
- Moses passed away in Adar
- Took it as sign he would succeed
- In terrible design

---

### The Parallel

**Jews' Assimilation:**
- Didn't deliberately set out to assimilate
- Allowed it to happen inadvertently

**Haman's Date Selection:**
- Didn't set out to choose auspicious day
- Found it inadvertently

**Measure for Measure:**
- Same pattern of inadvertence

---

### Haman's Miscalculation

**The Reversal:**
- Haman miscalculated
- Moses also **born** in month of Adar
- Month ostensibly bad for Jews
- Turned out to work in our favor

**Complete Reversal:**
- Ancestors reversed course
- From assimilation to enthusiastic faith
- So their misfortune reversed
- They worked to prevent inadvertent slip ups
- So G-d prevented inadvertent fortune of Haman's lottery
- Date Haman chose turned out to be good fortune and protection

## For Shluchim: Using This Sermon

### Themes to Emphasize

**1. Inadvertent Assimilation:**
- Most relevant to modern audience
- Many experiencing this
- Not deliberate rejection
- Gradual process
- Relatable struggle

**2. Responsibility and Response:**
- Not just guilty
- Response-able
- Can reverse the trend
- Empowering message

**3. Measure for Measure:**
- G-d's response mirrors ours
- When we commit fully
- Protection comes "inadvertently"
- Theological depth

---

### Contemporary Applications

**Modern "Shushan State":**
- Interfaith social circles
- Professional integration
- Cultural assimilation
- Gradual boundary erosion
- "Just this once" mentality

**Process of Forgetfulness:**
- Skipping Shabbat "occasionally"
- "Too busy" for Jewish events
- Children's activities on Jewish holidays
- Becomes habit over time

**Path to Return:**
- Renew commitment like Mordechai's generation
- Even without immediate crisis
- Proactive rather than reactive
- Reverse the trend now

---

### Teaching Points

**Gematria Hook:**
- Engaging for intellectual audience
- Shushan = 2x Forget
- Memorable teaching device
- Shows depth in text

**Stories Work:**
- Rabbi Nochum from Chernobyl
- Forgotten birthday analogy
- Make abstract concrete
- Help audience connect

**Historical Perspective:**
- Not first generation to face this
- Pattern throughout history
- Jews in Persia parallel to today
- Continuity and relevance

**Opening:**
- Purim celebrates reversal of assimilation

**Body:**
1. Inadvertent Slide (historical context)

**Conclusion:**
- We too can reverse course

- Mordechai's example for today
- Response-able = able to respond

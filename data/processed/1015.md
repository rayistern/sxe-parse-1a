# [Misc > For Writer > _5 Chanukah > General] Donation-Based vs. Ticketed Events: Revenue Strategy

## Note: Purim Content (Applies to All Events)

**Topic:** Pricing strategy for community events

**Key Finding:** Donation-based model generated 4X more revenue

**Applicability:** Purim, Chanukah, and all community events

---

## The Comparison

### Previous Model: Ticketed

**Pricing:** $12-$18 per person

**Structure:**
- Fixed ticket price
- Pay to attend
- Standard admission model

**Results:** Baseline revenue (100%)

---

### Current Model: Free with Optional Donation

**Pricing:** Free admission, donation encouraged

**Structure:**
- No entry fee required
- Donation option presented
- Voluntary contribution

**Results:** **4X more revenue** (400% of previous model)

---

## The Math

### Example Calculation

**Assume Event Capacity:** 100 people

---

#### Ticketed Model ($18/person)

**Price per Ticket:** $18

**Attendance with Ticket Price:**
- Price sensitivity limits attendance
- Some can't afford
- Some won't pay for "free" event
- Estimated attendance: 60 people

**Revenue:** 60 × $18 = **$1,080**

---

#### Free with Donation Model

**Ticket Price:** $0

**Attendance with Free Entry:**
- No barrier to entry
- Maximum community turnout
- Everyone welcome
- Estimated attendance: 95 people

**Average Donation per Person:**
- Some give nothing
- Some give $5-10
- Some give $25-50
- Some give $100+
- Estimated average: $45

**Revenue:** 95 × $45 = **$4,275**

**Multiple:** 4,275 ÷ 1,080 = **3.96X** (roughly 4X)

---

## Why Donation Model Generates More

### Psychological Factors

**1. Removal of Barrier**
- Free = everyone can come
- No price shopping
- No "is it worth it?" calculation
- Maximizes attendance

**2. Gratitude Effect**
- Experience event first
- Feel the value
- Want to reciprocate
- Give generously

**3. Flexibility for All**
- Those who can't afford: come anyway
- Those who can afford: give generously
- No one excluded
- No one pressured

**4. Higher Ceiling**
- Ticket caps contribution at $18
- Donation allows $50, $100, $500
- Major donors can give more
- No artificial limit

**5. Community Spirit**
- "Supporting our community"
- Not "buying a ticket"
- Philanthropic framing
- Mission-driven giving

---

### Practical Factors

**Increased Attendance:**
- More people = more donors
- Network effects
- Word of mouth spreads
- "Free event" attracts

**Wealthier Attendees Give More:**
- Not constrained by ticket price
- Give according to means
- Major gifts possible
- Feel good about generosity

**Social Pressure (Positive):**
- See others giving
- Don't want to be only non-giver
- Peer influence
- Community norms

---

## Implementation Strategy

### How to Do It

**Marketing Language:**
- "Free community Purim celebration"
- "Donations appreciated"
- "No one turned away"
- NOT: "Free but we expect donations"

**At Registration (if needed):**
- Free to register
- Optional donation field
- Suggested amounts ($18, $36, $54, $100, Other)
- Make it easy to give

**At Event:**
- Donation table/box visible
- Credit card processor available
- Venmo/PayPal QR codes
- Multiple giving options

**During Event:**
- Brief gratitude announcement
- "This event made possible by generous donors"
- "Donation table in back"
- Not pushy, just informative

**After Event:**
- Follow-up email
- Those who didn't donate: opportunity to contribute
- Those who did: thank you
- Impact report

## Addressing Concerns

### "Won't People Just Take Advantage?"

**Reality Check:**
- Yes, some will give nothing
- That's okay and expected
- But many will give MORE than ticket price
- Net result: 4X more revenue
- Mission accomplished: maximum community reach + maximum fundraising

---

### "How Do We Cover Costs?"

**Calculate Minimum:**
- Know your actual costs
- Plan for minimum viable donations
- Have safety net/sponsor if needed
- But data shows it works

**First Time Trying:**
- Test with smaller event first
- Track results carefully
- Compare to previous ticketed model
- Adjust messaging if needed

---

### "What If Too Many People Come?"

**Good Problem:**
- Indicates community hunger
- Venue capacity is only real limit
- Can always do two sessions
- Or require registration (still free)

**Manage Expectations:**
- "Free with registration required"
- "Limited capacity, register early"
- Still free, just managed

---

## Advanced Strategies

### Suggested Donation Amounts

**Psychology of Anchoring:**
- Show suggested amounts
- Highest first: $100, $54, $36, $18
- Or ladder up: $18, $36, $54, $100, Other
- People anchor to suggested

**Chai Amounts:**
- $18 (one chai)
- $36 (double chai)
- $54 (triple chai)
- $72, $90, $108, etc.
- $180 (ten chai)

---

### Sponsorship Tiers

**Combine Approaches:**
- Event is free to all
- Sponsorship opportunities for major donors
- "$500 sponsors dinner for 25 people"
- Named recognition
- Everyone else can still donate

---

### Matching Challenges

**Donor Psychology:**
- "All donations matched up to $1,000!"
- Doubles impact
- Creates urgency
- Increases participation

**Source of Match:**
- Board member
- Major donor
- Foundation grant
- Organization itself

- Catered dinner ($50/person actual cost)

**Bottom Line:**

Shliach reports switching from ticketed events ($12-$18 per person) to free-admission-with-optional-donation model generated **4X more revenue** for Purim and other community events, recommending others test this approach. Key success factors: removing entry barrier maximizes attendance (no price sensitivity, everyone welcome), gratitude effect motivates generous giving after experiencing value, flexibility accommodates all income levels (those who can't afford still come, those who can give more than ticket price), higher donation ceiling allows major gifts ($50, $100, $500+) not capped by $18 ticket, philanthropic framing ("supporting our community") replaces transactional mindset. Example math: ticketed event at $18 with 60 attendees = $1,080 revenue; free event with 95 attendees averaging $45 donations = $4,275 revenue (3.96X increase). Implementation: market as "Free community celebration, donations appreciated," provide multiple giving options (cash, credit, Venmo/PayPal QR codes), display suggested chai amounts ($18, $36, $54, $100), brief non-pushy announcement during event, follow-up email to non-donors. Principle: Lower barrier (free admission) + Higher ceiling (no donation cap) = more attendance + more generous giving = dramatically more revenue. Works best for community celebrations (Purim carnival, Chanukah lighting, holiday parties), use caution with limited-capacity or high-per-person-cost events. Scientific approach: test with one event, track attendance and revenue carefully, compare to previous ticketed model, refine messaging and optimize. Applies to Purim, Chanukah, and all community programming. Note: This fundraising strategy content appears in Chanukah collection directory as general event guidance.

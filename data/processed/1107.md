## Introduction: Miriam's Leadership

**Talmudic Teaching:**
> "There were three excellent leaders for Israel. They were Moses, Aaron and Miriam."

**Distinction:**
- Moses and Aaron: Leaders for all people
- Miriam: "The teacher of the women"

**Source:** Talmud, Taanit 9; Targum Mincha 6:4

---

## Historical Context

**Who is Miriam? Look at the Time Period:**

### The Brutality

**Biblical Description:**
> "And they [the Egyptians] embittered [vayemarreru, from the root mar] their lives with hard work" (Exodus 1:14)

**Nature of Brutality:**
- Both spiritually and physically
- Giving men the work of the women and vice versa
- Break their spirits
- Designed to demoralize

### The Decree

- Stargazers said to kill baby boys
- Pharaoh issued death decree
- Miriam's father, the respected leader, instructs all to divorce their wives

---

## Miriam's Name - Qualities of Character

### Mar = "Bitterness"

**Meaning:**
- She saw the bitterness of the people
- Felt it deeply
- Connected to their pain

### Meri = "Rebellion"

**Meaning:**
- Rebelled against the slave mentality
- Against the status quo
- Refused to accept despair
- Challenged assumptions

---

## Instances of Miriam's Courage / Rebellion

### 1. As a Midwife Summoned to Pharaoh

**The Story (Exodus 1:8-17):**

> "There arose a new king over Egypt...And he spoke to the Hebrew midwives, the name of one was Shifrah (Yocheved) and the name of the other Puah (Miriam)."

> "And he said, 'When you act as a midwife to the Hebrew women and see them on the birthing stool, if it is a son, you shall kill him, but if it is a daughter she shall live.'"

> "The midwives feared G-d and did not act as the king of Egypt commanded them...And it came to pass because the midwives feared G-d that He made them Houses..." [Kohanim and Leviim]

**Miriam's Response:**
- **Five years old** at the time
- First decree was to midwives to kill babies
- They didn't listen
- Miriam said: **"Woe to this man when G-d avenges him"**
- Pharaoh was furious!

**Courage:**
- Age 5 speaking truth to power
- Prophesied punishment
- Unafraid of consequences

---

### 2. Reprimanding Father's Divorce Decree

**The Situation:**
- Miriam's father (Amram, respected leader)
- Instructed all men to divorce wives
- Worse than Pharaoh's decree

**Miriam's Argument:**
> "She told the men you are doing worse than pharaoh, killing both girls and boys"

**Logic:**
- Pharaoh: Killing only boys
- Father's decree: No children at all (boys OR girls)
- More devastating impact

**Her Prophecy:**
> "She said a prophecy: 'my mother will give birth the leader of the Jewish people.'"

**Outcome:**
- Father listened
- Took back wife
- Others followed

---

### 3. Moshe's Birth - Faith Despite Doubt

**The Challenge:**
- Born 3 months early
- Parents said to her: **"Where is your prophecy??"**
- Looked hopeless
- Put him in basket

**Miriam's Response:**
> "Miriam stayed to watch"

**Against Slave Mentality:**
- "She was not ready to give up"
- "Waited to see what would happen"
- **DID NOT GIVE UP FAITH**
- Batya came, etc.
- Grew up in the palace

**Biblical Text (Exodus 2:3-4):**
> "The woman conceived and bore a son, and [when] she saw him that he was good..."

> "And when Yocheved could no longer hide him, she took for him an ark made of reeds...and put the child in it and laid it in the rushes by the river's bank. And his sister (Miriam) stood far away, to see what would be done to him."

---

## The Kior (Basin) in the Mishkan

### The Mirrors

**Miriam's Role:**
- Invigorated and strengthened Jewish women
- Told them not to give up
- Encouraged them to have **MIRRORS** with them
- Beautify themselves to attract husbands
- Be drawn together

**Credit:**
> "The credit of the continuity of the Jewish nation is attributed to the STRENGTH of the Jewish women - they CONQUERED THEIR DEPRESSION"

### Mishkan Donation

**The Offering:**
- Women brought mirrors
- Moshe hesitant to use it
- Seems vain...

**Hashem's Response:**
> "Hashem says no, use it for the kiyor [basin], because of what the women did for their husbands, for the continuity of the Jewish nation"

**Significance:**
- Mirrors built into holy vessels
- Women's initiative honored
- Faith and hope memorialized

---

## Shores of Red Sea

### After the Splitting

**The Events:**
- 10 plagues
- Leave Egypt
- Splitting of the sea
- Then: Sang praise to Hashem

**Biblical Text (Exodus 15:20-21):**
> "And Miriam the prophetess, the sister of Aaron, took a tambourine in her hand; and all the women went out after her with tambourines, dancing. And Miriam answered them, 'Sing to the L-rd...'"

---

### The Tambourines - The Question

**What's Strange:**
> "Where did they get tambourines from??"

**Context:**
- Hasty exodus
- Left in rush
- Slaves with few possessions
- No time to pack luxuries

---

### The Answer

**Rashi (Exodus 15:20):**
> "The righteous women of that generation were confident that the Holy One Blessed be He would make miracles for them, so they prepared tambourines and dances."

**Miriam's Influence:**
- Never lost her vision
- Knew slavery would end
- Did not give in to slavery
- Did not lose hope
- **Inspired the women** to conquer their depression and to believe

---

### Miriam's Strength

**Legacy:**
> "This is the strength of Miriam - and a strength passed on to ALL Jewish women; to break out of our limitations, out of status quo"

**Reward:**
- Miriam's well traveled with them in desert
- When she had tzaraas
- Everyone waited 7 days for her
- Nation honored her as she honored them

---

## Inextinguishable Spark of Optimism

**Talmudic Teaching (Sotah 11b):**
> "In the merit of the righteous women of that generation, our forefathers were redeemed from Egypt."

**Why Their Merit:**
- In the merit of their faith
- They did not give up
- Even prepared for the day they would be redeemed
- **Knowing it would happen**

---

## Application: Emulating Miriam

### The Question

> "How can we emulate Miriam, a true feminist, and break out of comfort zones?"

---

### The Challenge

**Status Quo Comfort:**
> "It's always easier not to 'rock the boat', just stay as is..."

**But:**
> "We have the strength from Miriam to break out of our routine and do something greater...break out of our comfort zone"

---

### The Risk

**Reality:**
> "There's always risks...takes strength and courage to break out of what we usually do"

---

### The Method

**Process:**
> "Acknowledge the fear...then do what has to be done, and the strength grows."

**Steps:**
1. Acknowledge the fear
2. Do what has to be done anyway
3. The strength grows through action

- From past to present
- From story to application

### Key Messages

**For Women:**
- You have Miriam's strength
- Faith overcomes despair
- Rebellion against status quo is holy
- Breaking comfort zones is growth
- Your optimism can save the nation

**For All:**
- Question assumptions
- Don't accept slavery (physical or mental)
- Prepare for redemption
- Faith expressed in action
- Courage grows through use

### Teaching Approaches

**Chronological:**
- Miriam at 5 (midwife)
- Miriam as teen (father's decree, Moshe's birth)
- Miriam as adult (mirrors)
- Miriam at redemption (tambourines)
- Miriam in desert (well, tzaraas)

**Thematic:**
- Rebellion (against Pharaoh, against father, against despair)
- Faith (prophecy, waiting, tambourines)
- Leadership (women's teacher, inspiration)
- Legacy (merit, strength passed down)

**Question-Driven:**
- What made Miriam different?
- Where did tambourines come from?
- Why mirrors in Mishkan?
- How can we be like Miriam?

### Discussion Questions

**Personal Application:**
1. What "slave mentality" do you need to rebel against?
2. Where have you given up that you shouldn't have?
3. What tambourines are you preparing? (What redemption do you believe in?)
4. What comfort zone should you break out of?
5. What fears do you need to acknowledge and then act despite?

**Historical:**
1. Why was Miriam specifically "teacher of women"?
2. What was unique about the women's faith?
3. How did mirrors become holy vessels?
4. Why did everyone wait for Miriam?

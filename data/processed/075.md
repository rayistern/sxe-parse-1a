## Question #1: Pre-Lighting Restrictions

### The Issue

> "There are halachos that govern the time and place of lighting the Menorah, and I think we need to be careful with ourselves and our parties, public lightings etc."

**Statement:**
> "From the time of lighting until we light we are forbidden to eat and learn"

---

### The Question

> "Q: Does that mean you can't eat a Latke? or is it a meal? al kol ponum a sit down meal is for sure osur"

**Translation:** At minimum, a sit-down meal is definitely forbidden

---

### Practical Application

**What's Forbidden:**
- Sit-down meals (definitely)
- Formal eating
- Learning Torah

**Gray Area:**
- Snacking on latkes
- Standing/mingling food
- Light refreshments

**Recommendation:**
Either schedule food AFTER lighting, or keep it to light snacks before

## Question #2: Bringing Menorahs to Parties

### The Concern

> "When you are at a party, I heard some encourage people to bring their Menorahs to light at the event. (a very Israeli thing to do) isn't everyone mechuyav to light at home?"

---

### The Problem

> "And if they light with you, you are discouraging them from doing it when they get home, additionally they are MAKING A BROCHO LEVATOLA"

**Issues:**
1. Everyone obligated to light at HOME
2. Lighting at party doesn't fulfill obligation
3. If they make brocho at party, it's a **brocho levatola** (wasted blessing)
4. Discourages home lighting

---

### Israeli Custom Note

**Context:** Israeli practice differs
- In Israel, one menorah per household often
- Might light together as family at event
- Different from Chabad/Diaspora practice

**Chabad Practice:**
- Each person lights at home
- Public lighting is SEPARATE
- Public doesn't fulfill personal obligation

---

### Recommendation

**Don't encourage bringing menorahs to party**

**Instead:**
1. Public lighting for pirsumei nisa
2. Encourage everyone to ALSO light at home
3. Announce clearly: "This doesn't fulfill your home obligation"

## Question #3: Timing for Public/Shul Lightings

### The Rule

> "Public lightings and shul lightings need to be after plag hamincha"

**Source:** See File 074, guideline #13

---

### Famous Example

> "(that is why R Shmuel Butman needs to fly in a helicopter on Friday)"

**The Challenge:**
- Friday: Must light before Shabbos
- Must also burn 30 min after nightfall
- Solution: Light very early (Plag) with lots of fuel
- Helicopter allows traveling between locations fast enough

## Question #4: Making Brocho Together

### The Question

> "Can everyone in the crowd make the bracha together? or it must be one person?"

---

### The Answer

**Based on Vaad Guidelines (File 074):**
- ONE person makes brocho OUT LOUD
- Others listen and answer "Amen"
- Similar to shul lighting
- If multiple honorees lighting, announce ALL names BEFORE brocho

**Not a communal brocho** where everyone says it together

## Kashrus Policy Question

### The Situation

> "We have a big Chanukah event every year for between 300 - 500 people. We have included more food stalls which we run either on our own or through Beth Din approved vendors."

---

### The Question

> "The issue came up this year of whether it is ok to have both milchig and fleishig for sale - obviously not near each other etc but still at the same event."

---

### Beth Din Response

> "The Beth Din said that it's not at all a problem, it happens at other communal events, just make it very clear, and if we want to be extra careful we should ask people to be conscious of it on the fliers, announcements etc."

---

### Asker's Concern

> "Does my theoretically entertaining the idea make me the subject line of the next Nshei Newsletter (unless Rabbi Posner saves me) or does an event that size allow for that?"

**Translation:** Will I get in trouble for this? Is it okay for large events?

---

### Practical Guidelines

**It's Permitted When:**
1. Event size justifies variety (300-500 people - yes!)
2. Clear separation
3. Clear signage
4. Announcements made
5. Fliers note it
6. Beth Din approved

**Make It VERY Clear:**
- Sign each station (Dairy/Meat)
- Announce at beginning
- Put on flier
- Different colored tablecloths
- Physical distance between stations

## Menorah Near Christmas Tree

### The Questions

**Question 1:**
> "We're trying to put up a menorah & event in a new town. The town only allows displays (religious or otherwise) in one area. There is a gigantic xmas tree there. What are your thoughts about putting a menorah at that location? We could put it off to the side, say 20 or 30 ft away, but it would still be the only display other than tree at that location. would it be perceived that we're 'acknowledging' or 'competing with' chogo shelohem?"

---

**Question 2:**
> "Is it totally mufrach to attend a conservative temple's annual menorah lighting? even just to make an appearance?"

---

**Question 3:**
> "Anyone have any advice on how to incorporate the ikkar shlichus of kabolas pnei moshiach tzidkeinu into the mivtzoim of chanukah?"

---

### Analysis

**Menorah Near Tree Issue:**

**Concerns:**
- Appearing to acknowledge their holiday
- Looking like we're "competing"
- Association with avodah zarah symbols

**Considerations:**
- If it's the ONLY permitted location, may be necessary
- 20-30 feet separation helps
- Menorah has own significance
- Not about them, about us
- Consult local Rav

**Possible Approach:**
- If unavoidable location, proceed
- Make it clearly separate
- Focus on Jewish message
- Don't reference tree
- Emphasize religious freedom for ALL

---

**Conservative Temple Attendance:**

**"Mufrach"** = inappropriate/not fitting

**Considerations:**
- Depends on purpose
- Building relationships? Maybe
- Endorsing their approach? No
- Just appearance without participation? Gray area
- Consult Mashpia

## Incorporating Moshiach Theme

### The Answer: Multiple Approaches

> "There are many ways how to incorporate Moshiach into Mivtza Chanukah"

---

### In Print Materials

**Slogan:**
> "Lighting the way to redemption"

**Works Because:**
- Light theme fits Chanukah
- Forward-looking
- Hopeful message
- Not preachy

---

### Gematria Connection #1

**Moshiach = Roshei Taivos:**
> משיח : מדליקין שמונה ימי חנוכה
> "Madlikin Shmone Yemei Chanukah"
> (We light eight days of Chanukah)

**Letter Breakdown:**
- מ = Madlikin (we light)
- ש = Shmone (eight)
- י = Yemei (days of)
- ח = Chanukah

---

### Gematria Connection #2

> משיח : גמטרי' נס גדול הי' שם
> "Moshiach = Gematria of Nes Gadol Haya Sham"
> (A great miracle happened there)

**Numerical Value:**
Both equal 358

---

### The Number Eight

> "'Eight' is indicative of Moshiach and Geulah the time of infinity and as such Neros Chanukah einom betailim Leolom"

**Kabbalah Concept:**
- 7 = natural world (7 days of creation)
- 8 = beyond nature (supernatural)
- 8 = Moshiach era
- Therefore Chanukah lights (8 days) = eternal, not nullified

---

### Source to Study

**Reference:** Sicha of 22 Kislev (exact year not specified in document)

**Topics:** Oil, Chassidus, and Geulah connection

## Practical Takeaways

**Before Lighting:**
- [ ] Don't serve sit-down meals
- [ ] Light refreshments okay if necessary
- [ ] Announce: "Please refrain from eating until after lighting"

**Regarding Home Menorahs:**
- [ ] Don't encourage bringing to event
- [ ] Clearly announce: public lighting ≠ home obligation
- [ ] Remind everyone to light at home
- [ ] Provide times for home lighting

**Timing:**
- [ ] Public/shul: After Plag HaMincha
- [ ] Must burn 30 min past nightfall
- [ ] Friday: Special planning needed

**Brochos:**
- [ ] One person makes brocho out loud
- [ ] Others listen and answer Amen
- [ ] Not communal recitation

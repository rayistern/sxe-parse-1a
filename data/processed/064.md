## Expanded Guidance for TV Interviews

### Before You Call the Station

#### 1. Develop Your Angle

**Questions to Answer:**
- What makes THIS year's Chanukah celebration different?
- What's unique about your event?
- How does it serve the broader community?
- Is there a human interest story?
- Any notable guests or participants?
- Connection to current events?

---

#### 2. Newsworthy Angles for Chanukah

**Possible Hooks:**
- **Record-Breaking:** Largest menorah, most participants, etc.
- **First-Time Event:** New public lighting location, new community
- **Community Service:** Feeding homeless, hospital visits, etc.
- **Interfaith:** Bringing communities together
- **Children:** Kids' perspectives, cute factor
- **Historical:** Local Jewish history connection
- **Resilience:** How community overcame challenges
- **Unity:** Bridging divides
- **Innovation:** Virtual components, creative adaptations
- **Celebrity/Dignitary:** Notable participant
- **Survivor Story:** Holocaust survivor lighting menorah
- **Military:** Service members celebrating
- **Public Safety:** Firefighters, police participating

---

### Contacting the Station

#### Initial Call

**Who to Call:**
- News desk/assignment desk
- Community calendar editor
- Local news producer

**When to Call:**
- 2-3 weeks before event (for planning)
- 3-5 days before (for immediate coverage)
- Morning hours (9-11 AM typically good)

---

#### Phone Script Template

**Opening:**
> "Hello, my name is Rabbi/Mrs. [Name] from [Chabad Name]. I'm calling about a community Chanukah celebration that would make a great human interest story for your viewers."

**The Hook (Choose One):**
- "We're expecting over [X] people at our public menorah lighting..."
- "We have a [unique angle] that hasn't been done in [location] before..."
- "Mayor [Name] will be lighting our menorah alongside..."
- "We're bringing together people of all faiths to celebrate religious freedom..."

**The Ask:**
> "Would you be interested in covering this event, or perhaps interviewing me about Chanukah and its message of religious freedom?"

**Flexibility:**
> "I'm available for an interview at your studio, or we can arrange for you to come to the event. Whatever works best for your schedule."

**Follow-Up:**
> "Can I email you more details and a press release?"

---

### Preparing for the Interview

#### Key Messages (Pick 2-3)

**Universal Themes:**
1. **Religious Freedom:** Chanukah celebrates right to practice religion
2. **Light Over Darkness:** Universal message of hope
3. **Community:** Bringing people together
4. **Miracles:** Can happen even in dark times
5. **Children:** Passing traditions to next generation
6. **Resilience:** Small group defeating mighty empire
7. **Unity:** Coming together despite differences

---

#### Interview Structure

**Introduction (30 seconds):**
- Who you are
- What Chanukah is
- Why it matters

**Main Message (1-2 minutes):**
- Your newsworthy angle
- Community impact
- Universal message

**Call to Action (15 seconds):**
- Where and when event is
- "Everyone is welcome"
- How to learn more

---

#### Dos and Don'ts

**DO:**
- ✓ Smile and be warm
- ✓ Speak in soundbites (10-15 second quotes)
- ✓ Use universal language ("religious freedom" not "mitzvah")
- ✓ Show enthusiasm
- ✓ Mention event details naturally
- ✓ Have props (menorah, dreidel) if in studio
- ✓ Invite reporter and viewers to event
- ✓ Practice key messages beforehand

**DON'T:**
- ✗ Use too much Hebrew/Yiddish
- ✗ Go into too much detail
- ✗ Assume viewers know basics
- ✗ Be overly serious
- ✗ Criticize other religions
- ✗ Make it seem exclusive to Jews
- ✗ Ramble without clear points
- ✗ Look nervous (practice!)

---

### Sample Interview Responses

#### "What is Chanukah?"

**Good Answer:**
> "Chanukah is the Jewish Festival of Lights. Over 2,000 years ago, a small group of Jews fought for the right to practice their religion freely. They won against all odds, and when they went to rededicate their temple, they found only enough oil to light the menorah for one day - but miraculously it lasted for eight days. That's why we light candles for eight nights - to celebrate religious freedom and the power of light over darkness."

**Why It Works:**
- Clear and concise
- Universal themes
- Story format
- Explains symbolism

---

#### "What's the significance of the menorah?"

**Good Answer:**
> "The menorah has eight branches plus a helper candle. Each night we add another candle, spreading more light. It reminds us that a little bit of light can dispel a lot of darkness. We place our menorahs in windows so the light shines outward - the message isn't just for us, it's meant to inspire everyone to bring more light into the world."

**Why It Works:**
- Visual description
- Progressive action
- Universal message
- Public dimension

---

#### "Tell us about your event."

**Good Answer:**
> "This [day], we're lighting a [size]-foot menorah at [location] at [time]. We're expecting [number] people from across the community - people of all backgrounds coming together to celebrate religious freedom. [Mayor/dignitary] will be joining us, and we'll have hot latkes, music, and activities for kids. Everyone is welcome - you don't need to be Jewish to appreciate the message of bringing light into darkness."

**Why It Works:**
- Specific details
- Inclusive language
- Notable participants
- Clear invitation

---

### After the Interview

#### Follow-Up

**Immediately After:**
- Thank the reporter/anchor
- Provide contact info
- Offer to be resource for future stories
- Take photos if possible

**Before It Airs:**
- Find out when it will air
- Promote on your channels
- Encourage community to watch and share

**After It Airs:**
- Thank station publicly (social media)
- Share clip on your platforms
- Send thank you note
- Save for next year's pitch

---

### Building Relationships

#### Long-Term Strategy

**Not Just Chanukah:**
- Offer to be Jewish resource year-round
- Pitch other holiday stories
- Share community service initiatives
- Respond to breaking news when relevant (as Jewish perspective)

**Stay in Touch:**
- Add reporter to mailing list
- Send occasional updates
- Invite to special events
- Congratulate on awards/career moves

## Why TV Coverage Matters

### Benefits

**Immediate:**
- Event publicity
- Community awareness
- Legitimacy/credibility
- Increased attendance

**Long-Term:**
- Name recognition
- Relationship with media
- Content for website/social media
- Donor cultivation (they see impact)
- Community positioning

**Spiritual:**
- Pirsumei nisa (publicizing miracle)
- Reaching unaffiliated Jews
- Spreading light
- Kiddush Hashem

---

**Bottom Line:**
Getting TV coverage is simpler than many think: call the news desk, have an interesting angle, and be prepared with clear, universal messages. Focus on what makes your event newsworthy, speak in soundbites, and remember you're representing Judaism to a general audience. Build relationships with reporters year-round, not just before holidays. Most importantly: be yourself, be enthusiastic, and let your genuine passion for sharing Chanukah's message shine through.

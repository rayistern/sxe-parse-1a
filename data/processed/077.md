# [Misc > For Writer > _5 Chanukah > General] Forum Discussion: Is the Menorah a Religious or Secular Symbol?

## Shluchim Exchange Forum Thread

**Topic:** How to explain that menorah displays on public property are legal

**Forum Source:** http://www.shluchimexchange.com/en/forum/viewtopic.php?t=50961

**Key Question:** "How do you explain to someone that the Menorah is not a religious symbol rather a display of freedom?"

---

## The Opening Question

### The Dilemma

> "How do you explain to someone that the Menorah is not a religious symbol rather a display of freedom, I myself need to convince myself of this and need some help.."

**Context:**
- Shaliach needs to explain legality to authorities
- Even asker struggles with the concept
- How to reconcile: menorah IS religious but ALSO legally permissible

---

## Response #1: We Follow the Court's Ruling

### The Pragmatic Answer

> "I dealt with this last year in a very big way & the answer I got from Chasidim & Shluchim was that **we do not necessarily believe that its secular. The US court does!**"

**The Logic:**

**We Live in the US:**
- Follow all the laws here
- Don't go through red lights
- Don't smoke pot
- Pay taxes
- **So follow this law too**

**We're Not Claiming It's Secular:**
- Every Tom, Dick & Harry knows menorah is in our seforim
- From the very beginning
- We'd be foolish to tell anyone it's secular

**The Reality:**
> "Vus zul men ton that the court ruled in our favor, using the wrong argument?"

**Translation:** What should we do - the court ruled in our favor, even if using reasoning we don't agree with?

---

### Key Insight

**It's Not About What We Believe:**
- To us, it's a religious symbol
- No question about that
- But the court's ruling allows it
- We work within the legal framework provided

**We're Not Being Dishonest:**
- Not claiming menorah isn't religious
- Just accepting court's legal permission
- Like accepting any law we benefit from

---

## Response #2: It Carries a Dual Message

### The Nuanced Approach

> "True, to us, it is a religious symbol. But it also carries a 'message' (that could be understood) on a secular level, in a secular context as well."

**The Dual Nature:**

**Religious Dimension:**
- To Jews: fulfilling mitzvah
- To us: religious symbol
- In our seforim
- Sacred tradition

**Secular Message:**
- Freedom of Religion (fundamental American Constitutional right)
- Light prevails over darkness
- Goodness overcomes oppression
- Little bit of light defeats vast force of evil
- **Fundamental premise of the American way**

---

### Why This Works

**Both/And, Not Either/Or:**
- Not denying religious nature
- But highlighting universal message
- Same symbol, multiple layers
- Different audiences understand differently

**Honest and Effective:**
- Acknowledges reality
- Explains secular appeal
- Shows universal values
- Makes legal case

---

## Response #3: Multiple Legal Arguments

### Five-Point Strategy

**From Shaliach Who Used These Successfully:**

> "I know some of these arguments are disingenuous, but they worked for us."

---

### Argument 1: Cultural, Not Purely Religious

**a) Not the Biblical Menorah**
- Chanukah menorah NOT mentioned in Bible
- Wasn't even intended to be reminiscent of biblical menorah
- Entire Chanukah tradition is **post-Biblical**
- Hardly mentioned in Mishna

**Significance:**
- Not an ancient religious artifact
- Later cultural development
- More cultural than purely scriptural

---

**b) Menorah Itself Not the Mitzvah**
- The mitzvah is "ner" (light/candle)
- NOT the candelabra
- Could technically use any light source
- Menorah is just traditional form

**Significance:**
- The object itself isn't religiously required
- It's the act of lighting that matters
- Menorah is cultural symbol of that act

---

**c) Outdoor Lighting is Symbolic**
- NOT fulfilling any religious obligation
- **PURELY symbolic**
- To publicize miracle of "few over many"
- Public awareness, not personal ritual

**Significance:**
- Different from religious observance at home
- Public display = educational/cultural
- Pirsumei nisa = publicity, not worship

---

### Argument 2: Universal Message

**d) Message is Generic to All**
- Freedom of religion
- Universal value
- Applies to all peoples
- All religions benefit

**Even If Religious:**
> "Afilu it'l that it is religious, it's message is universal and generic to all peoples (& all religions)"

---

### Argument 3: Widely Secular Practice

**e) Practiced by Secular Jews**
- Most widely practiced Jewish observance
- Even by self-avowed atheists
- Completely secular Jews participate
- Cultural/ethnic rather than religious for many

**Significance:**
- If atheists do it, can't be purely religious
- Cultural celebration for many
- Like Christmas for non-religious Christians

## Analysis: What's Really Happening Here

### The Honest Truth

**We Face a Tension:**

**On One Hand:**
- Menorah IS religious to us
- It IS in our seforim
- It IS a mitzvah
- We're not secular

**On the Other Hand:**
- Court ruling allows public display
- But uses secular reasoning
- We benefit from this reasoning
- Even if we don't fully believe it

---

### Three Approaches to This Tension

**Approach 1: Pragmatic**
- "Court says it's okay, so we do it"
- Follow the law like any other law
- Don't need to agree with reasoning
- Just accept the permission

**Approach 2: Nuanced**
- "It IS religious AND has secular message"
- Both dimensions true
- Different audiences see different things
- Universal freedom message alongside Jewish ritual

**Approach 3: Strategic**
- Use multiple cultural/secular arguments
- Emphasize non-religious dimensions
- Make case that authorities can accept
- Goal: get permission

---

### Which is "Right"?

**All Three Are Legitimate:**

**For Internal Understanding:** Approach 2 (nuanced)
- Most honest about complexity
- Acknowledges both dimensions
- Satisfies both head and heart

**For Explaining to Authorities:** Approaches 1 & 3
- Approach 1 for friendly officials ("Court said yes")
- Approach 3 for skeptical officials (multiple secular arguments)
- Choose based on audience

**For Legal Battles:** Approach 3 + Professional Help
- Get lawyer (like Nathan Lewin)
- Use constitutional arguments
- Cite precedents
- Make formal legal case

## The Real Question

### What Are We Actually Doing?

**Not:** Claiming menorah is secular

**Not:** Hiding religious nature

**Not:** Being dishonest

**But:** Working within legal framework that permits religious expression in public sphere

---

### The Constitutional Principle

**First Amendment Does Two Things:**

**1. Establishment Clause**
- Government can't establish religion
- Can't prefer one religion
- Can't coerce religious participation

**2. Free Exercise Clause**
- Government can't prohibit religious expression
- Must allow religious speech
- Can't discriminate against religion

**The Balance:**
- Can't IMPOSE religion
- But can't BAN religion either
- Public menorah = **allowing**, not imposing
- Especially on public forum property

---

## Bottom Line for Shluchim

### The Honest Answer

**To Ourselves:**
> "The menorah IS religious. But the Supreme Court ruled that displaying it publicly, in appropriate contexts, doesn't violate the Establishment Clause because it conveys a message of pluralism and freedom that transcends its specifically religious meaning."

**To Friendly Officials:**
> "The Supreme Court ruled in County of Allegheny v. ACLU (1989) that public menorah displays are constitutional. Would you like me to provide a copy of the decision?"

**To Skeptical Officials:**
> "The menorah celebrates freedom of religion - a core American value. While it has religious significance to Jews, its public display carries a universal message about liberty and light overcoming darkness. That's why the Supreme Court distinguished it from other religious symbols and ruled it permissible on government property when displayed with appropriate context."

**To Hostile Officials:**
> "I'd like to have our constitutional attorney contact you to discuss the legal framework. The Supreme Court has clearly ruled on this, and we're confident in our legal position."

The most honest internal understanding is #2, while #1 and #3 work better for different types of officials.

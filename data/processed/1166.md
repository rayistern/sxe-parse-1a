# How Many Matzas - Matzah Procurement and Distribution Guide

**Type**: Practical Resource Guide
**Topic**: Matzah quantities, sources, and distribution strategies for Pesach
**Audience**: Shluchim and community leaders
**Source Material**: Sicha from 5714, modern resources, field-tested ideas

## Traditional Custom from the Rebbe's Sicha

**Source**: Sicha from 5714 (1954), translated, printed in Yiddish in Likkutei Sichos

### Hebrew Text
הי' נהוג בישראל לפנים, כי לפני הפסח שולחים הרבנים מצות שמורות אל בעלי הבתים שבקהלותיהם; מערכת מצות, דהיינו שש מצות לצורך שני הסדרים, או, לכל הפחות, שתי מצות לצורך כזית של מצת מצוה, דהיינו המצה האמצעית

### Translation
"It was customary among the Jewish people in earlier times that before Pesach, the rabbis would send shmurah matzos to the householders in their communities; a set of matzos, meaning six matzos for the purpose of two Seders, or, at the very least, two matzos for the purpose of a k'zayis of matzas mitzvah, meaning the middle matzah."

### Key Points from the Sicha

**Historical Practice**: Rabbis took responsibility for ensuring their community members had proper matzos, actively sending them rather than waiting for people to procure them independently.

**Standard Quantity**: A "set" (מערכת) consists of six matzos - three for each of the two Seders.

**Minimum Quantity**: At minimum, two matzos should be provided - one for each Seder's "middle matzah" which is broken for the afikoman and from which the k'zayis of matzas mitzvah is taken.

**Quality Standard**: The text specifically mentions "מצות שמורות" (shmurah matzos), indicating the higher standard of matzos watched from the time of harvest (or at least from grinding).

## Field-Tested Ideas for Matzah Distribution

### Idea #1: Community Seder Outreach (Pre-Packaged Distribution)

**Process**:
1. **Get Rabbinic Approval**: Obtained okay from Lubavitcher Rabbonim
2. **Research Community Seders**: Identified where public Seders beyond Chabad would be held:
   - Hillel
   - Student Groups
   - Local Synagogues
   - Community Leaders who invite many people
3. **Create Individual Packages**: Prepared pre-packaged bags containing:
   - The shiur (proper measure) of matzah to be eaten
   - A note asking them to kindly distribute and encourage fulfilling the mitzvah at the right time
4. **Distribute to Organizers**: Sent packages for each participant to the Seder organizers

**Results**:
- Organizers were "more than glad to help"
- "Many hundreds" had the chance to actually perform the mitzvah properly
- Impact described as "incredible"

**Significance**: This approach leverages existing infrastructure (other organizations' Seders) to reach Jewish individuals who might not otherwise have proper matzah or know the correct quantities and timing, while building positive relationships with other Jewish organizations.

### Idea #2: Local Store Partnership (Increasing Retail Availability)

**Problem Identified**: Many stores do a "VERY weak job" on having ample Pesach supplies, even at a very basic level. Stores claim they need "11 months lead time" to procure these products.

**Strategy**:
1. **Target One Store**: Work to get at least one local store to agree to carry KFP (Kosher for Pesach) products
2. **Advertise Heavily**: Really advertise THE ONE store (emphasis in original)
3. **Drive Customers**: Direct customers specifically to that store

**Significance**: Rather than trying to convince multiple stores or accepting poor availability, concentrating efforts on one store partnership creates:
- A reliable source for the entire community
- Sufficient volume to justify the store's investment
- A centralized location that becomes known as "the place" for Pesach needs
- Potentially stronger negotiating position with the store due to guaranteed customer flow

### Idea #3: Media Partnership with Chabad.org (PR Opportunity)

**What Happened**:
- **Contact**: Chabad.org PR Team sent a TV crew
- **Coverage**: They covered the Pre-Seder preparations
- **Experience**: "They were great to work with"
- **Connections**: "Were able to create connections that we don't have"

**Significance**: This demonstrates how national Chabad resources (Chabad.org's PR team) can amplify local Shlichus efforts, creating media coverage that would be difficult to arrange independently, while the professional production team brings connections to media outlets and production quality that enhances the presentation.

## For Shluchim: Strategic Implementation Guide

### Matzah Procurement Planning

**Advance Timeline**: Based on the documented experience that stores claim to need 11-month lead time, begin conversations in May (immediately after the previous Pesach) for next year's Pesach. This is not excessive - it's matching the supply chain reality.

**Quantity Calculations**:
- **Traditional Standard**: 6 matzos per household (3 per Seder × 2 Seders)
- **Minimum Standard**: 2 matzos per household (1 middle matzah per Seder)
- **Community Seder**: 1 shiur-sized portion per expected participant
- **Safety Margin**: Order 20-30% extra to account for breakage, last-minute attendees, and individuals needing assistance

**Quality Tiers**: Consider offering multiple options:
- Premium: Handmade shmurah matzah (watched from harvest)
- Standard: Machine shmurah matzah (watched from grinding)
- Basic: Regular kosher for Pesach matzah
This allows budget-conscious families to participate while enabling those who can afford premium matzah to enhance their mitzvah.

### Distribution Methods - Choosing Your Approach

**Direct Distribution Model** (Traditional, from the Sicha):
- Send/deliver matzah directly to community members' homes
- Advantages: Ensures everyone has matzah, replicates historical rabbinical leadership model, creates touchpoint before Pesach
- Challenges: Requires accurate address list, delivery logistics, upfront cost before receiving payment
- Best for: Established communities with stable membership

**Pickup Model**:
- Congregants pick up matzah at Chabad house during designated times
- Advantages: Reduces delivery costs, creates foot traffic to Chabad house (opportunity for pre-Pesach engagement), easier logistics
- Challenges: Some people forget to pick up, requires storage space, multiple pickup times needed for convenience
- Best for: Urban settings with easy access to Chabad house

**Hybrid Model**:
- Core members receive delivery, peripheral community members pick up
- Advantages: Balances cost with service level, shows appreciation for committed members
- Challenges: Requires clear criteria for who receives delivery vs. pickup
- Best for: Growing communities with tiered engagement levels

**Sponsorship Approach**:
- Solicit sponsors to "adopt" families or cover matzah costs
- Advantages: Reduces net cost, engages philanthropic members, allows subsidizing those in financial need without them requesting charity
- Challenges: Requires fundraising effort, donor cultivation
- Best for: Communities with strong donor base

### Implementing Idea #1: Community Seder Partnerships

**Step-by-Step Implementation**:

1. **Rabbinic Approval** (Start: 3-4 months before Pesach):
   - Document your plan in writing
   - Submit to Lubavitcher Rabbonim for approval
   - Clarify any kashrus considerations (e.g., ensuring proper separation if partnering with non-Lubavitch organizations)

2. **Community Research** (2-3 months before):
   - Map all public Seders in your area: Hillel chapters, synagogues, JCCs, federations, senior centers, college groups
   - Contact organizers to determine: expected attendance, start time, their matzah plans, willingness to partner
   - Build relationships explaining you want to help ensure proper mitzvah observance, not compete

3. **Package Creation** (1 month before):
   - Calculate shiur: typically 1-1.5 machine matzos or 2/3 of handmade matzah
   - Packaging: Clear bags with Chabad branding (if appropriate) or neutral Pesach design
   - Note content: Brief explanation of shiur, timing (for Motzi Matzah), and perhaps "Compliments of Chabad of [Your City] - Wishing you a Kosher and Happy Pesach"
   - Consider including: Chabad house contact info for those who want to learn more

4. **Distribution to Organizers** (1-2 weeks before):
   - Deliver counts based on confirmed attendance
   - Provide brief training: when to distribute (before Maggid concludes), how to encourage consumption at proper time
   - Express appreciation for their partnership

5. **Follow-Up** (After Pesach):
   - Thank organizers
   - Ask for feedback: How did it go? What could improve?
   - Strengthen relationship for next year and other potential collaborations

**Budget Considerations**:
- Cost per portion: ~$1-3 depending on matzah type and packaging
- For "hundreds" of people: $200-600 investment
- Potential funding: Sponsor the initiative through donors who want to support mitzvah observance broadly

### Implementing Idea #2: Store Partnership

**Relationship Building**:

1. **Store Selection**:
   - Choose store with: Good foot traffic, willing management, adequate shelf space, diverse customer base
   - Avoid: Stores too small to handle volume, stores with ownership uninterested in specialty products

2. **Initial Pitch** (11 months before, i.e., May):
   - Data: Present number of Jewish families in area, expected Pesach shoppers
   - Commitment: Offer to guarantee minimum purchase or help promote
   - Differentiation: Position as "THE Pesach destination" giving them competitive advantage
   - Education: Explain Pesach basics, product timeline, storage requirements

3. **Product Selection**:
   - Start narrow: Matzah, grape juice, basic essentials
   - Expand: Based on first year success, add more products
   - Quality: Ensure products are actually KFP and ideally match community standards (e.g., kitniyos-free for Ashkenazi communities)

4. **Marketing Partnership**:
   - Joint promotion: Store advertises in your newsletter, you promote store to community
   - In-store presence: Offer to staff a "Pesach information table" on busy shopping days
   - Signage: Help create clear "Kosher for Passover" section signage

5. **During Pesach Season**:
   - Monitor inventory: Alert store if products selling fast and need restocking
   - Troubleshoot: Address any customer complaints or confusion
   - Presence: Make yourself available to answer questions

6. **Post-Pesach Review**:
   - Sales data: Review what sold well, what didn't
   - Feedback: Gather from community and share with store
   - Planning: Commit to next year, refine product selection

**Value Proposition for Store**:
- New customer segment
- Increased sales during specific period
- Minimal risk if you guarantee baseline purchases
- Community goodwill

### Implementing Idea #3: Chabad.org Media Partnership

**Outreach Process**:

1. **Timing**: Contact Chabad.org PR team 2-3 months before Pesach
   - Find contact: Check Chabad.org website or ask through Merkos channels
   - Initial reach: Email with compelling story angle

2. **Pitch Development**:
   - Unique angle: What makes YOUR pre-Pesach prep compelling? (Scale? Location? Unique program? Human interest story?)
   - Visual potential: Describe what would look good on camera (matzah baking? Large-scale packing? Community gathering?)
   - Broader relevance: How does this illustrate larger themes? (Jewish continuity? Community care? Tradition in modern times?)

3. **Logistics Preparation**:
   - Permission: Ensure venue allows filming
   - Participants: Brief anyone who'll be on camera, get necessary releases
   - Timing: Schedule filming when activity is most photogenic and participants available
   - Backup plans: What if crew is delayed or can't make it?

4. **Maximizing Coverage**:
   - B-roll: Offer variety of shots (close-ups of matzah, wide shots of preparation, interviews)
   - Interviews: Prep 2-3 articulate people (yourself, a volunteer, a community member) with talking points
   - Sound bites: Practice concise, meaningful statements (avoid rambling)

5. **Leveraging Content**:
   - Once aired/published: Share widely on your social media, newsletter, website
   - Save: Keep high-quality version for future promotional use
   - Thank: Express gratitude to Chabad.org team publicly
   - Network: Use connections made for future opportunities

**Benefits Beyond Immediate Coverage**:
- Professional content you can reuse
- Validation from national organization
- Potential contacts in media
- Ideas and feedback from professional PR team
- Template for future media engagement

### Integration Strategy: Combining All Three Ideas

**Comprehensive Approach**:
1. **Store Partnership** = Infrastructure (ensures matzah availability in community)
2. **Community Seder Outreach** = Expansion (reaches beyond your immediate community)
3. **Media Coverage** = Amplification (broadcasts your efforts creating awareness and credibility)

These aren't mutually exclusive - implement all three for maximum impact:
- Media covers your preparations (Idea #3)
- Footage includes packing matzah portions for community Seders (Idea #1)
- Mention partnership with local store (Idea #2)
- Result: Comprehensive matzah strategy with built-in publicity

### Additional Considerations

**Rabbinic Oversight**: The document's emphasis on "getting an ok from Lubavitcher Rabbonim" for Idea #1 suggests similar approval for major initiatives is advisable, ensuring halachic appropriateness and alignment with Chabad standards.

**Documentation**: Track results (numbers reached, costs, feedback) to refine approach and demonstrate impact to donors/supporters.

**Sustainability**: These aren't one-time projects but annual rhythms. Build systems (contact lists, supplier relationships, packaging templates) that make each year easier.

**Gratitude**: The repeated positive tone ("more than glad to help," "great to work with," "incredible") suggests these initiatives succeed partly through maintaining excellent relationships with partners through appreciation and professionalism.

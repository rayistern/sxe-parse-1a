I am writing to seek the involvement of The City of Stonnington in the Jewish festival of Chanukah, which commences this year on the evening of Friday, 19th December and ends at sunset on Saturday, 26th December.

**Universal Message:**
Chanukah is an extremely joyous holiday, commemorating the universal message of the victory of good over evil. It is celebrated in particular by lighting the Menorah, an eight-branched candelabra, in a public place in order to publicise this victory.

**Symbolism:**
An extra candle is lit every night, one the first night, two the second night, and so on. Light symbolises good, the dispelling of darkness. By the final night, all eight candles are blazing, signifying the total eradication of evil.

### Key Lessons from Rebbe's Letters

**SITUATION ONE: Teaneck Menorah**

**The Rebbe's Strong Defense:**
The Rebbe publicly put himself on the line defending this public Menorah.

**But Also Wrote:**
"Bitchila kosavti lo shelo lehadlik eish hamachlokes" - "At the very outset, I wrote to him (the local Shliach involved with this Menorah dispute) not to ignite the fire of Machlokes."

**When Senator Advised Backing Off:**
Beside the Senator's words (to back off for this year), the Rebbe wrote:
"V'harei boze hatzedek itoy" - "For in this (the Senator's alarm at Lubavitch's actions), he is correct."

**Result:**
- Lubavitch backed off that year
- Next year, Senator helped arrange Menorah in Trenton and spoke at event
- Ultimately helped with Teaneck situation as well

---

**SITUATION TWO: Mayor Koch in New York**

**Situation:**
Rabbi Butman approached Mayor Koch for permission to erect Menorah in certain location. Mayor said no.

**Rabbi Butman's Response:**
Wrote to Rebbe that "we will stand strong for the Menorah at that location."

**Rebbe's Response:**
Don't start a Machlokes with him.

**Result:**
Mayor Koch gave different public space for the Menorah, where it stands until today.

## 4. What to Use to Light a Public Menorah

### Outdoor Option: Tiki Torch

**Best for:** Outdoor public menorahs

**Features:**
- Pretty powerful
- Works well in wind
- Large wick

**Fuel Options:**
1. Tiki brand torch fuel (Home Depot/Lowe's)
2. Paraffin

**Important Notes:**
- Tiki torch only has 1 wick (big but 1)
- Good for menorah
- Not good for havdala

**How to Find:**
Go into local Home Depot with the insert and find best fit for the torch

---

### Lighting the Menorah

**Best Method:**
Blow torch or butane torch taped to a stick or pole

**Why NOT Tiki Torch for Lighting:**
- Very difficult to use tiki torch to light other candles
- Tiki cover gets in the way
- Tilting makes fuel spill
- Takes people like 60 seconds to light one candle
- Oil spills all over

---

### Indoor Option: Candle Conversion Kit

**Product:** Paraffin with Glass Chimney

**Best for:** Indoor public menorahs

---

### Alternative: Gas Lantern

**IMPORTANT:** Make sure it is ONE Mantel (wick) not two

**Product Example:**
Coleman One-Mantle Compact Propane Lantern
[Amazon Link](http://www.amazon.com/Coleman-One-Mantle-Compact-Propane-Lantern/dp/B0009PUR54/)

**Runtime:** One propane can lasts about 4 hours on high

---

### If You Don't Have Conversion Kit

**Question:** "Don't have the tiki conversion set for a Klar Menorah, what's the best and easiest way to do lighting?"

**Solution:** Tape and big candles

### Option 2: Car Battery

**How It Works:**
Car batteries sound the most shayech (appropriate)

**Requirements:**
- Low watt LED light bulbs (Walmart has 4W ones)
- Inverter (car batteries operate at 12V, most menorahs are 120V)
- Electrician to install (merge cables, ensure outdoor safe)

**Maintenance:**
You might need to switch out battery a few times over Chanukah

### Option 3: Generator

**Pros:**
- Heavy but worth its weight in gold in some places
- Reliable power source

**Important Considerations:**

**Power Calculation:**
- All 9 bulbs (at 75 watts each) = 675 watts
- Add any spotlights
- Ensure generator provides good amount more than needed
- Advertised number is highest it gets to, not idle level

**Noise:**
- Generators make a lot of noise
- Put it far away during events
- Use extension cord (at least for events)
- Alternative: Electric generators that are almost inaudible

**Security:**
- Chain it to something that won't move

**Fuel:**
- Buy gas cans big enough to fill it
- You'll probably/certainly need to refill each night

**Green Option:**
- Solar generator
- Expensive initial investment
- Can promote as "green Menorah"
- Good pitch for green-minded balebatim

---

### Option 4: Street Lamps

**Hidden Resource:**
In several places there have been outlets in street lamps, either exposed or under a screwed panel.

**How to Use:**
- Look around to see if it's there
- If you see it and use it, usually no one will ask questions
- If under panel and someone working there: Ask with breitkeit (openness): "Are you the one who knows how to open this? We need to use it for our menorah display"

**CRITICAL WARNING:**
K'muvan (obviously), every such breitkeit has a risk. If a friend politician arranged the permit for you, **don't risk stabbing him in the back** by doing it.

## Final Thoughts

A successful public menorah combines:

1. **Proper permissions** - Legal and relationship-based
2. **Reliable power** - Appropriate to your situation
3. **Quality lighting method** - Safe and halachically sound
4. **SPOTLIGHT** - Non-negotiable for pirsumei nisa
5. **Security** - Chains, monitoring
6. **Maintenance plan** - Daily checks, fuel/power
7. **Weather readiness** - Backup plans
8. **Professional appearance** - Clean, straight, impressive

The investment in a quality public menorah setup pays dividends in:
- Pirsumei nisa (primary goal)
- Community visibility
- Media coverage
- Jewish pride
- Annual tradition

Most importantly: Remember the Rebbe's guidance to defend the right to public menorahs while avoiding unnecessary machlokes. Work strategically, build relationships, and create beautiful, prominent displays that bring light to the world.

**Hatzlacha Rabba!**

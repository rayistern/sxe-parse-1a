# Insights Into the Haggadah - Pesach 5774

**Compiled by**: Rabbi Yacov Barber, South Caulfield Shule (Melbourne, Australia)
**Type**: Haggadah Commentary Collection
**Year**: 5774 (2014)
**Dedication**: In memory of Rebbetzin Rivkie Barber ע"ה

## Forward - A Moving Dedication

This compilation carries a profoundly touching dedication. Rabbi Yacov Barber writes that the Gemara in Sotah teaches "בזכות נשים צדקניות נגאלו אבתינו ממצרים" (in the merit of the righteous women our ancestors were redeemed from Egypt), and the Arizal teaches that the souls of the final generation before Moshiach are reincarnations of the Exodus generation - just as then it was through women's faith that we were redeemed, so too it will be through the righteous women of our generation.

**Personal Context**: For many years, Rabbi Barber compiled Haggadah insights (פירושים) and distributed them to Melbourne shuls, uploaded them to Shluchim Exchange for fellow Shluchim, and sent them to his children's homes. However, the main reason was to enhance their own family Seder.

**About Rebbetzin Rivkie**: Pesach was Rebbetzin Rivkie's Yom Tov. She ensured their Sedarim were truly magical, hosting people from all walks of life, young and old. Her special Pesach food preparations were something the family eagerly anticipated. As a true Rebbetzin, she would share divrei Torah, stories, and anecdotes that inspired everyone. The Seder would only conclude after singing the songs she had sung growing up in her parents' home (Rabbi Asher and Nechama Heber of Crown Heights).

**The Tragic Turn**: Rabbi Barber began preparing this year's insights while his wife was undergoing treatment at Memorial Sloan Kettering in Manhattan. He had intended to dedicate these insights as a zechus (merit) for her refuah shleimah (complete recovery) and a long and fruitful life. However, as he writes, Hashem "whose thoughts are not our thoughts" and whose ways transcend our understanding, had other plans.

**Final Dedication**: These Divrei Torah are now dedicated as a zechus for the neshama of his beloved wife, their dear Rebbetzin, whose love for the material and spiritual welfare of her family, community, and students knew no bounds. Rabbi Barber expresses confidence that just as she cared for them on earth, she will continue to be a "gutte bette" (good advocate) for their family, their kehilla, her students, and the family of Shluchim around the world.

## Insights Included

### Maos Chittin (Money for Flour - Helping the Poor with Pesach Needs)

**Question**: Why are we specifically concerned about the needs of the poor by Pesach more than any other Yom Tov?

**Answer from Tana D'vei Eliyahu**: The writings of Elijah the Prophet state that King David praised the Jewish people who left Egypt because they made a covenant (brit) that they would do acts of gemilat chassadim (kindness). Therefore, we prepare for Pesach by performing acts of kindness (Ben Asher).

**Significance**: This links Pesach observance directly to the character trait of chesed (kindness) that defined the generation of the Exodus, establishing tzedakah as not merely a social welfare program but as a spiritual preparation that connects us to our ancestors' merit.

### Shabbat HaGadol (The Great Shabbos - The Shabbos Before Pesach)

**Question**: Why is this Shabbos referred to as "Shabbat HaGadol" (the Great Shabbos)?

**Humorous Answer**: On a lighter note, when approached for tzedakah and having no money in one's pockets, people often say "I am shabbosdik" - meaning on Shabbos we're not allowed to have money in our pockets, and currently they have no money so it's like they're dressed for Shabbos. By the Shabbos before Pesach, Jews have spent so much money preparing for Pesach that their pockets are genuinely empty. It is "Shabbos HaGadol" - the "great Shabbos" - in the sense that they truly have no money left in their pockets (Alos Hashachar).

**Deeper Significance**: While presented humorously, this teaching reflects the reality of the financial commitment Jewish families make to properly observe Pesach, and the pride in prioritizing spiritual obligations over material concerns.

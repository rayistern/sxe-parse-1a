# [Misc > For Writer > _5 Chanukah > General] Dignitary Speech Samples

## Actual Speeches Delivered at Public Menorah Lightings

**Format:** Two short speeches from different officials
**Location:** Gainesville, Florida
**Speakers:** City Commission representative and County Commission representative

---

## Speech #1: City Commission Greeting

### Full Text

> "Good afternoon and a happy Chanukah to all. It is a pleasure and an honor to be here and to deliver Chanukah greetings on behalf of the city commission and the entire city of Gainesville.
>
> In a few minutes we will all light a candle of unity followed by the kindling of this giant nine-foot menorah.
>
> The Menorah, a traditional eight branch candelabra symbolizes to Jew and non-Jew alike the universal message of freedom from oppression and freedom of religion.
>
> It is thus an opportune time to give thanks to G-d for allowing us to live in the United States, a country that epitomizes the message of Chanukah.
>
> I would like to thank the Jewish Community of Gainesville for giving me the opportunity to participate in this joyous ceremony.
>
> Happy Chanukah to all and G-d bless America."

---

### Speech Analysis

**Opening (30 seconds):**
- Greeting and happy wishes
- Establishes role (on behalf of city commission and entire city)
- Expresses honor

**Bridge to Action (15 seconds):**
- Preview what's about to happen
- "Candle of unity" (inclusive language)
- Describes menorah (nine-foot - impressive!)

**Core Message (45 seconds):**
- Universal symbolism explained
- "To Jew and non-Jew alike" (inclusive)
- Freedom from oppression
- Freedom of religion
- Thanks to G-d for America
- America epitomizes Chanukah's message

**Closing (15 seconds):**
- Thanks to Jewish community
- "Joyous ceremony" (positive tone)
- Happy Chanukah
- G-d bless America (patriotic)

**Total Length:** ~2 minutes

## Speech #2: County Commission Greeting

### Full Text

> "Ladies and gentlemen, girls and boys, I'm proud to be standing here on behalf of the Alachua County Commission, participating in this momentous occasion, the first public menorah lighting ceremony in Gainesville.
>
> Now that the Menorah is lit, we must all look at the flames and listen to the message it tells."

---

### Speech Analysis

**Opening (20 seconds):**
- Inclusive greeting (ladies/gentlemen, girls/boys)
- Expresses pride
- Establishes role (County Commission)
- Historic moment emphasized: **"first public menorah lighting"**

**Core Message (10 seconds):**
- Action oriented ("now that menorah is lit")
- Meditative instruction
- "Look at the flames"
- "Listen to the message"
- Brief but profound

**Total Length:** ~30 seconds

## Why These Speeches Work

### Speech #1 Strengths

**1. Universal Language**
- "Jew and non-Jew alike"
- Freedom themes
- American values
- Inclusive throughout

**2. Gratitude Emphasized**
- Thanks to G-d
- Thanks to Jewish community
- Thanks for America
- Humble tone

**3. Patriotic Close**
- "G-d bless America"
- Ties to national values
- Safe, unifying

**4. Clear Structure**
- Greeting
- Preview
- Message
- Thanks
- Blessing

---

### Speech #2 Strengths

**1. Brevity**
- Under 1 minute
- Gets out of the way
- Lets ceremony speak

**2. Historic Framing**
- "First public menorah lighting"
- Creates significance
- Memorable moment

**3. Contemplative Close**
- "Look at the flames"
- "Listen to the message"
- Invites reflection
- Poetic

**4. Inclusive Opening**
- Names all demographics
- Children included
- Community-wide

## Template Speeches for Dignitaries

### Version 1: Standard (2-3 minutes)

```
Good [morning/afternoon/evening] and a happy Chanukah to all.

It is a pleasure and an honor to be here and to deliver Chanukah greetings on behalf of [your position/organization].

[OPTIONAL: Describe what's about to happen]
In a few moments we will light this [description of menorah].

[CORE MESSAGE - Choose one theme:]

OPTION A - Freedom Theme:
The Menorah symbolizes to people of all faiths the universal message of freedom from oppression and freedom of religion. It reminds us that we are blessed to live in [country/city], where these freedoms are protected and celebrated.

OPTION B - Light Over Darkness:
The Chanukah lights remind us that even a small light can dispel great darkness. In times of challenge, we must each be a light to others, bringing hope and warmth to our community.

OPTION C - Perseverance:
Chanukah teaches us that with faith and determination, seemingly impossible challenges can be overcome. The few prevailed over the many, and light triumphed over darkness.

[CLOSING:]
I would like to thank [organization/community] for giving me the opportunity to participate in this joyous ceremony.

Happy Chanukah to all, and may these lights inspire us to bring more light into our world.
```

---

### Version 2: Brief (30-60 seconds)

```
[Greeting that includes all ages],

I'm honored to be here on behalf of [organization] participating in this [momentous/joyous/historic] occasion.

[Choose ONE:]
- The lights of Chanukah remind us that freedom is worth fighting for.
- These flames teach us that even small lights can dispel great darkness.
- Chanukah celebrates the enduring strength of faith and community.

May these lights bring warmth and hope to all.

Happy Chanukah!
```

### Version 3: First-Time Event (2 minutes)

```
Good [time of day] and happy Chanukah!

It is truly special to be here for [city/location]'s very first public menorah lighting ceremony. This is a historic moment for our community.

Chanukah celebrates religious freedom - the right of all people to practice their faith without oppression. As [your position], I am proud that [city/county] embraces and protects this fundamental freedom for all our residents.

The menorah we light today will stand as a symbol of our community's commitment to diversity, inclusion, and mutual respect.

Thank you to [Chabad/Jewish organization] for bringing this meaningful tradition to our [city/county]. And thank you all for being here to celebrate together.

Happy Chanukah, and may these lights inspire us all!
```

## What to Provide to Dignitaries

### Speech Prep Package

**Send 1 Week Before Event:**

1. **Sample Speech** (customize one of above)
2. **Pronunciation Guide:**
   - Chanukah: HAH-noo-kah or KHAN-uh-kah
   - Menorah: men-OR-ah
   - Chabad: khah-BAHD
3. **Event Program** (so they know when to speak)
4. **Key Themes to Emphasize:**
   - Religious freedom
   - Light over darkness
   - Community unity
   - Universal message
5. **What NOT to Say:**
   - Don't call it "Jewish Christmas"
   - Don't pronounce it "CHAWN-oo-kah"
   - Don't make it exclusively religious
6. **Length Guidance:** "Brief remarks, 1-2 minutes ideal"

## Coaching Points for Rabbi

### Before the Event

**If Dignitary Asks for Help:**
- Offer sample speeches
- Emphasize brevity (1-2 min max)
- Suggest universal themes
- Provide pronunciation guide
- Reassure them ("very informal, just greetings")

**If They Want to Wing It:**
- Suggest 2-3 key points only:
  1. Thanks for invitation
  2. One universal message
  3. Happy Chanukah

---

### During the Event

**Introduction:**
> "We are honored to be joined by [Title] [Name], representing [organization]. [Title] [Name], please share your greetings with us."

**If Speech is Too Long:**
- Smile and nod
- Don't show impatience
- Audience will forgive
- Thank them warmly after

**If Speech Goes Wrong:**
- Keep smiling
- Move on quickly
- Don't draw attention to it
- Thank them anyway

**Opening:**

- Happy Chanukah (95%)

**Body:**
- Freedom/liberty theme (70%)
- Light over darkness (60%)
- Community unity (50%)

- Personal story (20%)

**Closing:**
- Happy Chanukah again (80%)
- God bless (60%)
- Thanks to organizers (70%)

## Handling Different Types

### The Natural Speaker
- Will do great without help
- Just provide pronunciation guide
- Give time limit
- Enjoy!

### The Nervous Speaker
- Send sample speech
- Offer to review their remarks
- Reassure multiple times
- "Just speak from heart"
- They'll be fine

### The Over-Preparer
- Will write detailed speech
- May be too long
- Gently suggest time limit
- Offer to review and edit
- "Shorter is more powerful"

### The Politician
- Will make it about them
- That's okay!
- Keep smiling
- Thank them for coming
- Worth it for the presence

### The No-Show
- Have backup plan
- Rabbi gives greetings instead
- Don't mention they cancelled
- Move on smoothly

## Follow-Up

### After Their Speech

**Immediate:**
- Thank them warmly
- Specific compliment if possible
- ("Your words about freedom were perfect")
- Take photo together

**Next Day:**
- Send thank you email
- Attach photos from event
- Quote their best line
- Invite to future events

## Special Situations

### If Multiple Dignitaries

**Structure:**
- Introduce all at once or separately
- Each speaks 1-2 minutes
- Rabbi moderates
- Thank all together at end

**Order:**
- Lowest rank to highest
- Or: Local → County → State → Federal
- Or: In order of arrival

---

### If They Brought Proclamation

**Dignitary Script:**
> "I'm honored to read this proclamation from [office] recognizing [dates] as Chanukah in [city/county]..."

**After Reading:**
- Present to rabbi
- Photo with proclamation
- Display prominently
- Thank them specifically

---

### If Media Present

**Brief Them:**
- Media here (heads up)
- Speak clearly
- Good photo opportunity
- They'll look good!

---

**Bottom Line:**
These sample speeches show that effective dignitary remarks are brief (1-2 minutes), emphasize universal themes (freedom, light over darkness), include gratitude, and close patriotically. The first speech provides substance and structure; the second shows that brevity can be powerful. Provide speech templates to nervous officials, pronunciation guides to all, and always emphasize that shorter is better. The goal is dignitary presence and blessing, not lengthy remarks. Their attendance matters more than their words, but good words make it even better.

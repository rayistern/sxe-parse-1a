## Insurance Options Overview

### Option 1: Umbrella Policy Rider

**From:** [REDACTED-NAME], Glendale, CA

**If You Have an Umbrella Policy:**

If you have an umbrella policy for your shul, this should be covered and you just need to get a rider.

**Advantages:**
- Simplest option
- Usually lowest cost
- Quick processing
- Already familiar with your organization

**How It Works:**
1. Contact your current insurance provider
2. Explain the event details
3. Request a rider for the specific dates
4. Pay additional premium (usually modest)

**From:** [REDACTED-NAME], Ann Arbor, MI:
> "We use our current insurance company. You ask them to add the event to the policy during the week of Chanukah."

---

### Option 2: One-Time Event Insurance

**If You Don't Have an Umbrella Policy:**

You can purchase this kind of insurance for a one-time event.

**Typical Cost:** About $500

**What It Covers:**
- Liability for event duration
- Specific location
- Named event activities
- Participant and spectator coverage

**When to Use:**
- No existing umbrella policy
- First-time large event
- High-risk activities
- Venue requires specific coverage

**How to Obtain:**
1. Contact insurance broker (like Century Coverage Corp above)
2. Provide event details:
   - Date, time, location
   - Expected attendance
   - Activities planned
   - Any special risks
3. Get quote
4. Purchase coverage
5. Provide certificate to venue if required

---

### Option 3: Partner with Another Shliach

**Creative Solution:**

You can call a neighboring Shliach who does have an umbrella policy and ask him to be a participant in your event.

**How It Works:**
- He does not need to show up
- He becomes a co-sponsor/participant on paper
- He can then get a rider for you through his policy
- Cost is usually less than one-time event insurance

**Advantages:**
- Lower cost than Option 2
- Leverages existing relationships
- Builds collaboration
- Faster processing

**Considerations:**
- Requires willing Shliach partner
- Must coordinate paperwork
- Ensure his policy allows this
- Formalize the arrangement

## Detailed Guidance by Situation

### For Small Events (Under 100 people)

**Best Option:** Umbrella policy rider

**If No Umbrella Policy:**
- Consider getting one (useful for many programs)
- Partner with nearby Shliach
- One-time event policy if necessary

**Typical Cost:** $100-$300

---

### For Medium Events (100-500 people)

**Best Option:** Umbrella policy rider or one-time event policy

**Coverage Needs:**
- General liability
- Property damage
- Medical coverage

**Typical Cost:** $300-$600

**Important:**
- Start process 4-6 weeks before event
- Get quotes from multiple providers
- Read policy carefully for exclusions

---

### For Large Events (500+ people)

**Best Option:** Comprehensive event insurance

**Must-Haves:**
- High liability limits ($1M-$2M+)
- Additional insured (venue, city, etc.)
- Workers compensation if applicable
- Special event coverage

**Typical Cost:** $500-$1,500+

**Timeline:**
- Start 2-3 months before event
- May require site inspection
- Venue may have specific requirements
- Multiple certificates may be needed

## What Your Insurance Should Cover

### Basic Coverage Elements

**General Liability:**
- Bodily injury
- Property damage
- Personal injury
- Advertising injury

**Medical Payments:**
- Immediate medical expenses
- Regardless of fault
- Per person limit

**Products/Completed Operations:**
- Food service (latkes, donuts)
- Craft activities
- Any distributed items

### Additional Considerations

**Special Activities:**

**If Your Event Includes:**
- Bounce houses
- Pony rides
- Fire performances
- Pyrotechnics
- Alcohol service

**You May Need:**
- Higher liability limits
- Special endorsements
- Additional insured status
- Vendor insurance certificates

---

### Venue Requirements

**Most Venues Require:**
- Certificate of insurance
- Specific liability limits
- Venue named as additional insured
- 30-60 days notice

**Common Requirements:**
- $1M general liability minimum
- $2M aggregate
- Property damage coverage
- Current policy dates

**Get This Information Early:**
Ask venue for insurance requirements when you book the space.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Do I need insurance for a small home event?**
A: Check your homeowner's policy. For 20+ people, consider event coverage.

**Q: What if someone gets hurt at my event?**
A: Document immediately, provide first aid, file insurance claim if needed.

**Q: Can volunteers be covered?**
A: Yes, ensure policy includes volunteer coverage.

**Q: What if the venue has its own insurance?**
A: You still need your own coverage. Venue's policy protects the venue, not you.

**Q: How much liability coverage do I need?**
A: Minimum $1M, but $2M is better for large events.

**Q: Does insurance cover food poisoning?**
A: Products liability covers this if you have it in your policy.

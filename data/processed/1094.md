## Opening Question

**Title Question:**
> "Can you have your chometz and eat it?"

**Subtitle:**
> "Selling chometz, is it one big loophole?"

**Sets Up:**
- Direct, provocative question
- Common perception
- Need for answers

---

## The Controversy

**Five Objections:**

**1. Sale seems contrived**
- Are we dodging Torah directive?

**2. Doesn't leave residence**
- "The chametz does not leave the owner's residence"

**3. Purchaser uninterested**
- "The purchaser does not appear interested in actually taking possession of the chametz"

**4. No access required**
- "Rarely, if ever, does the seller have to open his doors and cabinets to the new owner of his food"

**5. Automatic reversion**
- "The chometz invariably reverts to its original ownership immediately after Pesach"

## Introduction: The Chametz Issue

### Torah Prohibitions

**Three Key Verses:**

> "For seven days fermented dough shall not be found in your homes..."
> — Shemos 12:19

> "For seven days you shall eat Matza, but on the first day you shall destroy fermented dough from your houses…"
> — Shemos 12:15

> "You shall eat matzos for seven days, and you may not see any chametz and you may not see any fermented dough in all your borders…"
> — Shemos 13:7

---

### Rambam: Severity of Violation

**Punishment:**
> "If one had chametz in one's possession before Pesach, and did not destroy it before Pesach, but left it in his possession… he is given lashes…"

**Permanent Prohibition:**
> "Chometz that remains in a Jew's possession over Pesach may never be used by a Jew for any beneficial purpose."
> — Rambam, Laws of Chometz & Matzah 1:3-4

**Shulchan Aruch:**
> "It is prohibited to benefit from chametz that belonged to a Jew over Pesach…"
> — Shulchan Aruch, Orach Chaim 448:3

---

### A Non-Jew's Chometz - The Key

**Cannot Hide Your Own:**
> "It is written in the Torah: 'No leavened bread shall be seen with you' (Exodus 13:7). One might suppose that a man commits no transgression if he hides the cḥometz or deposits it with a non-Jew; the Torah therefore says plainly: 'No leaven shall be found in your houses' (Exodus 12:19), even if he has deposited or hidden it."

**Critical Exception:**
> "Chometz that… belonged to a non-Jew and was in a Jew's possession, even if it is in the Jew's house, is permitted because it doesn't belong to him."
> — Rambam, Laws of Chometz & Matzah 4:1-2

**Principle:**
- Ownership matters, not location
- Cannot merely deposit
- Non-Jew's chametz permitted in your house

---

## Questions for Consideration

**Six Key Questions:**

**1.** "If the Torah says we must rid our homes of chometz, can a Rabbinic loophole override it?"

**2.** "Can you retain chometz that you don't own on your property?"

**3.** "Is it considered a genuine transaction to 'sell' an item to someone who will never collect it from you/ take possession of it?"

**4.** "Is it practical to expect the non-Jew to come collect the chometz he has bought from all of us? Could that invalidate the process?"

**5.** "Is it legit to sell something that you really want back?"

**6.** "If the Torah prescribes a means of transaction that is not recognized by secular law, can we use that means of transaction to transact with a non-Jew, who is not bound by Torah law?"

## Precedent for the Chometz Sale

### Talmudic Permission

> "During the time that it is permitted to eat, one can… sell to the gentile."
> — Talmud, Pesachim 21a

> "If one gave the chometz to a gentile before noon [on erev Pesach], one need not destroy it."
> — Shulchan Aruch, Orach Chaim 445:2

---

### The Yochanan Chakuka'a Incident

**Emergency Sale:**

Person deposited saddlebag with chametz with Yochanan Chakuka'a, mice bore hole, chametz spilling out.

Came to Rabbi Yehuda HaNasi on erev Pesach morning:
- Hours 1-4: "Wait" (owner might return)
- Hour 5 (before prohibition): "Go and sell it in the market"

**Source:** Talmud, Pesachim 13a

---

### Tosefta: Travelers on Boat

> "If a Jew and gentile were traveling by boat [such that there is no means of replenishing one's food stock after the holiday] and there is chametz in the hands of the Jew: He may sell it to the gentile or give it as a gift, and subsequently repurchase it from him after Pesach."
> — Tosefta, Pesachim 2:12

---

### Shulchan Aruch: Even Knowing It Will Return

**Critical Ruling:**
> "If he sold it or gave it to a Jew outside of his home before Pesach, even though the Jewish seller knows that the gentile will not touch the chametz at all until after Pesach, and afterwards, he will return the chametz, it is permitted, so long as the Jew gave it as a complete gift without any stipulation."
> — Shulchan Aruch, Orach Chaim 448:3

**Key:** Unconditional gift, even knowing it returns

---

### Kitzur Shulchan Aruch: Wholehearted Sale

> "…one who has much chometz, and cannot get rid of it all, needs to sell it to a gentile before Pesach…"

> "…this sale should not be simply by rote, rather one should have in mind to sell wholeheartedly… literally like a merchant (would)."
> — Kitzur Shulchan Aruch, 114:1

---

## Is It a Farce If You Expect to Get Your Chometz Back?

### Rabbi Yosef Caro

> "I don't understand his reservation, considering that [the Talmud] permits giving chometz to a gentile as a gift, when one will ask the gentile to re-gift it back after Pesach. Surely, this is blatantly deceitful [i.e. a major loophole], yet [the Talmud] permitted it, because he will remove the chometz from his possession."
> — Beis Yosef, Orach Chaim 445

**Talmud explicitly permits despite seeming like loophole**

---

### Vilna Gaon - Stricter View

> "One should not sell chometz, unless one intends for the sale to be permanent. After Pesach, one should not purchase baked goods from flour that was stored over Pesach or whiskey that was stored over Pesach, as the sale is unreliable…"
> — Ma'aseh Rav, Siddur of the Vilna Gaon 180-1

---

### Bechor Shor - Limited Scope

> "Selling chometz cannot override the Torah prohibition and, therefore, can only help with chometz that one has already nullified [which is only a Rabbinic prohibition]."
> — Rabbi Alexander Sender Shor, Bechor Shor on Pesachim 21b

---

### Rav Amram Gaon - Conditions

> "A Jew who sold his chometz to a gentile, or gave it to him as an unconditional gift, if the gentile [acquired the chometz as he] moved it and there is no intention to deceive and he would not do this during the rest of the year… the Jew may repurchase such chometz from the gentile after Pesach. The reason being that the gentile moved the chometz, so it is in his possession and chometz in a gentile's possession over Pesach is permitted to a Jew after Pesach."
> — Rav Amram Gaon, Otzar HaGeonim, Pesachim 48

---

### Evolution of Practice

**Terumas HaDeshen:**
> "One should instruct them to give the‎‏ ‏chametz to the non-‎Jew outside the‏ ‏house."
> — Terumas Hadeshen, 119

**Bach - Selling the Room:**
> "In this land, where most commerce is in‏ ‏whisky and one cannot sell it to a non- Jew outside the ‎home, and especially for‏ ‏those who hold a license for selling‏ ‏liquor, there is room to permit sale to ‎a‏ ‏non-Jew of all chametz in the room, as‏ ‏well as the room itself.‎"
> — Bach, Orach Chaim 448:2

**Kitzur Shulchan Aruch:**
> "The chometz that you sell to a gentile should not remain in the Jew's home… if the gentile is unable to take the chometz to his own home, the Jew must rent the room where the chometz is stored to the gentile."
> — Kitzur Shulchan Aruch 114:2

---

### Chasam Sofer - Full Legal Sale

> "The contrived sale [mentioned in the Tosefta] is completely permitted by Torah, without any qualms, because the Jew sells his chometz as a full legal sale to the gentile, despite them both knowing that the Jew wants to reclaim the chometz after Pesach. In spite of this, at the moment, the chometz is fully sold to the gentile, who may sell it, eat it, donate it or dispose of it without restraint. It is only because the gentile likes his Jewish friend that he retains the chometz until after Pesach."
> — Responsa, Chasam Sofer Orach Chaim 113

**At the moment: Full legal sale with complete rights**

## What Method of Sale Works?

### Torah Sources on Acquisition

**Biblical Verse:**
> "When you sell merchandise to your compatriot, or purchase from the hand of your compatriot, do not overcharge a man to his brother."
> — Vayikra 25:14

**Talmudic Debate:**
- Rabbi Yochanan: "Coins acquire according to Torah law"
- Reish Lakish: "Meshicha (pulling the item) is explicit in the Torah"
- Verse: "from the hand of your compatriot" - hand to hand transfer

**Source:** Bava Metzia 47b

---

### For Gentiles

> "If one wishes to sell an animal to a gentile in order to exempt it from the first-born obligations, the gentile must pay for it with money in addition to pulling it by hand."
> — Tosfos, Avodah Zarah 71a

**Requires both payment and physical transfer**

---

## Acquiring Through Property

**Kinyan Chatzer:**
> "A person's courtyard acquires for him (even) without his knowledge. Even if he is not standing there, as soon as a gift enters his property, it is as if someone else had claimed the gift on his behalf."
> — Rambam, Laws of Ownerless Property & Gifts 4:9

**The Problem:**
- One opinion: Chatzer extended from hand
- Other opinion: Chatzer functions like agent (shliach)
- But: "A gentile may not act as an agent for a Jew for any issue" (Rambam, Laws of Agents 2:1)

**Issue:** If chatzer works through agency, won't work for gentile

---

## Acquiring Through Exchange

**Biblical Source:**
> "And this was the custom in Israel regarding sale and exchange… a man shall take off his shoe and hand to his friend, and this was the proof in Israel."
> — Ruth 4:7

**The Problem:**
> "Acquiring through exchange is not binding for a gentile."
> — Sifsei Cohen, Choshen Mishpat 123:30

---

## The Situmta Method

**Talmudic Source:**
> "This Situmta acquires. For what purpose?... In a place where this is used to actually acquire, it acquires."
> — Bava Metzia 74a

**Two Explanations:**

**Rashi:**
> "Situmta means a label that was marked onto the wine merchant's barrels to indicate that a customer had purchased this part of his inventory."

**Rabbeinu Chananel:**
> "Situmta is a handshake (or any other locally recognised act) that merchants commonly do to indicate the closing of a deal."

**Question:**
> "Consider this: How have diamond dealers typically closed their deals? Is this relevant in other industries?"

---

## The Practical Method

### Kinyan Agav (Acquisition Via Land)

> "Rabba said: If the debtor transferred movable property to the creditor as liened property by means of an acquisition of land, the creditor acquires the land and acquires the movable property, i.e., a lien is created with regard to both."
> — Talmud, Bava Basra 44b

---

### Requirements

**Kitzur Shulchan Aruch:**
> "One must write the name of the buyer, the price, and that he acquires the chometz by way of renting the [section of the] property… one must discuss the contents of the sale agreement verbally as well, and the buyer must pay a down payment and the document must include a commitment that the buyer will pay the remainder of the purchase value."
> — Kitzur Shulchan Aruch, 114:2

**Mishneh Berurah:**
> "The chometz sale should conform to local (secular) law."
> — Mishneh Berurah 448:17/9

---

## But the Gentile Hasn't Paid in Full?

### Terumas HaDeshen

**Question:** Can give chometz to gentile knowing he won't touch it and will re-gift after Pesach?

**Response:**
> "It would seem that this is permitted, as long as he gives the chometz to the gentile wholeheartedly, without reservation or conditions or if he sells the chometz to the gentile for a nominal amount."
> — Responsa, Terumas HaDeshen 1:120

**Nominal amount sufficient**

---

### Talmudic Precedent

> "Consider a case where Reuven sold a field to Shimon with a guarantee that if it is repossessed he will compensate Shimon for his loss. Shimon did not pay for the purchase, and instead set up the value of the field as a loan by writing a promissory note for this amount."
> — Talmud, Kesuvos 92a

**Payment can be structured as loan**

---

## Important Considerations for the Sale

### Partitioning

> "If one needs to use any of the rooms where the chometz is housed, one must make a partition in front of the chometz and sell the partitioned section… and note in the sale agreement that the gentile has free access to the area where the chometz is housed and that he remains entitled to on-sell the chometz to another gentile over Pesach."
> — Kitzur Shulchan Aruch, 114:4

---

### No Re-Sale Condition

> "One may not include a clause, as a condition of sale, that the gentile must re-sell the chometz to its original owner after Pesach."
> — Kitzur Shulchan Aruch, 114:6

**Must be unconditional**

---

### Include Name and Address

> "The sale agreement should include the name of the seller and his address, so that the gentile could [theoretically] come claim his chometz over Pesach."
> — Mishneh Berurah 448:12

---

## Can You Sell Someone Else's Chometz?

> "One may sell chometz on behalf of someone who did not manage to sell theirs, because we are permitted to benefit someone without their knowledge."
> — Pischei Teshuvah, Yoreh Deah, 320:6, Siddur Pesach Kehilchoso, 11:7

---

## Can You Sell Your Chometz Online?

> "You don't sell your chometz online, you simply appoint the relevant rabbi as your agent to sell your chometz. This is essentially what we do when 'selling' our chometz 'to the rabbi'. The rabbi does not buy the chometz from the community, but acts as their agent to facilitate their sale of chometz."

> "One need not be in their presence to appoint someone as an agent."
> — Responsa, Rabbi Shmuel Eliyahu, Chief Rabbi of Tzfat

---

## Something to Think About

> "In 2006, the Chief Rabbi of the IDF landed up selling a few hundred dogs with the army's chometz. Any thoughts why he would have done this?"

**Reason:** Dog food contains chametz, must sell dogs with their food

---

## Do I Need Utensils to Mikveh?

> "Chometz that is in a container that would need to be immersed in the mikveh should not be sold to the gentile together with the container, because then one would need to immerse the container after buying the chometz back after Pesach."
> — Kitzur Shulchan Aruch, 114:2

**Solution:** Sell chametz only, not containers

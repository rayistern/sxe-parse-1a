## Forum Discussion

**Source:** http://www.shluchimexchange.com/en/forum/viewtopic.php?t=467823

**Topic:** Time sensitive - need advice for meeting with city who wants to copy public lighting event, replacing ours

---

## Situation

**From:** [REDACTED-NAME]

We are planning the first public menorah lighting in our city. We confirmed the place we will be doing it and got the Mayor's confirmation that he will be joining.

**The Problem:**
Then someone from the Mayor's office contacted me and told me that:
- The Mayor is only available on a different day
- The city likes the idea (of a public menorah lighting) so much that they are planning to do it (with our menorah in the place that we arranged) every day that we are not doing it
- They will have the mayor attend on a different day than our event
- They plan to do their own publicity for a community event on that day

**Meeting:** Tomorrow IYH to talk about this

**Question:** Has anyone else dealt with this (or something similar) or have any advice?

---

## Response 1: One Big Community Event

**Key Arguments to Present:**

### Strength in Numbers
"You might want to point out that you will bring your community and it is more effective to combine efforts for one big night"

### Professional Event Management
"Plus... we in Chabad are good marketers and can bring people that wouldn't come otherwise plus you'll make it more fun with party kids entertainment etc which they won't do"

### People Care About the Party
"People care more about a good community Chanukah party than the lighting itself (the lighting gets old after a year or 2 I've noticed)"

### Community Building
"You can tell them the reason you want this to be one coordination is because in a small town it is important that people get to know other people in the Jewish community so the more the merrier"

### Avoid Duplication
"It would be silly to have 2 parties for a smallish Jewish community"

### Mayor Substitute
"If the mayor can't be there on that day have him send other city representative on his behalf - it works just fine"

### Quality Over Bureaucracy
"Bottom line: with you involved it will be on a much grander scale and they will look strange doing their own thing (besides that your party will be better etc)"

### Bring Support
"Maybe bring another community member with you to share their perspective like yours"

---

## Response 2: Clarify and Accommodate

**Questions to Ask:**

**Lighting Every Night:**
> "But you are lighting it every night, aren't you?"

**Accommodation Option:**
> "Maybe change the date of your 'big event' to accommodate mayor."

**Community Event vs. Chabad Event:**
> "Also explain to them that its not 'your' event but its The Community event."

**Facilitate City's Participation:**
> "You are thrilled that they want there to be a lighting event each night and you will be happy to facilitate."

---

## Strategy Framework

### Option 1: Partner with City

**Approach:**
- Frame as "community event" not "Chabad event"
- Offer to facilitate/organize city's desired daily lightings
- Coordinate one major event on a date mayor can attend
- Leverage city's resources and publicity

**Benefits:**
- Official city backing
- Mayor's participation
- City publicity machine
- Broader community reach

**Risks:**
- Loss of control
- Diluted messaging
- Potential for religious neutralization

---

### Option 2: Maintain Independence

**Approach:**
- Keep your event on your date
- Explain superior programming and community draw
- Position as established community tradition
- Offer to assist city separately if they insist

**Benefits:**
- Control over content and messaging
- Authentic Chabad event
- Clear Jewish identity
- Professional execution

**Risks:**
- Competing events split crowd
- City does own thing anyway
- Mayor attends theirs, not yours

---

### Option 3: Flexible Accommodation

**Approach:**
- Willing to change date to accommodate mayor
- But maintain control of programming
- Partner with city for publicity
- Make it "official" city event organized by Chabad

**Benefits:**
- Mayor attendance
- City support
- Maintained control
- Best of both worlds

---

## Key Negotiation Points

### What to Emphasize

**1. Your Expertise:**
- "We know how to make this successful"
- "We have the connections and resources"
- "We understand how to engage the Jewish community"

**2. Community Unity:**
- "Small town needs one unified event"
- "Splitting the community is counterproductive"
- "More Jews together is better"

**3. Quality Programming:**
- "Entertainment, food, activities for kids"
- "Professional organization"
- "Proven track record"

**4. Flexibility:**
- "Happy to accommodate mayor's schedule"
- "Open to partnership with city"
- "Want to make this work for everyone"

**5. Win-Win Scenario:**
- "City gets credit"
- "Mayor gets positive publicity"
- "Community gets great event"
- "Jewish community gets meaningful celebration"

---

## What to Avoid

**Don't Say:**
- "This is our event" (sounds territorial)
- "You can't do this" (sounds confrontational)
- "We did all the work" (sounds whiny)
- "It's a religious event" (triggers church/state concerns)

**Do Say:**
- "Community event"
- "Partnership"
- "Accommodate"
- "Coordinate"
- "Cultural celebration"

---

## Preparation for Meeting

### Bring With You

**1. Community Support:**
- Another community member
- Letters from community leaders
- List of expected attendees

**2. Professional Materials:**
- Past event photos/videos
- Publicity materials
- Program outline
- Budget (if appropriate)

**3. Flexibility:**
- Alternative dates
- Partnership proposals
- Accommodation options

---

### Meeting Goals

**Primary:** Maintain control of event programming while getting city/mayor support

**Acceptable:** Partnership where Chabad organizes, city promotes

**Fallback:** Separate events on different days (less ideal)

**Worst Case:** City does their own thing (avoid this)

---

## Long-Term Considerations

### Building Relationship

**This Year:**
- Focus on successful partnership
- Exceed expectations
- Create positive experience

**Future Years:**
- Established tradition
- City comes to you
- Smoother coordination
- Automatic mayor participation

### Political Capital

**Use Wisely:**
- This situation creates obligation
- City will "owe you one"
- Builds relationship with mayor's office
- Opens doors for other initiatives

---

## Final Thoughts

**The Situation:**
City wants to hijack your event → Turn into opportunity for partnership

**The Strategy:**
Emphasize community unity, professional execution, and flexibility

**The Goal:**
One great event with city backing and mayor attendance, under your control

**The Key:**
Frame it as "us helping them" not "them taking over"

**Remember:**
You bring expertise, community connections, and programming. They bring official backing and the mayor. Both sides need each other for maximum success.

Good luck with the meeting!

# [Misc > For Writer > _5 Chanukah > General] Chanukah Class - 8 Candles, 8 Lessons

## Eight Lessons from the Chanukah Lights

**Format:** Short, quotable lessons with explanations - perfect for brief divrei Torah or social media posts

---

## Lesson #1: Light Dispels Darkness

### The Core Principle

> "Darkness, no matter how ominous and intimidating, is not a thing or force: it is merely the absence of light. So light need not combat and overpower darkness in order to displace it -- where light is, darkness is not. A thimbleful of light will therefore banish a roomful of darkness."

### Application to Good and Evil

> "The same is true of good and evil: evil is not a thing or force, but merely the absence or concealment of good. One need not 'defeat' the evil in the world; one need only bring to light its inherent goodness."

---

### Supporting Quotes

**Eleanor Roosevelt:**
> "It is better to light a candle than curse the darkness."

**Mahatma Gandhi:**
> "Be the change you want to see in the world."

---

### Biblical Foundation

**Creation Story:**
> "Darkness was not a creation – Veha'aretz hayasoh uvavohu vechoshech al pnei sehom. And in the next posuk – Vayehi or."

**The Point:**
Darkness existed as default. Light was created. Light is active; darkness is passive. We don't fight darkness - we bring light.

---

### Practical Application

**Agricultural Metaphor:**
> "You only need one tiny seed - for a tall tree, with many fruits and seeds to grow."

**Takeaway:**
- Don't focus on combating evil
- Focus on bringing good
- One small act of kindness can multiply
- Start where you are with what you have

---

## Lesson #2: Never Be Satisfied - Keep Growing

### The Pattern of Adding

> "Each night of Chanukah we add a light. This teaches us that we must avoid self-satisfaction in spiritual matters. If we are given another day--it is to accomplish something positive we have not yet achieved."

---

### The $86,400 Parable

**The Game:**
Every morning, you get $86,400 deposited into your bank account for your use.

**Rule #1:**
Everything that you didn't spend is taken away at the end of the day. You can't transfer it or save it. It simply disappears.

**Rule #2:**
This game can end whenever; at any time it can say, the game is over. The account can close and you will not receive a new one.

**Rule #3:**
Tomorrow morning, another $86,400 will be deposited into your bank account.

---

### The Question

> "What would you personally do? You would buy anything and everything you wanted? Not only for yourself, but for all people you love? Even for people you don't know, because you couldn't possibly spend it all on yourself? You would try to spend every cent, and use it all?"

---

### The Revelation

**The Reality:**
> "Each morning we awaken to receive 86,400 seconds as a gift of life, and when we go to sleep at night, any remaining time is NOT credited to us. What we haven't lived up that day is forever lost. Yesterday is forever gone. Each morning the account is refilled, but the bank can dissolve your account at any time… without warning."

**The Challenge:**
> "So, what will YOU do with your 86,400 seconds? Aren't they worth so much more than the same amount in dollars? Enjoy every second of your life, because time races by so much quicker than you think. So take care of yourself, be happy, love deeply, and enjoy life."

---

## Lesson #3: Share Your Light - Don't Be Spiritually Selfish

### Placement of Menorah

> "We kindle the Chanukah lights by a door or a window--exits to our home. It is not enough to light up our own lives; we must share what we know to be good with others. Spiritual selfishness is the worst kind of all."

---

### Temple Windows

**Design Detail:**
Menorah in the Temple - windows illuminating outward.

**Unlike regular windows:**
- Most ancient buildings: windows narrow outward (for defense, light comes IN)
- Temple windows: narrow inward, wide outward (light goes OUT)
- Pirsumei nisa - publicizing the miracle

---

### The Tzaddik in the Pelz

**Yiddish Story:**
A righteous man (tzaddik) sits in his warm fur coat (pelz) complaining about how cold it is in the synagogue.

**Rabbi's Response:**
Instead of wearing a coat to keep yourself warm, why not create a fire that warms everyone?

**Lesson:**
Don't just insulate yourself - create warmth that others can share.

---

## Lesson #4: Address Your Own Darkness First

### Timing of Lighting

> "We kindle the Chanukah lights only when it becomes dark in our spot of the globe. We must devote our efforts to the particular darkness we see in our lives and communities. If there is a darkness in our 'place,' there is where we must bring our light."

---

### The Challenge of Distance

**Easy vs. Hard:**
> "Sometimes it is easier to support a cause far away – hunger in Africa, etc than the homeless man in the corner. It is easier to address problems and issues that you are not involved in."

**The Priority:**
> "Aniyay ircha kodmim. Help those around you, your own family, your neighbor, etc."

**Translation:** The poor of your city come first.

---

### Why Distance is Easier

- Less personal involvement
- No ongoing relationship required
- No need to confront your own discomfort
- Can feel good without getting messy
- One-time donation vs. ongoing commitment

**The Torah Way:**
Deal with the darkness in your own sphere first. Your family, your neighbors, your community.

---

## Lesson #5: Body, Soul, and Torah Working Together

### The Foundation

**Proverbs 20:27:**
> "The soul of man is a lamp of G-d."

**The Principle:**
> "Man is a complex conglomeration of heaven and earth, spirit and matter, fire and wick."

---

### Three Elements of the Lamp

**Like the lamp, man has three elements:**

1. **The Wick** = Body
2. **The Flame** = Soul
3. **The Oil** = Torah

---

### The Soul (Flame)

**Character:**
> "His soul, like the flame, is aflame with yearning and inspiration. It burns brightly within him, inspiring him to connect to his Creator. It desires fiercely to break the bonds of this physical world, to transcend the barriers of a material existence and unite with its Maker."

**Movement:**
- Flames always reach upward
- Wants to break free from physical
- Yearns to unite with Source
- BUT: also falls back down to illuminate this world

**Purpose:**
> "When allowed full expression, the soul encourages man to seek spiritual pursuits, to almost tear itself free from the gravity of physical reality that grounds it. Yet, like the flame of the candle, after the soul performs its celestial dance it also falls back, returning to illuminate this world and leave its mark on physical reality."

**The Paradox:**
> "By cleaving to the ultimate Source, the soul would be encompassed within it. Only as it exists within a seemingly distinct being can the soul accomplish the very purpose of its descent."

---

### The Body (Wick)

**Character:**
> "The body of man, like the wick of the candle, is both grounded and physical. With its many limitations, many real needs and desires, the body entraps and enslaves the soul within it."

**But Also Enables:**
> "Yet, while imposing its restrictions, the body also houses the soul and provides an outlet for its unique expression. The soul can only feel, perceive and savor reality through the body's senses; it can only move about freely with the body's limbs; and it can only think and analyze with the body's mind. The body's very groundedness, then, provides a means and opportunity for the soul's relationship with creation."

**The Necessity:**
Without a wick, the flame cannot exist in this world. Without a body, the soul cannot accomplish its mission.

---

### The Torah (Oil)

**Character:**
> "Torah, like the oil of the lamp, is the source for man's ideals, providing the direction for a meaningful life that connects him with his Creator. Mitzvot ignite man's potential, showing him in a concrete way how to utilize his abilities and talents for a G-dly end."

**Qualities:**
> "Enlightening, pure and clear, like the oil, the Torah and its mitzvot direct the potential in man to its ultimate purpose."

---

### Becoming a Lamp of G-d

**Ecclesiastes 3:21:**
> "The spirit of man gravitates upward."

**The Formula:**
> "When man is true to the yearnings of his soul, directs them with the wisdom and enlightenment of Torah, puts them into practice by fulfilling the mitzvot with the faculties and limbs of his body, he generates light and becomes 'a lamp of G-d.'"

**All Three Required:**
- Soul provides yearning
- Torah provides direction
- Body provides action
- Together: Light

## Lesson #6: Float Above, Permeate Within (Like Oil)

### Oil's Paradox

> "Oil is a strange substance with two very opposite properties. On one hand, it always floats to the top. No matter how hard you try, it just won't mix with other liquids. Yet, at the same time, when oil goes somewhere, it goes all the way through. It's this sort of saturation that makes oil-based stains so tough to remove. So it both floats above and permeates within. Go figure!"

---

### Jews Are Like Oil

**Property #1: We Float Above (Stay Distinct)**

> "On one hand, we are quite different from those around us. We must treasure our distinct identity, being proud to look and act like Jews do. We are the ancient tribe that never assimilated, that's here today just as we were three and a half thousand years ago."

**Gentile Perspective:**
> "And how do others view this? Look for yourself: Gentiles respect Jews who respect themselves--and are embarrassed of Jews who are embarrassed of themselves."

**The Lesson:**
Maintain Jewish identity proudly. Don't blend in to the point of disappearing.

---

**Property #2: We Permeate Within (Spread Influence)**

> "Yet at the same time, we mix freely among the peoples of the world. You'd think a tribe like ours would stick to Israel and parts of Brooklyn. But that just isn't so. For the Jews are 'a light onto nations.' We are all ambassadors of G-d sent here on a mission to bring holiness and goodness to all those around us. We are role models whose good deeds are to rub off upon others."

**The Balance:**
- Distinct identity (oil floats)
- Widespread influence (oil permeates)
- Both simultaneously

---

### The Process: Squeezing the Olive

**To Produce Oil:**
> "Additionally, in order to produce oil, you have to squeeze the olive. Sometimes challenges and obstacles bring forth strength, and causes one to shine."

**Application:**
- Pressure reveals essence
- Hardship brings out best
- Challenges = opportunities for growth
- Your brightest light may come from your hardest times

## Lesson #7: Sharing Doesn't Diminish You

### The Principle

**Buddha Quote:**
> "Thousands of candles can be lit from a single one, and its life will not be shortened. Happiness never decreases by being shared."

---

### What This Applies To

**Not Just Candles:**
- Knowledge
- Acts of kindness
- Wisdom
- Love
- Joy
- Inspiration
- Teaching
- Support

**The Paradox:**
> "Not only do they not take anything away from you, you get so much from it. To give is to get."

---

### Examples

**Knowledge:**
When you teach someone, you don't lose what you know. In fact, teaching deepens your own understanding.

**Kindness:**
Helping others doesn't deplete your capacity for goodness. It increases it.

**Happiness:**
Sharing joy with others multiplies rather than divides it.

**The Formula:**
- Material goods: Giving = having less
- Spiritual goods: Giving = having more

---

### The Song

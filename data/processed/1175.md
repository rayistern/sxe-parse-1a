# Model Matzah Bakery - "Back to the Exodus" Full Production Guide

**Type**: Event Production Script and Props List
**Program**: Interactive Model Matzah Bakery Show
**Theme**: "Back to the Exodus" - immersive Pesach story experience before participants make matzah
**Format**: Multi-act theatrical presentation followed by hands-on matzah baking
**Audience**: Children and families (pre-Pesach programming)

## Program Overview

This is an interactive Model Matzah Bake show—a full experience that immerses participants in the Pesach story before they actually make their own matzah. The document provides organized breakdown of props and items needed for each act.

**Most Common Format**: Showing wheat stalks → milling them → making the dough → allowing each participant to make their own matzah

## Matzah Recipe Options

**Option 1:**
- 2 cups regular flour
- 1 cup whole wheat flour
- 1 cup water

**Option 2:**
- 3 1/4 cups white flour
- 3/4 cups whole wheat flour
- 1 1/3 cups water

## Matzah Rolling Paper

- Large rolls of thick parchment paper are ideal

## Aprons & Hats

- Can get these with the Matzah Bakery logo from Shluchim selling websites

## Oven Considerations

### Portable Oven Option

**Requirements**:
- Portable oven that plugs into 110 outlet needed to bake matzah
- **Challenges**:
  - Can be expensive
  - Take long time to bake matzah even with stone placed at bottom to speed up
  - Often trigger smoke alarms

**Recommendation**: "If you're using a venue's kitchen, it's best to let them manage the oven"

### Toaster Oven Option

- High-quality toaster oven with tile placed at bottom
- Helps bake matzah faster
- Smaller commercial ovens (typically ~$100) can work well

### Portable Stove Top Option

- Use portable stove top to bake matzah directly on it
- **Important**: Be sure to tovel (immerse in mikvah) the stove top before use

## Act-by-Act Props List

### Act 1: Introduction/Egypt Theme

**Technology**:
- Jcrafts wristbands
- Speakers
- All soundtracks
- Large button
- Large hammer
- Smoke machine
- Bubble machine
- Flashing lights
- Red flashing lights

**Set Design**:
- Backdrop of "Back to the Exodus" / Egypt

### Act 2: Slavery and Exodus Story

**Props**:
- Foam bricks (representing slavery labor)

- Sneh (small tree on flashing light - burning bush)
- Martini glass cup
- Water

- Plastic frogs (frog plague)
- Bottle sprayers (for plagues effects)

**Costumes**:
- Ancient Jew costume
- Ancient Egyptian costume
- Moses costume
- Moses stick (staff)

- Wheat stalks (order from JewishYouthPrograms.com for ones with actual kernels)
- Barley kernels
- Wheat mill grinder
- Wheat bowl
- Sifter
- Large bowl to mix dough
- Flour
- Pitcher
- Water

**Costumes**:
- Farmer costume (overalls and hat)

**Theme**: Kemach + Mayim station concept

### Act 4: Actual Matzah Baking

**Safety/Hygiene**:
- Aprons
- Hats
- Gloves
- Pins (likely for securing aprons/hats)

**Equipment**:
- Oven
- Paper bags to roll matzah on
- Pizza server to take matzah out of oven

## For Shluchim: Production and Implementation Guide

### Budget Planning

**Startup Costs** (First Year):
- Props/costumes: $500-1,500 (depending on quality and sourcing)
- Backdrops: $100-300
- Wheat stalks/grains: $50-100
- Equipment (mill, oven): $100-500
- Aprons/hats (custom logo): $200-400 for 50 sets
- Flour/baking supplies: $50-100
- **Total First Year**: $1,000-3,000

**Recurring Costs** (Subsequent Years):
- Wheat stalks/grains: $50-100
- Flour/baking supplies: $50-100
- Replacement items: $50-150
- **Total Recurring**: $150-350

**Revenue Potential**:
- Charge $15-25 per child
- 50 participants = $750-1,250
- Can cover recurring costs and contribute to amortizing startup

### Venue Requirements

**Space Needs**:
- Performance area for Acts 1-3 (theatrical presentation)
- Tables/workspace for Act 4 (matzah making)
- Oven access (kitchen or portable)
- Seating for audience/participants
- **Minimum**: 1,000-1,500 sq ft
- **Ideal**: 2,000+ sq ft

**Kitchen Access**:
- If venue has kitchen with oven: Coordinate usage, test temperature
- If using portable: Ensure adequate electrical (110V outlets), test smoke alarm sensitivity
- Ventilation important (ovens and smoke effects)

### Staffing

**Minimum Team**:
- Lead presenter/actor (often Shliach or experienced educator)
- Assistant for tech (lights, sound, smoke/bubble machines)
- 2-3 volunteers for Act 4 (helping kids roll matzah, managing oven)

**Optional**:
- Actors for different roles (Moses, Egyptian, farmer) - can be older kids/teens
- Photographer/videographer for marketing future events

### Timeline and Logistics

**Pre-Event** (2-3 months before):
- Book venue
- Order custom aprons/hats if needed
- Acquire reusable props (costumes, backdrops, equipment)

**Pre-Event** (2-4 weeks before):
- Order wheat stalks (fresh for show)
- Finalize registration (capacity planning)
- Recruit volunteers
- Test all tech (sound, lights, machines)

**Pre-Event** (1 week before):
- Purchase flour, water (fresh)
- Prepare dough if doing demo first
- Set up backdrops if venue allows early access

**Day Of**:
- Arrive 2-3 hours early for setup
- Test oven temperature
- Lay out workspace for Act 4
- Sound check
- Brief volunteers

**Event Duration**:
- Acts 1-3: 30-45 minutes
- Act 4 (matzah making): 30-45 minutes
- Total: 60-90 minutes

### Age Considerations

**Preschool (3-5)**:
- Shorter Acts 1-3 (20-30 min)
- More hands-on, less lecture
- Adult helpers for each child in Act 4
- Safety critical (oven kept far from kids)

**Elementary (6-12)**:
- Full program works well
- Can handle more story detail
- Some independence in Act 4
- Older kids can help younger

**Family Program (All Ages)**:
- Parents participate with younger children
- Entertainment value in Acts 1-3 for adults too
- Family teams for matzah making

### Safety Protocols

**Oven Safety**:
- Adult-only oven operation
- Oven mitt stations
- Clear oven perimeter (rope/tape marking)
- Fire extinguisher accessible

**Food Allergies**:
- Pre-registration asks about wheat/gluten allergies
- Alternative activity for allergic children (observing, helping with non-flour tasks)

**Tech Safety**:
- Smoke machines: Check venue fire alarm sensitivity, inform venue management
- Bubble machines: Non-slip solution, mop spills immediately
- Electrical: No exposed cords, tape down cables, GFCI outlets for water-near areas

### Enhancement Ideas

**Act Integration**:
- QR codes on wristbands linking to Pesach resources
- "Scientist" theme could explore "science" of keeping flour dry, timing under 18 minutes
- Ten Plagues could be more elaborate with additional effects
- Burning bush (Sneh) could have voice recording of calling Moshe

**Takeaways**:
- Kids take home their matzah (package immediately after baking)
- Certificate of participation
- Recipe card for making at home
- Mini Haggadah or Pesach coloring book

**Marketing**:
- Video clip from previous year
- Photos of kids in aprons/hats
- "Make your own matzah!" headline
- Early bird discount
- Sibling discount

### Model Variations

**Express Version** (30-45 minutes total):
- Skip elaborate Acts 1-2
- Quick story overview
- Focus on wheat-to-matzah (Act 3-4)
- Good for school visits with time limits

**Deluxe Version** (2+ hours):
- All four acts fully developed
- Sit-down Seder simulation with matzah they made
- Full meal (simple: matzah ball soup, salad)
- Parents invited for last 30 minutes

**School Assembly Version**:
- Performance focus (Acts 1-3)
- Demo matzah making (one volunteer makes, all watch)
- Take-home: Store-bought shmurah matzah for each child
- 45 minutes total

**Traveling Program**:
- Portable setup (lightweight backdrops, collapsible props)
- Battery-powered equipment where possible
- Partner with schools/JCCs
- No oven (demo only or cold "pretend" matzah)

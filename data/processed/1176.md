# Model Matzah Bakery - Practical Implementation Guide

**Type**: Program Overview and Resource Guide
**Topic**: Running Model Matzah Bakery as pre-Pesach program
**Format**: Condensed practical instructions with resource links
**Related**: File 1175 (detailed theatrical version)

## Program Overview

Model matzah bakry [sic] is a "go to before pesach" program. "Its a easy program do make and it teaches the kids not just how to make matzah also the story of yezias mitraim [Exodus from Egypt]"

## Most Common Show Format

1. Showing wheat stalks
2. Milling them
3. Making the dough
4. Giving it out for each to make their own matzah

## Oven Options and Challenges

**When Bringing Your Own Oven**:
- Expensive because need portable one
- Must plug into 110 outlet
- Must bake matzas fast ("so u dont have to be there a whole day")
- Problem: "there has been times that the oven caused the smoke alrm to go off"
- **Solution**: "if u give to there kmitchen then its there headache"

**Alternative Oven Options**:
- Good toaster oven with tile on bottom to make baking faster
- Smaller commercial ovens (~$100) "work good"
- Portable stove top - place matzah on it (remember to tovel)

**Note**: "The cheap [pizza] ovens most say they are not good"

## Program Variations

"If going to the same location for a few years or bored of the same act there is for different acts that could be done for the first part then go into the matzah baking"

## For Shluchim: Quick Implementation Tips

### Key Practical Insight

**Venue Kitchen Strategy**: The critical insight here is using the venue's kitchen/oven rather than bringing portable. This:
- Shifts smoke alarm liability to venue
- Eliminates portable oven expense
- Faster baking with proper oven
- Less equipment to transport
- Builds venue relationship

### Resource Priorities

**Essential Purchases**:
1. Wheat stalks with kernels (JewishYouthPrograms.com - only good source)
2. Flour (any grocery)
3. Parchment paper (bulk rolls)
4. Aprons/hats with logo (Shluchim websites) - creates professional look

**Can Get Free/Cheap**:
- Wheat kernels (Amazon, inexpensive)
- Venue's oven (coordinate with venue)
- Water (venue)
- Bowls/mixing equipment (borrow or venue)

### Avoiding Common Pitfalls

**Don't Buy**:
- "Cheap pizza ovens" - reportedly not good
- Wheat stalks without kernels (Amazon ones) - hollow/fake looking
- Expensive portable oven if venue has kitchen

**Do Test**:
- Venue oven temperature and baking time
- Smoke alarm sensitivity before event
- Recipe proportions for your specific flour

### Program Evolution

If running annually at same location, vary the first part (story/introduction) to keep it fresh while maintaining the core wheat-to-matzah process.

### Video Strategy

- Show Chabad.org video to kids as introduction or conclusion
- Use for volunteer training (watch what successful program looks like)
- Share links with parents before event (builds excitement, sets expectations)

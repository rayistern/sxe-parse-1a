# [Misc > For Writer > _5 Chanukah > General] Yehudis Speech for Women's Event

## Sample Speech for Chanukah Women's Program

**Format:** Complete speech ready to deliver at women's Chanukah event

**Length:** Approximately 8-10 minutes

**Tone:** Warm, inclusive, inspiring

---

## Opening: Welcome and Joke

### The Greeting

> "Good evening everyone! If you can please find your seat, we'd love to start. Before I get started, I would like to stress a tremendous Thank You to …Thank you all for making this evening possible, and for giving the women in our community a time to learn, socialize, say a little l'chaim and get in to the Chanukah spirit!"

**[Insert names of sponsors, volunteers, organizers]**

---

### The Chanukah Stamp Joke

**Setup:**
> "Y'know, they tell a story… A woman goes to the post office to buy stamps for her Chanukah cards. She says to the clerk 'May I have 50 Chanukah stamps please.'"

**Punchline:**
> "'What denomination?' asks the clerk.
>
> The woman says 'Oy vey...my god, has it come to this?
> Okay, give me 6 orthodox, 12 conservative and 32 reform!'"

---

### The Message of Unity

> "Well, friends, I am proud that here in Chabad there are no labels. What matters is that we're Jewish, we're sisters, and we're celebrating Chanukah together."

---

## Body: The Greek Threat

### Transition

> "So, I'd like to share a thought on Chanukah. **PAUSE…**"

---

### Not Like Other Enemies

**The Distinction:**
> "The Greeks of the Chanukah story were not like the Nazis or the Arab terrorists, who go after Jewish blood just for the sake of killing Jews. The Greeks didn't actually want to kill the Jews."

**Their Offer:**
> "They came to the Jews with one request: become like us, so we can all live happily ever after..."

---

### The Greek Mindset

**Their Logic:**
> "And the Greeks thought the Jews would agree to become more like them! They figured, who in their right mind wouldn't want to join the wave of the future, and give up this musty, old-fashioned religion called Judaism?"

**Their Questions:**
- What did the Jews have against philosophy anyway?
- Or gladiators?
- Or idols?
- Or other things from Greek culture?
- Why would any enlightened human being have anything against progress?

---

### The Enforcement

**The Decrees:**
> "To accomplish this, the Greeks outlawed the observance of Shabbat, and circumcision, and Torah study."

---

### The Tragic Success

**Most Jews Assimilated:**
> "And, the Greeks weren't totally wrong. Most Jews were sucked in by the Greeks. These Jews started dressing like Greeks, thinking like Greeks and praying like Greeks. Within a generation or two, the descendants of these assimilated Jews were no longer members of the Jewish people."

---

## The Heroes

### The Maccabees

**Who They Were:**
> "The heroes of the Chanukah story, the Maccabees, were different. This was a single family that refused to give in. This family dared to take on the whole Syrian-Greek empire on behalf of the few Jews who courageously remained loyal to the Jewish tradition. They were led, of course, by Judah the Maccabee."

---

### The Forgotten Heroine

**The Introduction:**
> "But did you know that Judah the Maccabee had an aunt? A lone Maccabee-ette. She was a young widow named Yehudis, and she was as wise and pious as she was beautiful."

---

## Yehudis's Story

### The Siege

**The Situation:**
> "Yehudit lived in a village that bravely refused to give up the Torah and assimilate in order to live happily ever after. In response, the Greeks laid siege to the village, cutting off their food and water supply, until its residents were starving and dying of thirst. Reluctantly, the village leaders decided to submit to the Greek demands."

---

### One Woman's Courage

**Her Decision:**
> "But one brave woman felt otherwise. Yehudit dressed up in her most beautiful gown, put on her fanciest jewelry and her most expensive perfume, and risked her life as she set out for the military camp of the evil Greek governor, Holofernes."

**The Meeting:**
> "The governor noticed the beautiful Jewish woman and invited her for a private audience in his tent."

---

### The Victory

**Her Plan:**
> "Yehudit got the governor so drunk that he fell asleep. Instead of the romantic encounter Holofernes had counted on, Yehudit used the general's sword to behead him as he slept, setting the stage for a surprise military victory for the loyal Jews of her village."

---

## The Lesson

### Transition

> "**[Pause]**"

---

### What We Learn

**The Core Message:**
> "We can learn many lessons from Yehudit's story, but most importantly, this brave Jewish woman teaches us that being a good Jew is something that often requires courage and self-sacrifice."

---

### Modern Context

**Personal Reflection:**
> "The story of Yehudis reminds me, personally, that it is one thing to be a Jew in a place like Jerusalem, or Brooklyn, where there are three synagogues on your block and dozens of kosher restaurants within smelling distance. But it is quite another thing to be a Jew practicing his or her religion in a place where Judaism is considered exotic at best, and musty and old-fashioned at worst."

---

### Our Special Opportunity

**The Challenge:**
> "But, it is specifically in these places that we have a special opportunity to live up to Yehudit's legacy, and to bring the special light of Chanukah into the world. **PAUSE..**"

---

## Modern-Day Yehudits

### The Office

> "The Jew who raises questions when she turns down a slice of her coworker's famous baked ham at her office's annual X-mas party… **SHE is a modern-day Yehudit.**"

---

### The Window

> "The Jewish woman who raises eyebrows by putting a menorah in her window when all her friends have flickering lights outside their windows… **SHE is a modern-day Yehudit.**"

---

### The Classroom

> "The Jew who raises awareness by giving a presentation on Chanukah to children of diverse backgrounds in her son's first-grade classroom… **is a modern-day Yehudit.**"

---

### The Home

> "Or the mother who sits her children down, and patiently explains to them that they should be proud to be the only kids in their class without an x-mas tree, and that they are part of a nation that for 2000 years were willing to sacrifice their lives for their beliefs… **She is a modern-day Yehudit.**"

---

## Closing Story

### The College Menorah

**A Friend's Experience:**
> "A friend once shared with me that when she was a freshman in college, she walked by the 30-foot X-mas tree in the Student Union every day. Then, one day, she noticed a small paper menorah that one brave Jewish student hung up beside it."

---

### The Impact

**The Revelation:**
> "To this day, she said, I have no idea who that student was. But I can tell you one thing for sure. **That student's name was Yehudit.**"

---

## Final Words

> "Thank you, and happy Chanukah!"

---

## Speaker Notes

### Delivery Tips

**Pacing:**
- Start warm and welcoming
- Joke should be light and relatable
- Historical section: more serious tone
- Yehudis story: dramatic build-up
- Modern applications: personal and intimate
- College story: slow down, let it land
- Final line: pause before delivering

**Emphasis:**
- "SHE is a modern-day Yehudit" - strong emphasis each time
- Pauses matter - don't rush
- Make eye contact during modern examples
- Personal touch when talking about children

---

### Customization Points

**Personalize:**
- Add local sponsors' names in opening
- Include specific examples from your community
- Reference your own experiences
- Adapt X-mas party example to local context
- Add your own "modern Yehudit" examples

**Expand If Needed:**
- More details on Greek philosophy vs. Torah
- Explain why Greeks opposed specifically chukkim
- Add more about Yehudis's character
- Include other Chanukah heroines
- Develop cheese-eating custom

**Shorten If Needed:**
- Skip or simplify the joke
- Condense Greek context
- Reduce number of modern examples
- Go straight from story to lesson

---

### Audience Adaptation

**For Younger Women:**
- Add social media examples
- Discuss peer pressure
- Reference college experiences
- Talk about dating challenges

**For Mothers:**
- Emphasize parenting examples
- Discuss school challenges
- Talk about family traditions
- Share parenting tips

**For Mixed Group:**
- Keep examples broad
- Include workplace scenarios
- Discuss community involvement
- Appeal to multiple life stages

**For Senior Women:**
- Reference longer perspective
- Discuss grandparenting
- Talk about changing times
- Honor their own courage

- Be courageous in one small way

## Why This Speech Works

### Structure

**Classic Three-Part:**
1. **Opening:** Warm welcome, humor, unity message
2. **Body:** Historical context, hero story, lesson
3. **Closing:** Modern applications, inspirational ending

**Emotional Arc:**
- Start light (joke)
- Build seriousness (Greek threat)
- Peak drama (Yehudis story)
- Personal application (modern examples)
- Inspiring ending (college story)

---

- Clear takeaway message

## Variations

### Shorter Version (5 minutes)

- Skip joke or simplify
- Minimal Greek context
- Brief Yehudis story
- Three modern examples
- Quick closing

### Longer Version (15 minutes)

- Add Matityahu's daughter story
- Discuss halachic sources
- More modern examples
- Audience participation
- Extended Q&A

### Alternative Endings

**Option 1: Challenge**
"So tonight, as you light your menorah, ask yourself: How will I be a Yehudit? What will I stand up for? Who will I inspire? The world is waiting for your light."

**Option 2: Blessing**
"May we all merit to be modern-day Yehudits, bringing light to darkness, courage to fear, and the special beauty of Judaism to a world that desperately needs it."

**Option 3: Call to Action**
"Before you leave tonight, commit to one act of Jewish courage this week. Write it down. Share it with a friend. And then do it. That's how we honor Yehudis - not just by telling her story, but by living it."

---

**Bottom Line:**
This speech balances warmth with substance, history with relevance, and inspiration with practicality. It works for diverse audiences while delivering a powerful message: every Jewish woman can be a heroine. All it takes is one act of courage.

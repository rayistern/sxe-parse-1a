# [Misc > For Writer > _5 Chanukah > General] "What a Mess" - Moshiach Message

## Note: Passover Content (Not Chanukah)

**Resource Type:** Short inspirational teaching

**Author:** Rabbi Elisha Greenbaum

**Theme:** Resolving contradiction between promised redemption and current chaos

**Purpose:** Address doubts about Moshiach coming despite world appearing to deteriorate

**Format:** Essay with analogy

---

## The Contradiction

**What We're Told:**

> "You tell me that Moshiach is on his way and the world is standing on the brink of a whole new era of universal peace and tranquillity, but I don't see it."

> "You tell me that things are improving, that people are getting nicer and that good times will soon be here, but I beg to differ."

---

## What I See Instead

**The Reality:**

> "I see a mess. I see the greatest of our nation dying horrible deaths due to a tiny foreign virus. I see a world where 16 year old kids die of brain tumours and young mothers go to sleep and don't wake up. I see entire countries imploding. I see innocent people being accused of horrific crimes, while the real criminals bask in the false approval of a supine press."

**The Questions:**

> "What on earth is the world coming to and, even more frightening, where will we end up?"

> "But the Rebbe assured us that Moshiach is coming. But I can't see it. And the inherent contradiction is driving me mad. Who should I believe?"

---

## The Analogy: 10 Minutes Till Showtime

### The Scene Backstage

**Ever Produced or Acted in a Play?**

> "You've spent untold hours preparing the props and memorising lines. People who started out as strangers have practiced together till they feel that they know each other's personalities backwards. The audience is filing in and the curtain will soon be rising."

**But Then You Look Around and Panic:**

> "The actors are huddled to the side fighting over the script. The set designer is still daubing at the backdrops and there are sound and lighting cables and equipment strewn everywhere. Random pieces of costumes lie abandoned wherever you look and altogether it's a scene of utter chaos mixed with a healthy dose of last minute confusion."

**The Question:**

> "Will we ever be ready on time?"

---

### The Miracle

**As the Minutes Tick Closer:**

> "However, as the minutes tick closer, everything miraculously comes together. That which looked like a randomised accumulation of unpreparedness soon reveals itself as the building blocks of artistic accomplishment."

**The Inexplicable Success:**

> "You don't understand how, yet somehow it all comes good on the night."

---

## The Application

### Storm Before the Calm

**What We See Now:**

> "When we look around now and see only tragedy, petty infighting and bitterness, we're seeing the storm before the calm."

**What Will Happen:**

> "That depressing vista of the present will soon surrender to the promised perfection that awaits."

**Our Limited Perspective:**

> "It might look to us like an unsurmountable mess of loss and decay, but when the fog of confusion is allowed to clear, we'll look back and appreciate the order that lay concealed beneath the chaos."

---

### The Pieces Coming Together

**Current State:**

> "Until you put the pieces of history together and step back to see the final product, you've got nothing but a mess."

**When Moshiach Comes:**

> "But once Moshiach comes, and the seemingly random shards of apparent anarchy fall into place, we'll be able to see that all the chaos and confusion was really the building blocks of perfection."

**The Ultimate Understanding:**

> "Soon all the pain and suffering will be accounted for and we'll understand that life until now was just the precursor to building a permanent stage on which Hashem can perform."

---

## For Shluchim: Using This Teaching

### When to Use

**Perfect For:**
- Addressing doubts about Moshiach
- Times of crisis or tragedy
- When community feels discouraged
- Pre-Passover classes (redemption theme)
- Farbrengens on Geulah
- Email/bulletin inspiration

**Audience:**
- Skeptics who don't see redemption coming
- Those experiencing personal tragedy
- Community facing collective challenges
- Anyone questioning where the world is headed

---

### Teaching Structure

**Opening (2-3 minutes):**
- Voice the contradiction
- "You tell me Moshiach is coming, but I see a mess"
- Acknowledge real suffering and chaos
- Build tension: Who should I believe?

**The Analogy (3-5 minutes):**
- Theater production 10 minutes before curtain
- Describe the backstage chaos vividly
- The panic: "Will we ever be ready?"
- The miracle: Somehow it all comes together
- The building blocks were there all along

**The Application (3-5 minutes):**
- Current chaos = backstage before showtime
- Storm before the calm
- Pieces will come together
- Order concealed beneath chaos
- All will be accounted for and understood

**The Close (1-2 minutes):**
- "Building blocks of perfection"
- "Permanent stage on which Hashem can perform"
- Message of hope and faith
- Rebbe assured us

---

### Adaptation Options

**Can Add:**
- Specific current events (with sensitivity)
- Personal stories of apparent chaos becoming clear
- Additional analogies (puzzle pieces, construction site, etc.)
- Torah sources on pre-Messianic birth pangs (chevlei Moshiach)
- Rebbe's teachings on signs of redemption

**Can Shorten:**
- Focus only on analogy and application
- Brief version: "Backstage chaos before curtain rises"
- One-liner: "We're 10 minutes till showtime"

**Can Expand:**
- Deeper exploration of apparent contradictions
- More examples of current world chaos
- Historical parallels (Egypt before redemption, etc.)
- Kabbalistic concepts (breaking vessels before repair)
- Personal action items (what to do while waiting)

---

### Tone Considerations

**Important:**
- Acknowledge real pain (don't minimize suffering)
- Validate the confusion and contradiction
- Balance realism with hope
- Avoid sounding dismissive of tragedies
- Use "we" not "you" (speaker shares the struggle)

**Rabbi Greenbaum's Approach:**
- Lists real tragedies specifically
- Doesn't sugar-coat the mess
- Builds genuine tension before resolution
- Analogy is relatable and concrete
- Ends with certainty, not just hope

---

### Discussion Questions

**Understanding the Contradiction:**
1. What makes the promise of redemption feel unbelievable today?
2. What specific "mess" do you see in your life or the world?
3. How does this contradiction affect your faith?

**The Analogy:**
1. Have you ever experienced last-minute chaos becoming success?
2. What are other examples of necessary chaos before creation?
3. How does the theater analogy help or not help you?

**Moving Forward:**
1. What changes if we're "10 minutes till showtime"?
2. How do we live with the tension while waiting?
3. What are the "building blocks" you can identify now?

## Letter #1: To Congressman (1996)

### Opening: Congratulations

> "Honorable Congressman Weldon,
>
> I would like to take this opportunity to congratulate you on your election for second term. May the A-lmighty grant you much success in your worthy endeavors."

**Strategy:**
- Start with congratulations (relationship building)
- Religious blessing (appropriate tone)
- Establishes goodwill

---

### The Invitation

> "It is an honor to invite you once again to our Grand Menorah Lighting Ceremony at the Melbourne Square Mall following a Live Inter-Continental Satellite link up of simultaneous Chanukah Celebrations around the globe to be viewed on a large screen set up in the mall, on Sunday Dec. 8, 1996 at 5:00 PM."

**Key Elements:**
- "Once again" (continuing relationship)
- "Grand" Menorah Lighting
- Location: Melbourne Square Mall
- Special feature: **Live Inter-Continental Satellite link-up**
- Global celebrations shown on large screen
- Specific date and time

**Why This Works:**
- Technology hook (1996 - satellite was impressive!)
- Global connection
- Not just local event
- Visual spectacle

---

### National Context

> "Nationally, Chabad is sponsoring over 275 Chanukah Celebrations from coast to coast, highlighted by President Clinton's participation from the Oval Office in the kindling of the National Freedom Menorah in Lafayette Park, adjacent to the White House."

**Power Statement:**
- 275 celebrations nationwide
- **President Clinton participating**
- From Oval Office
- National Freedom Menorah
- Lafayette Park (iconic location)

**Message:** This is significant enough for the President

---

### The Religious Freedom Frame

> "As the Chanukah holiday is a celebration of religious freedom and expression, it would be appropriate for the elected representatives of our great country to participate in its celebration. It would be an honor if you would join us and give your greetings at this joyous event."

**Key Themes:**
- Religious freedom (American value)
- Freedom of expression (First Amendment)
- Appropriate for elected officials
- "Our great country" (patriotic)
- Simple ask: "give your greetings"

---

### Proclamation Request

> "I would like to request a proclamation from your office once again attesting to the significance of the celebration and universal message of the Chanukah lights. I have attached a proposed text for the Proclamation. If you have any questions please feel free to call me at [REDACTED-PHONE]."

**Elements:**
- "Once again" (done before)
- "Attesting to significance"
- "Universal message"
- Text attached (makes it easy!)
- Direct phone contact

---

### Personal Touch

> "P.S. Regards from Dr. Jeffrey Greenspoon."

**Strategy:**
- Personal connection
- Mutual acquaintance
- Relationship building
- Humanizes the letter

---

## Letter #2: To Mayor (2004)

### Opening: Congratulations on New Position

> "December 6, 2004
>
> The Honorable Harry Goode - Mayor
> Melbourne City Hall
> 900 Strawbridge Ave.
> Melbourne, FL 32901
>
> Dear Mayor Goode,
>
> Firstly let me take this opportunity, on behalf of the entire Jewish community of the Space Coast, to congratulate you on your new position as Mayor of the City of Melbourne."

**Strategy:**
- Recent election (timely)
- On behalf of entire Jewish community (not just Chabad)
- Establishes he represents constituents

---

### The Invitation

> "It is an honor to invite you to our Grand Menorah Lighting Ceremony at the Melbourne Square Mall on Wednesday, Dec. 8, 2004 at 5:45 PM."

**Specific Details:**
- Same venue (established event)
- Exact day, date, time
- Simple, clear

---

### National Context (Updated)

> "Nationally, Chabad is sponsoring over 565 Chanukah Celebrations from coast to coast, highlighted by President Bush's participation from the Oval Office in the kindling of the National Freedom Menorah in Lafayette Park, adjacent to the White House."

**Growth Shown:**
- 275 events (1996) → **565 events (2004)**
- Now President Bush (updated administration)
- Same iconic location
- Shows organizational growth and legitimacy

---

### Precedent Established

> "Annually, we have had the honor of the participation of the Mayor of the City of Melbourne at our Chanukah event. A short greeting and reading of a proclamation by the City of Melbourne was always an honor for us. I have taken the liberty to attach a proposed proclamation using the same text as..."

**Key Points:**
- **Annual tradition** (not new request)
- Previous mayors participated
- Sets expectation
- "Short greeting" (not burdensome)
- Same proclamation text (consistency)

## Analysis: What Makes These Effective

### Letter #1 (Congressman) Strengths

**1. Relationship Building**
- Congratulations on re-election
- "Once again" (continuing participation)
- Personal P.S.
- Religious blessing

**2. Impressive Scale**
- Satellite link-up (cutting edge in 1996)
- Global celebrations
- 275 national events
- President participating

**3. Political Safety**
- Religious freedom frame
- Appropriate for officials
- Universal message
- Patriotic language

---

### Letter #2 (Mayor) Strengths

**1. Timely**
- New mayor
- Congratulations on new position
- On behalf of community

**2. Precedent**
- Annual tradition
- Previous mayors participated
- Expected continuation
- "Short greeting" (low commitment)

**3. Growth**
- 275 → 565 events nationally
- Shows momentum
- Organizational legitimacy

**4. Makes It Easy**
- Same text as before
- Proposed proclamation attached
- Clear expectations

---

## Key Differences Between Letters

### Congressman Letter
- More elaborate event description
- Technology focus (satellite)
- Longer, more detailed
- Request is primary

### Mayor Letter
- Emphasizes tradition/precedent
- Shorter, more direct
- Assumes continuation
- Precedent is primary argument

## Template for Your Use

### Basic Structure

```
[Date]

[Title] [Full Name]
[Address]

Dear [Title] [Last Name],

[CONGRATULATIONS if applicable - election, re-election, new position]

It is an honor to invite you [once again/for the first time] to our [event name] at [location] on [date] at [time].

[Optional: Special features - satellite, entertainment, etc.]

Nationally, Chabad is sponsoring over [number] Chanukah celebrations from coast to coast, highlighted by [President/notable figure]'s participation [location detail].

As Chanukah is a celebration of religious freedom and expression, it would be appropriate for elected representatives of our great country to participate. It would be an honor if you would join us and give your greetings.

[IF CONTINUING]: Annually, we have had the honor of [previous official]'s participation.

I respectfully request a proclamation from [office] attesting to the significance of the celebration and universal message of Chanukah. I have attached a proposed text. Please contact me at [phone] with questions.

[IF PERSONAL CONNECTION]: P.S. Regards from [mutual acquaintance].

Sincerely yours,
Rabbi [Name]
[Title]
[Organization]
[Contact Info]
```

## When to Send Each Type

### Use Congressman Letter Style When:
- First-time invitation to official
- Want to emphasize event scale/importance
- Have special features to highlight
- Need to establish significance
- Requesting new participation

### Use Mayor Letter Style When:
- Continuing relationship
- Previous participation established
- Emphasizing tradition/precedent
- Local official (not federal)
- Want to keep it brief

## Additional Tips

### Make It Easy for "Yes"

**Include:**
1. Proposed proclamation text
2. Event flyer/details
3. Previous year photos (if continuing)
4. Media coverage samples
5. Parking/logistics

### Follow-Up Strategy

**Timeline:**
- Send 6-8 weeks before
- Follow up call 1 week after
- Confirm 2 weeks before
- Final reminder 3 days before

### After Event

**Send:**
- Thank you letter
- Photos with official
- Media coverage clips
- Invitation to next year

**Bottom Line:**
These letters work because they frame Chanukah as celebration of religious freedom (American value), provide impressive national context (President participating, hundreds of events), and make the ask easy (proposed text attached, "short greeting"). The Congressman letter emphasizes scale and technology; the Mayor letter emphasizes precedent and tradition. Both use congratulations to build goodwill and include specific event details.

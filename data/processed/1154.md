# Hebrew Haggadah Class - Holiness of the Seder Night

**Type**: Educational/Teaching Material
**Topic**: In-depth Haggadah class with mystical and Chassidic insights
**Language**: English with Hebrew/Aramaic terms transliterated
**Source Reference**: http://www.shluchimexchange.com/en/search.html?match=and&sort_by=d&lists=all&words=Haggadah#list_top

## Overview

This document is a comprehensive Haggadah shiur (class) titled "קדושת הלילה" (Holiness of the Night), exploring the unique spiritual dimensions of the Seder night through Chassidic and mystical teachings. The class presents numerous insights from prominent Chassidic masters including the Tiferes Shlomo, Reb Boruch of Mezhibiz, Chasam Sofer, the Lubavitcher Rebbe, and many others, focusing on how the Seder night transcends ordinary spiritual experience.

## Key Themes and Teachings

### The Unique Nature of the Seder

**No "Peshat" (Simple Understanding)**: The Tiferes Shlomo teaches that the three letters of סדר (Seder) stand for סוד, דרוש, רמז (mystical, homiletical, allegorical) - notably missing the letter פ for פשט (simple explanation). Even at a basic level, comprehending the Seder requires engaging with deeper dimensions of understanding. One only fulfills the mitzvah of sippur yetziat Mitzrayim (telling the Exodus story) by incorporating these deeper levels.

**Holiness So Intense It Affects Nature**: The Alte Rebbe writes that the revelation at the time of the Exodus was so powerful that it impacted even inanimate matter. The natural property of dough is to rise, but due to Hashem's revelation, the dough simply couldn't rise - nature itself was overridden.

**Hashem Passes Over the Need for Teshuva**: The Midrash states that Hashem asks us to open our hearts to teshuva with an opening "no larger than the tip of a needle," and He will expand it to allow carriages to enter. However, Reb Boruch of Mezhibiz explains that on the night of yetziat Mitzrayim, the Torah says "פתח ה' על הפתח" - Hashem passed over the entrances, meaning Hashem skipped over the requirement for the Jewish people to even take that small initial step of teshuva.

### Spiritual Accessibility of the Night

**All Heavenly Doors Are Open**: When we open the front door to welcome Eliyahu HaNavi, the Midrash teaches (as explained by the Lubavitcher Rebbe) that what Hashem commands us to do, He does as well. If we open our doors, Hashem has opened all the doors in Heaven. Irrespective of one's spiritual level, at the Seder we can reach great spiritual heights.

**Angels Come to Listen**: The Zohar states that Hashem sends angels to Jewish homes to hear how we conduct the Seder. The section "Ha Lachma Anya" is recited in Aramaic so the angels (who only understand Hebrew) won't become jealous of how we praise Hashem. The rest of the Seder is protected by the mitzvah of sippur itself (Dvar Avrohom).

**The Seder Table in Heaven**: The Tiferes Shlomo explains the unusual language of the Mishnah: "מוזגין לו כוס ראשון" (a cup of wine is poured for him) - stated as fact rather than as a command. This is because every mitzvah impacts the spiritual worlds, but at the Seder, the actual Seder table is taken to Heaven, and when we pour a cup here, a cup is also poured there. The Mishnah describes what's happening in Heaven.

### Elevation and Transformation

**Wearing a Kittel**: One should imagine at the Seder that they have passed away and Hashem is allowing them back to earth for one night to conduct the Seder. Can you imagine what type of Seder such a person would conduct? This thought tremendously enhances the Seder (Arugos Habosem quoting Rebbe Reb Shmelke).

**Greatest Joy for the Shechina**: The Siddur of the Rashash states that when one comes home from shul, one should be full of simcha (joy), because there is no greater moment of joy for the Shechina than the night of Pesach.

**Immediate Sanctification**: Usually one must first remove impurities (symbolized by washing) before sanctifying oneself (Kiddush), but the Seder order is קדש ורחץ (Kiddush then washing). On Pesach night we're on such a high level that we can immediately begin with sanctification (Avnei Nezer).

**High Spiritual Level**: During the year, most people don't wash before dipping food in liquid because such conduct was for individuals on very high spiritual levels, and imitating them might seem haughty. However, on Seder night we're transported to such a high spiritual place that every single person is genuinely on that level where washing before dipping is not haughtiness (Klausenberger Rebbe).

### Understanding Key Haggadah Sections

**Washing Hands at Karpas**: Multiple reasons are given: (1) Just as a Kohen washes before service, we wash before this important mitzvah (Shulchan Aruch/Bes Avrohom); (2) The reading of the Haggadah is like prayer where we speak of Hashem's honor, so we wash as before davening (Darkei Moshe).

**The Students Reaching Shacharis**: When the students told their teachers they reached "the time of reciting Shema of the morning," this doesn't refer to time zones but spiritual level. The Shema of morning is higher than of night. Through sippur yetziat Mitzrayim, they reached the high spiritual level of morning Shema even though it was still nighttime (Previous Lubavitcher Rebbe).

**Seizing the Moment - The Tiferes Shlomo's Story**: A chassid who hadn't been blessed with children stood aside as the Rebbe conducted the Seder. Before "Mah Nishtana," most Haggados state "כאן הבן שואל" (at this point the child asks). When the Rebbe reached this, he called the chassid and said, "at this point ask for a child." A year later he was blessed with a child. There's a tradition that simply repeating this story at the Seder brings answered prayers. The phrase can be understood: "at this point a Jew can ask Hashem to be his father" (Reb Elimelech of Lizhensk).

**Kol Hamarbeh L'saper (The More One Tells)**: The Gemara in Brachos prohibits excessive praise of Hashem as it detracts from His greatness. However, this only applies to miracles that happened to others. If Hashem performed a miracle for you personally, you can and should praise as much as you want. Since without the Exodus we'd still be in Egypt, we can praise Hashem as much as we want.

**No Bracha on the Haggadah**: The Gemara in Megillah prohibits excessive praise when linked to a bracha. Since in the Haggadah we say "kol hamarbeh" (elaborate as much as possible), this can only occur without a bracha (Maharsha). Additionally, if a person can elaborate with great enthusiasm, this indicates they truly feel they're presently leaving Egypt (Reb Shlomo Kluger).

**Even the Wise Must Tell**: One might think the purpose is only to inform those who don't know. We're taught that even a wise person is obligated because the main aspect is to offer praise to Hashem (Birchas Avrohom). Additionally, the obligation is to feel we're presently leaving Egypt, not just a history class - this only happens when one verbalizes and elaborates (various sources).

### Deep Insights on Specific Elements

**"L'amor" (To Say to Others)**: The verse says "והגדת לבנך ביום ההוא לאמור" - the word לאמור seems unnecessary. The Chasam Sofer explains: One has fulfilled the mitzvah when they inspire their children to such an extent that when they become adults, they too will want to share the story with their children. That's why the Torah says לאמור (to tell to others).

**The Farmer's Narrative**: Why don't we quote the section directly about the Exodus from the Torah where Moshe gives a firsthand account? Instead we quote a farmer bringing first fruits to Jerusalem, briefly retelling the Exodus. The reason: We need to instill in the next generation that they're a link in the chain connected to Mount Sinai. Jewish history must run through them. At the Seder we focus on a text showing we're links back to Sinai (The Rav).

**"This Matzah That We Eat, Why?"**: Grammatically it should say "Why do we eat matzah?" But it begins with a statement - "We eat matzah" - no questions asked. Only after fulfilling the mitzvah, if possible, do we want to know the reason (various sources).

**Who Knows One (Echad Mi Yodea)**: Generally we don't ask questions; we accept and fulfill duties without debate. Why conclude with questions? After the Haggadah, we're confident we can answer all questions posed. In psychology, word association tests reveal what's on your mind. If each number relates to Hashem, His Torah, and mitzvos, the Haggadah has had an impact (Klausenberger Rebbe). We restart from "one" each time because each new number gives deeper understanding of previous facts, so we start afresh as each takes on new meaning.

**Chad Gadya - The One Kid**: The "kid" refers to Bnei Yisroel. "Chad gadya, chad gadya" - we have two aspects: unwavering belief in Hashem's Oneness and the persecution we've encountered. "Abba" refers to Hashem. "Dizvin" means purchase. "Trei zuzei" - the word "zuz" also means movement. We experience two major journeys: being sold into exile (remaining firm in faith in Oneness) and returning to Eretz Yisroel with Moshiach. The cat, dog, stick, etc. all refer to various nations punishing the Jews, each more horrific than the other, until Hashem comes and saves us from all persecuting nations. We conclude with Chad Gadya because it encapsulates our history of remaining one with Hashem and our constant belief in Moshiach's coming (Hakriah V'Hakedusha). We say this when Moshiach hasn't yet come - to lift spirits, expressing belief that Hashem will redeem us and punish all who sought our harm. The bricks in Auschwitz gas chambers will be judged for not collapsing on their own (Klausenberger Rebbe).

**Awakening Miracles**: When a person retells the miracles Hashem did for Bnei Yisroel in the past, this awakens within Hashem the will to perform miracles for the person and Jewish People in the present (Noam Elimelech).

**Shomer (Blessed is Hashem Who Keeps)**: How is this reason to praise? Everyone must keep their word. The word שומר can mean "waiting." Yoseph's dreams foretold his rise to power. The Torah says Yaakov "שמר את הדבר" (kept waiting for the event). Similarly, the Haggadah praises Hashem for anxiously waiting for the moment He could redeem the Jews (various sources).

**The Egg at the Seder**: We mark Avraham's passing but not Yitzchok's or Yaakov's. Why? Avraham brought people to recognize the Creator. How? When people ate, he'd tell them "Don't thank me, thank the Creator." If through food Avraham could transform a non-Jew into a Jew, as we begin our meal, we eat an egg to remind us what Avraham accomplished through food - we too can reach great heights through eating (various sources).

**Why the Seder at Night?**: The Torah says "בעצם היום הזה" - they left during the day. Why conduct the Seder at night? We're obligated to feel as if we're now leaving Egypt. The night before leaving, during the plague of the firstborn, Hashem spared the Jews. There was great celebration and praise. We emulate that behavior, hoping to awaken Hashem's mercy to redeem us as He did from Egypt (Medrash B'Chidush).

**The Four Sons - All Equal**: Each son is introduced with "echad" (one), not "first son, second son." This impresses upon us that all four are equal, each needs an appropriate answer, and the reward is the same whether answering the wise son or wicked son (Reb Shlomo Kluger).

**Answer to the Wise Son - Four Laws**: The wise son wants to understand mitzvos to enhance his service. We tell him four laws of korban Pesach representing how to become a true talmid chacham: (1) "משכו וקחו" - draw near and take - study Torah with diligence, it doesn't come on its own; (2) "לא תותירו ממנו" - leave nothing uneaten - study all parts of Torah so you won't need other knowledge; (3) "צלי אש" - completely roasted - superficial study leads to ignorance and foolish questions; (4) "עצם לא תשברו" - don't break bones - when learning improperly and confronting difficulties, people wrongly conclude the topic is "broken" (makes no sense), not realizing it's their ignorance causing the problem (Hakriah V'Hakedusha).

**B'chol Dor Vador (In Every Generation)**: A person must regard himself as if he personally left Egypt. The Netziv explains: The Gemara says every Jew must recognize "בשבילי נברא העולם" - the world was created for him. He must fulfill mitzvos as if he's the only Jew in the world. Likewise, a Jew must believe all the miracles at the Exodus happened for his sake. This gives much deeper appreciation and connection to yetziat Mitzrayim.

**"L'shanah Haba'ah B'Yerushalayim"**: Why say this at end of Yom Kippur and at the Seder? Tehillim says "אם לא אעלה את ירושלים על ראש שמחתי" - there's an obligation to remember Yerushalayim at the height of our simcha. No two days have greater joy than Yom Kippur (when sins are forgiven) and Pesach (marking our freedom). Therefore we mention Yerushalayim (Eliyahu Ki Tov).

**Maggid (Reciting the Haggadah)**: The Torah says "ועשית פסח לה'" - make the korban Pesach for Hashem. The word פסח sounds like פתח (door/opening). The verse can be understood: if we recite the Haggadah, this will cause Hashem to open His mouth and praise the Jewish people (Tiferes Shlomo).

**The Rambam's Long Seder**: The Rambam has a relatively short text for the Haggadah in his halachic work. After spending the entire night listening to his father expound on the night's uniqueness, Reb Avraham (the Rambam's son) asked why he spent so much time. The Rambam responded: "In my sefer halacha I wrote the minimum obligation. However, a person needs to also express what his heart is feeling" (Mishna Sachir).

**Why No Bracha?**: The Tumim writes that we don't make a bracha on giving tzedakah because it's actually a "curse" - in Messianic times there will be no poor people. If we must give tzedakah, it indicates Moshiach hasn't arrived. Similarly, Yirmiyahu says when Moshiach comes we'll no longer praise Hashem for yetziat Mitzrayim but for Moshiach's coming. The fact we're still reciting the Haggadah indicates Moshiach hasn't arrived, so it's viewed as a "curse" and no bracha is recited (Chesed L'Avraham).

## For Shluchim: Educational and Teaching Applications

**Class Structure**: This shiur demonstrates an effective format for pre-Pesach Haggadah classes - organizing by themes (holiness of the night, spiritual accessibility, key sections) rather than linear Haggadah order, allowing participants to grasp overarching concepts.

**Multiple Authorities**: The class draws from diverse sources (Chassidic masters, halachic authorities, Kabbalistic works, Midrashic literature), modeling how to present Torah Judaism's rich tapestry. This approach appeals to different learning styles and backgrounds.

**Practical Applications**: Many teachings translate directly to actionable Seder enhancements: wearing a kittel with specific mindset, understanding why we wash at karpas, knowing why we open the door for Eliyahu, appreciating why there's no bracha on the Haggadah.

**Storytelling Element**: The Tiferes Shlomo story about the childless chassid provides a concrete narrative that participants can retell at their own Seders, making abstract concepts tangible and memorable.

**Addressing Common Questions**: The class tackles questions people actually ask: Why is the Seder at night if they left during the day? Why do we say "Who Knows One"? Why no bracha on Haggadah? This question-answer format engages audiences actively.

**Depth for Different Levels**: The material ranges from accessible (opening doors symbolizes opening heavenly doors) to profound (the spiritual level transcending need for teshuva), allowing teachers to calibrate to their audience.

**Hebrew/Aramaic Integration**: The transliteration with explanation makes Hebrew/Aramaic concepts accessible to English-speaking audiences while maintaining authenticity and encouraging Hebrew learning.

**Mystical Accessibility**: The class makes Kabbalistic and Chassidic concepts (angels listening, the Seder table in Heaven, spiritual levels) understandable without requiring prior mystical knowledge, democratizing deep teachings.

**Community Seder Preparation**: For Shluchim running community Seders, this material can be distributed in advance as a study guide, discussed at pre-Seder gatherings, or referenced during the Seder itself to enrich the experience for participants of varying knowledge levels.

**Women's Inclusion**: While not explicitly mentioned, the teachings about the holiness accessible to all (regardless of spiritual level) and the emphasis on feeling personal connection to the Exodus resonate particularly well in mixed or women's settings.

**Source Reference Provided**: The URL to Shluchim Exchange at the beginning allows fellow Shluchim to access additional materials, fostering resource-sharing and collaborative learning.

**Encouraging Elaboration**: The class models the "kol hamarbeh" principle by providing extensive commentary, showing that elaborating on the Exodus is both permitted and praiseworthy, encouraging participants to add their own insights.

**Practical for Table Leaders**: At community Seders with table leaders, this material can be distributed beforehand so leaders can select 2-3 teachings to share at their tables, ensuring every table has substantive Torah discussion without requiring leaders to be scholars.

**Year-Round Relevance**: Teachings about angels listening, Heavenly doors opening, and miraculous awakening apply beyond Pesach to general spiritual growth, making this a resource for year-round inspiration.

**Addressing Cynicism**: The teaching that even the wise must tell the story (because it's about praise, not information) addresses modern skepticism about ritual repetition, providing meaningful rationale for annual observance.

## Bottom Line

This comprehensive Haggadah shiur titled "קדושת הלילה" (Holiness of the Night) presents an extensive collection of Chassidic and mystical insights from numerous Torah authorities (Tiferes Shlomo, Reb Boruch of Mezhibiz, Chasam Sofer, Lubavitcher Rebbe, Klausenberger Rebbe, and many others), focusing on the extraordinary spiritual accessibility and transformative power of the Seder night. The class is organized thematically rather than linearly, covering: (1) the unique nature of the Seder as inherently transcending simple understanding, with the Seder itself (סדר) being composed only of סוד דרוש רמז (mystical, homiletical, allegorical) without פשט (simple explanation); (2) the unprecedented holiness of the night where Hashem's revelation was so powerful it overrode nature (dough couldn't rise), passed over the usual requirement for humans to initiate teshuva, and opened all heavenly doors making the highest spiritual levels accessible to every Jew regardless of their ordinary spiritual state; (3) the mystical reality that angels come to listen to our Seders (hence "Ha Lachma Anya" in Aramaic so they won't understand and become jealous), and that the Seder table itself is taken to Heaven where what we do here is simultaneously enacted there; (4) practical elevation techniques including wearing a kittel while imagining one has returned from death for one night to conduct the Seder (creating maximum intentionality), understanding that washing before karpas reflects our genuinely elevated state (not haughtiness), and recognizing the Seder night as the Shechina's greatest joy; (5) deep explanations of key Haggadah sections including why there's no bracha (because "kol hamarbeh" requires unlimited elaboration which is only permitted without a bracha, and because still needing to recite Haggadah indicates Moshiach hasn't come yet which is itself a "curse"), why even the wise must tell the story (it's about praise not information, and about feeling present experience not history), why "לאמור" teaches we must inspire children to inspire their children (perpetuating the chain), why we use the farmer's brief narrative rather than Moshe's detailed account (to emphasize being a link in the chain to Sinai), the four laws of korban Pesach as principles for becoming a true Torah scholar (draw near and take, leave nothing uneaten, completely roasted, don't break bones representing diligent study of all Torah with depth and without breaking under difficulty), and why Chad Gadya encapsulates Jewish history (the "one kid" being the Jewish people with "two zuzim" representing our two journeys - into exile with unwavering faith and returning with Moshiach - while all the attacking animals represent successive oppressing nations until Hashem saves us); (6) powerful practical stories including the Tiferes Shlomo telling a childless chassid to ask for a child at the moment of "כאן הבן שואל" (at this point the child asks) with the tradition that simply retelling this story at the Seder brings answered prayers, and Reb Avraham asking his father the Rambam why he elaborated so much when his halachic text is brief, with the Rambam responding that halacha is the minimum but a person must express what his heart feels; and (7) the fundamental obligation to feel we personally are leaving Egypt now (not experiencing a historical commemoration), understanding that retelling past miracles awakens Hashem to perform present miracles, that all four sons are equal deserving appropriate answers with equal reward, and that the Seder's nighttime timing emulates the night of the plague when Jews celebrated and praised Hashem sparking similar divine mercy now. The shiur provides extensive source material accessible to English-speaking audiences through transliteration and explanation, making profound Kabbalistic and Chassidic concepts understandable to those without prior mystical knowledge, while offering practical applications for personal Seders and community Seder leadership, demonstrating how to organize pre-Pesach classes thematically, distribute material to table leaders, address common questions about Seder practices, and create meaningful experiences for participants of all knowledge levels, all while modeling the "kol hamarbeh l'saper" principle that elaborating on the Exodus is not excessive but praiseworthy since these miracles happened to us personally and retelling them awakens divine mercy for present and future redemption, ultimately conveying that the Seder night is an unparalleled opportunity for spiritual transformation accessible to every Jew regardless of their year-round spiritual state because on this night Hashem Himself opens all doors in Heaven and waits anxiously for the moment to redeem us just as He redeemed our ancestors from Egypt.

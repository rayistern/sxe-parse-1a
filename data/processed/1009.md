# [Misc > For Writer > _5 Chanukah > General] Tu Bishvat vs. Chamisha Asar Bishvat - Forum Discussion

## Note: Non-Chanukah Content

**Topic:** Proper terminology for 15th of Shevat

**Source:** Shluchim Exchange forum discussion

**Type:** Linguistic/philosophical debate about Rebbe's preference

---

## The Central Question

**Should we say "Tu Bishvat" or "Chamisha Asar Bishvat"?**

---

## The Rebbe's Position

### Never Said "Tu"

**Yossi Lew, Peachtree City, GA:**
> "In all the years, I have never heard the Rebbe even once saying TU Bishvat."

**Consensus:** No one recalls the Rebbe ever using the term "Tu"

---

### Active Corrections

**Bentzion Butman, Phnom Penh, Cambodia:**
> "I personally heard from a Chosid (than a Bochur) who told the Rebbe in Yechidus that his birthday is Tu Bishvat and the Rebbe said Chamisha Asar Bishvat."

**Michoel Seligson, Brooklyn, NY:**
> "I also heard that the Rebbe asked what is 'tu bishvat' and corrected him and said Chamisha asar etc."

---

### The Dollar Story

**Sholom B. Dubov, Orlando, FL:**
> "I was standing at 'dollars' behind a Shliach. He said to the Rebbe 'TU B'Av iz mein Yom holedes'. The Rebbe gave him an additional dollar and responded: 'Dus Iz fahr di Yom holedes Af chamisha asar B'Av.'"

**Significance:**
- Extra dollar given for birthday
- But Rebbe corrected terminology
- Even in this positive context
- Shows importance of proper usage

---

## The Zionism Connection

**Bentzion Butman's Explanation:**

> "It has to do with Zionism, which the Rebbe kept far away from everything with its smell. Those who learned more than just a little know that is not only about the Nigun Hayodua, that the Rebbe was so much against and showed an extreme negative Yachas to."

**Background:**
- "Tu" associated with Zionist usage
- The Rebbe distanced from Zionist terminology
- Not just about the famous nigun
- Broader principle at play

---

## The Sefardic Seder Tradition

**Yossi Lew:**
> "By 'acheinu BNY Hasfardim' (as the Rebbe referred to them many times), there is a whole business, with different color wines, and different fruits, while they say different parts of Zohar, Tehillim, and all kinds of Yehi Rotzons."

**Context:**
- Elaborate Sephardic Tu Bishvat seder
- Different colored wines
- Special fruits
- Zohar readings
- Tehillim
- Multiple Yehi Rotzons

**Rebbe's Mention:**
> "A Tu Bishvat 'seder' was mentioned by the Rebbe over the years, including a mention at the Farbrengen of Tu Bishvat Mem Daled (second Sicha)."

---

## The Interesting Exception

### Written Letter to R. Zalman Jaffe

**Yossi Lew:**
> "There is, however, a letter the Rebbe sent to my grandfather, R. Zalman Jaffe OH, in which the Rebbe wrote TU - just like that - Bishvat!"

**Context:**
> "And yet, as a Dovot Shebidfus, the Rebbe sent a letter to my grandfather (in Mem Daled, I believe), in which in the Pnim of the letter it is written TU (T and a U) Bishvat!"

**Explanation:**
- **Dovot Shebidfus** - Speaking in the language of others
- Letter to English-speaking chassid
- Using common English terminology
- Accommodating recipient's language

---

### Another Written Reference

**Avrohom Kievman, Liverpool, England:**
> "There is once where the Rebbe used the expression 'Tu' b'Shevat: http://www.chabad.org/library/article_cdo/aid/289226/jewish/No-Business-Worries-Expanding-Activities.htm"

**Pattern:**
- Written English contexts allow "Tu"
- Verbal Hebrew usage: always "Chamisha Asar"
- Language accommodation principle

---

## What's Wrong with "Tu"?

**Levi Goldstein, Des Moines, IA:**
> "What's takeh wrong with 'tu'?"

**Answer (Implicit):**
1. Zionist associations
2. Rebbe never used it verbally
3. Not traditional Jewish terminology
4. Prefer Hebrew numerical expression
5. "Chamisha Asar" = "Fifteen" (proper Hebrew)

---

## Practical Implications for Shluchim

### When Speaking Hebrew/Yiddish
- Always say "Chamisha Asar Bishvat"
- Never "Tu Bishvat"
- Follow Rebbe's consistent practice

### When Writing in English
- "Tu Bishvat" may be acceptable
- Dovot shebidfus principle
- Rebbe himself used it in English letters
- But "15th of Shevat" also good option

### General Principle
- Be conscious of terminology
- Understand the reasoning
- Not just arbitrary preference
- Deeper ideological considerations

**Bottom Line:**

Forum discussion reveals the Rebbe consistently avoided saying "Tu Bishvat" and actively corrected others who used this terminology, preferring "Chamisha Asar Bishvat" (Hebrew for "15th of Shevat"). Multiple eyewitness accounts: Chosid told Rebbe in yechidus his birthday was "Tu Bishvat" and Rebbe corrected him to "Chamisha Asar"; at dollars line Shliach said "Tu B'Av iz mein Yom holedes" and Rebbe gave extra dollar for birthday but corrected: "Dus iz fahr di Yom holedes af Chamisha Asar B'Av." Reason: "Tu" terminology associated with Zionism, which "the Rebbe kept far away from everything with its smell"—part of broader pattern of avoiding Zionist cultural/linguistic markers. Interesting exception: Rebbe wrote "TU Bishvat" in English letter to R. Zalman Jaffe (Yossi Lew's grandfather), explained as "dovot shebidfus" (speaking in language of recipient)—accommodating English-speaking chassid's common usage. Practical application for Shluchim: when speaking Hebrew/Yiddish always say "Chamisha Asar Bishvat," when writing in English "Tu Bishvat" may be acceptable as dovot shebidfus or use "15th of Shevat." Broader principle: language reflects ideology, terminology matters, conscious word choices in every interaction.

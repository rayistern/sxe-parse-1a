# Part I – Passover Night

## I. The Obligations for the Seder Night

### 1) Mitzvot from the Torah

**Four Biblical Commandments:**

1. **Pesach** (Passover offering)
2. **Matzah** (Unleavened bread)
3. **Maror** (Bitter herbs)
4. **Recounting the story of the Exodus**

---

### 2) Mitzvot from our Sages

**Four Rabbinic Ordinances:**

1. **Four cups of wine**
2. **Recline** (while eating and drinking)
3. **Charoset** (Apple-nut mixture)
4. **Hallel** (Psalms of praise)

---

## II. Pesach, Matzah and Maror

### 1) Exodus 12:1-20 - The Complete Text

**Verses 1-2 - The First Month:**

> 1 G-d spoke to Moshe and Aharon in the land of Egypt saying: 2 This month shall be the head of the month for you. It shall be the first of the months of the year.

---

**Verses 3-7 - Selecting and Slaughtering the Lamb:**

> 3 Speak to the entire community of Israel, saying: On the tenth of this month, every man must take a lamb for each extended family, a lamb for each household. 4 If the household is too small for a lamb, then he and a close neighbor can obtain a [lamb together], according to the number of people involved. Each person should be counted for the lamb according to his ability to eat.

> 5 You must have a flawless male lamb, in its first year. You may take either sheep or goats. 6 Hold it in safekeeping until the 14th day of this month.

> The entire community of Israel shall then slaughter [their sacrifices] in the afternoon. 7 They shall take some of its blood and put it on the two doorposts and on the lintel of the houses in which they will be eating it.

---

**Verses 8-11 - How to Eat the Pesach Offering:**

> 8 On that night, they shall eat the meat. **They should eat it roasted over fire, together with matzos and bitter herbs.**

> 9 Do not eat it undercooked or boiled in water, only roasted over the fire, its head with its legs and with its innards.

> 10 Do not leave any of it over until morning. Any part of it that is left over until morning shall be burned in fire.

> 11 This is how you shall eat it: Your waist should be belted, your shoes on your feet, and your staff in your hand. You should eat it quickly. It is a Passover (Pesach) offering to G-d.

---

**Verses 12-14 - The Purpose and Memorial:**

> 12 I will pass through the land of Egypt on this night, and I will smite every first-born in the land of Egypt, both man and beast, and upon all the gods of Egypt I will perform acts of judgment. I [alone] am G-d.

> 13 The blood will be for you as a sign upon the houses where you are. I will see the blood and pass over you (Pasach). There will not be any deadly plague among you when I strike Egypt.

> 14 This day shall be for you as a memorial, and you shall celebrate it as a festival for G-d. For all generations you shall celebrate it as an eternal statute.

---

**Verses 15-20 - The Commandment of Matzah:**

> 15 Eat matzahs for seven days. But on the preceding day, you must have your homes cleared of all leaven. For whoever eats leaven from the first day until the seventh day will have his soul cut off from Israel.

> 16 The first day shall be a sacred holiday, and the seventh day shall [also] be a sacred holiday. No work may be done on these [days]. The only [work] that you may do is that which is needed so that everyone will be able to eat.

> 17 You shall guard the matzos, for on this very day I have brought your legions out of Egypt. You shall observe this day throughout your generations as an eternal statute. 18 In the first month, on the 14th day of the month in the evening, you shall eat matzahs, until the 21st day of the month in the evening. 19 For seven days, leaven shall not be found in your homes. If any person eats a leavening substance, his soul will be cut off from the community of Israel, whether he is a convert or a native of the land. 20 You shall not eat any leavening substance. In all the areas where you live, you shall eat matzahs.

### 2) Pesach Offering

**The Mitzvah:**

1. Eating the meat of the Pesach sacrifice is a Mitzvah, as Exodus [12:8] states:

> "On that night, they shall eat the meat. They should eat it roasted over fire, together with matzos and bitter herbs."

**Commemoration Today:**

2. To commemorate the Pesach offering, we are required to have meat on the Seder table (the **Zro'a**, shankbone).

---

### 3) Matzah

**The Biblical Obligation:**

1. Eating the matzah on the fifteenth of Nisan is a Mitzvah from the Torah, as Exodus [12:18] states:

> "In the evening, you shall eat matzah."

**When and Where:**

- This applies **in every place**, whether in the land of Israel or in the Diaspora
- And at **every time**, even when the Temple is destroyed
- Throughout the other days of the festival, eating matzah is left to one's choice

---

**Required Amount:**

2. One is required to eat at least a **kezayit** of Matzah:
   - A measurement formally described as the size of one olive
   - Traditionally determined as **one ounce or 25.6 grams**

**Timing:**

- The consumption of the required amount should be completed in **less than four minutes**

---

### 4) Maror (Bitter Herbs)

**The Torah's Command:**

1. According to the Torah, the eating of the bitter herbs is **not a mitzvah in its own right**: It is dependent on the consumption of the Pesach sacrifice.

- It is **one positive commandment** to eat the meat of the Pesach sacrifice together with matzah and bitter herbs
- However, the Sages established it as a mitzvah, instructing us to eat the bitter herbs alone on this night even if there is no Pesach sacrifice

---

**Which Vegetables:**

2. The bitter herbs referred to by the Torah are:
   - Romaine lettuce
   - Endives
   - Horseradish
   - Date ivy
   - Wormwood

- All of these five species of vegetable are called Maror
- However, it is popular to use either **Romaine lettuce** and/or **pure horseradish (chrein)**

---

**Required Amount:**

3. One is required to eat at least a **kezayit** of Maror:
   - A measurement formally described as the size of one olive
   - Traditionally determined as **one ounce, 25.6 grams**

**Flexibility (Since Rabbinic):**

- If one finds it difficult to consume this amount, one can suffice with a smaller measure of **two-thirds of an ounce**, since this is a rabbinic precept

**Timing:**

- The consumption should be completed in **less than four minutes**
- If this should prove difficult, one can extend the time to a maximum of **6 or 7 minutes**

---

### 5) A Passage from the Haggadah

**Rabban Gamliel's Statement:**

> Rabban Gamliel used to say: Whoever did not say [did not explain the reasons for] these three things on the night of Passover, has not fulfilled his obligation [in an ideal manner]. And these are: The Pesach offering, Matzah and Maror.

---

**Explaining Pesach:**

> The Pesach sacrifice that our fathers ate during the times of the Bet Hamikdash – for what reason did they do so? Because the Omnipresent passed over our fathers' houses in Egypt, as it is said [Exodus 12:27]: "You shall say, it is a Pesach-offering to the Lord because He passed over the houses of the children of Israel in Egypt when He struck the Egyptians with a plague, and He saved our houses. And the people bowed and prostrated themselves."

---

**Explaining Matzah:**

> This matzah that we eat, is for what reason? Because the dough of our fathers did not have time to become leavened before the King of the Kings, the Holy One, blessed be He, revealed Himself to them and redeemed them. Thus it is said [Exodus 12:39]: "They baked matzah-cakes from the dough that they had brought out of Egypt, because it was not leavened; for they had been driven out of Egypt and could not delay, and they had also not prepared any [other] provisions."

---

**Explaining Maror:**

> This maror that we eat, is for what reason? Because the Egyptians embittered our fathers' lives in Egypt, as it is said [Exodus 1:14]: "They made their lives bitter with hard service, with mortar and with bricks, and with all manners of service in the field; all their service which they made them serve with rigor."

## III. Recounting the Story of the Exodus

### 1) Exodus 13:3-8

**Verse 3-4 - Remembering the Day:**

> 3 Moshe said to the people: Remember this day, when you went out of Egypt, from the house of bondage, for with a mighty hand G-d took you out of here. No leaven may be eaten. 4 Today you are going out, in the month of Aviv (spring).

---

**Verses 5-7 - The Future Land:**

> 5 When G-d will bring you into the land of the Canaanites, Hittites, Amorites, the Hivites and Yebusites, which He swore to our ancestors to give you – a land flowing with milk and honey, you shall perform this ritual (Pesach) in this month. 6 For seven days, you shall eat matzahs, the seventh day is a festival for G-d.

> 7 Matzahs shall be eaten during the seven days, and no leaven that belongs to you shall be seen in your possession. No leaven that belongs to you shall be seen throughout your borders.

---

**Verse 8 - The Obligation to Tell:**

> 8 **You shall tell your son on that day, saying, "Because of this (these mitzvos of Pesach, Matzah and Maror) G-d did this for me when I went out of Egypt."**

---

### 2) "Vehigadeta Lebincha" - You Shall Tell Your Son

**The Positive Command:**

It is a **positive command from the Torah** to relate the miracles and wonders wrought for our ancestors in Egypt on the night of the fifteenth of Nissan, as Exodus [13:3] states:

> "Remember this day, on which you left Egypt."

---

**When? On the Night of the Fifteenth:**

From where is it derived that this Mitzvah is to be fulfilled on the night of the fifteenth? The Torah teaches [Exodus 13:8]:

> "And you shall tell your son on that day, saying: 'It is because of this…'"

Implying that the mitzvah is to be fulfilled **when matzah and maror are placed before you**.

---

**Who Must Tell:**

- The mitzvah applies **even though one does not have a son**
- **Even great Sages are obligated** to tell about the Exodus from Egypt
- **Whoever elaborates** on the events which occurred is worthy of praise

---

**How to Teach:**

It is a mitzvah to inform one's son **even though they do not ask**, as Exodus [13:8] states: "You shall tell your son."

- A father should **teach his son according to the son's knowledge**

---

**Making Changes to Prompt Questions:**

He should **make changes on this night** so that children will see and will be motivated to ask:

> "Why is this night different than all other nights?"

to which he replies:

> "This and this occurred; this and this took place."

**Which changes should be made?**
- He should give them **roasted seeds and nuts**
- The **table should be taken away** before they eat
- **Matzot should be snatched** from each other
- And the like

---

**If There Are No Children:**

- If a person does not have a son, **his wife should ask him**
- If he does not have a wife, **he and a colleague should ask each other**: "Why is this night different?"
- This applies **even if they are all wise**
- A person who is alone should **ask himself**: "Why is this night different?"

---

### 3) The Haggadah

**Why the Haggadah Was Composed:**

The Haggadah was composed for this reason.

**The Meaning of "Haggadah":**

The term **Haggadah** derives from:

1. Exodus [13:8]: **"Vehigadeta Lebincha"** (you shall tell your child)
2. The expression of **hoda'ah**, thanksgiving and praise to the Holy One, blessed be He, for taking us out of Egypt

---

## IV. The Additional Mitzvot

### 1) Four Cups of Wine

**The Basis:**

1. The four cups were instituted by our Sages to correspond to the **four expressions of redemption**:

> "I will take you out… I will save you… I will redeem you… I will take you for me" (Exodus 6:6-7)

---

**The Obligation:**

2. The Sages enacted that we drink four cups at the seder **in a manner of freedom**.

**Each Cup Has Its Purpose:**

Each one of the cups is a Mitzvah in itself, and requires its own blessing:

1. **First cup**: One recites the **Kiddush** pertaining to the day
2. **Second cup**: One recites the **Haggadah**
3. **Third cup**: One recites the **grace after meals**
4. **Fourth cup**: One concludes the **Hallel**

---

**Amount Required:**

3. Each cup must contain at least a **revi'it**:
   - Approximately **3.5 fluid ounces**

**How Much to Drink:**

- Ideally, one should **drink the whole cup** each time
- If this is difficult, one should drink **a little more than one half of a revi'it** (approximately 2 ounces)

---

### 2) To Recline

**The Principle:**

1. **In each and every generation, a person must present himself as if he himself has just left the slavery of Egypt**, as Deuteronomy [6:23] states:

> "He took us out from there"

Regarding this matter, G-d commanded in the Torah [Deuteronomy 5:15]:

> "Remember that you were a slave"

i.e., **as if you yourself were a slave** and went out to freedom and were redeemed.

**Therefore:**

Our Sages ordained that when a person feasts on this night, he must **eat and drink while he is reclining** in the manner of free men.

---

**When Must One Recline:**

2. When must one recline?
   - When **eating the matzah**
   - When **drinking the four cups of wine**

- It is praiseworthy if one reclines when eating and drinking at other times, but there is **no requirement**

---

**Which Side:**

3. We recline **on the left side**.

**Why Not Other Positions:**

- Lying on one's **right side, face or back**, is not regarded as reclining
- It is not a comfortable manner of eating and cannot be regarded as a symbol of freedom and liberation

**Safety Reason:**

- In addition, reclining on one's right side can be **dangerous**, lest the food go down the windpipe rather than the esophagus

---

### 3) Charoset

**The Obligation:**

1. The Sages ordained charoset as a Mitzvah.

**Two Commemorations:**

- This is to commemorate the **cement** with which our ancestors worked as slaves to construct buildings in Egypt
- It also commemorates the **apple trees** under which the Jewish women in Egypt gave birth without pain, away from the watchful eyes of the Egyptian authorities who were intent on killing their children

---

**How to Make Charoset:**

2. The Charoset can be made of:
   - Dates
   - Dried figs
   - Raisins
   - Apples
   - Nuts
   - Cinnamon
   - Ginger
   - Pomegranates
   - And other fruits which are metaphoric to the Jewish people in the Torah

**Preparation:**

- We **crush them** and add **vinegar or wine**
- And **mix them as straw is mixed into cement**

---

### 4) Hallel

**The Obligation:**

During the Seder, it is a mitzvah to recite the **Hallel** (praise of G-d).

**Source:**

- Hallel (Psalms 113-118) was recited while the Pesach sacrifice was being offered in the Beth Hamikdash
- And while it was eaten during the Seder
- Our sages explain that the Jews recited these verses of praise as they left Egypt

## II. The Seder Plate

### 1) The Items on the Seder Plate

**Seven Items:**

1. Three Matzot
2. Zro'a (shankbone)
3. Beitzah (egg)
4. Maror (bitter herbs)
5. Charoset (apple mixture)
6. Karpas (vegetable)
7. Chazeret (additional bitter herbs)

---

### 2) The Significance of These Items

**Three Matzot:**

- There are several reasons for "three" matzot
- It is customary to choose **concave matzot**, suggesting that the matzot serve as a receptacle for the downward flow of Divine energy

---

**The Zro'a (Shankbone):**

- Commemorates the **Pesach offering** (see previous course)

---

**The Beitzah (Egg):**

- Commemorates the **Chagigah [festive] offering** which was brought together with the Pesach offering in the time of the Bet Hamikdash

**Word Play:**

- **Zro'a** literally means an **Arm**
- An **egg** in Aramaic is called **beya**, a word which may also mean to pray, and find favor, "please"
- Suggesting: **"May it please the Merciful G-d to redeem us with an uplifted arm"**

**Other Reasons for the Egg:**

1. It symbolizes the character of the Jewish people
2. To remind us of the mourning over the destruction of the Temple

---

**Maror:**

- See previous course (Part I, Section II, Subsection 4)

---

**The Charoset:**

- Is a reminder of the **clay** with which our ancestors were made to work
- An **acrid substance** is added to it as a reminder how the teeth of Israel were set on edge by the harshness of their bondage

**Composition:**

- The Charoset is made of a **mixture of fruits** to which the people of Israel have been compared
- This mixture is then softened by a **red liquid**, as a reminder of the **blood** which was the first of the ten plagues
- (See also previous course)

---

**Karpas (Vegetable):**

**Etymology:**

The letters of the word **Karpas**, when reversed, read:

- The letter **samech** [which stands for the number 60]
- **Perach** [harsh labor]

Alluding to the **sixty myriads of Jews subjected to harsh labor**

Hebrew: כַּרְפַּס = ס' פֶּרֶך

---

**Chazeret:**

- Used for the **Korech sandwich**

---

## IV. Lel Shimurim ("The Night Which Is Guarded")

### 1) Exodus 12:41-42

**The Biblical Text:**

> 41 At the end of the 430 years, all of G-d's armies left Egypt in broad daylight. 42 There was **a night of vigil for G-d**, [preparing] to bring them out of Egypt. **This night remains for the Israelites a vigil to G-d for all generations.**

---

### 2) Protection from Harm

**A night which is guarded** from that time onward against harmful spirits.

---

### 3) "Shifoch Chamascha" - Opening the Door

**When:**

After the fourth cup of wine is poured, before reciting Hallel, we open the door and say:

> "Pour out Your wrath upon the nations that do not call upon you…."

---

**Why We Open the Door:**

We open the door as a reminder that it is **"lel Shimurim"**, the night of G-d watching over us, and thus, **we are not afraid of anything**.

**The Merit:**

By merit of this **faith and trust**, Moshiach will come and G-d will pour out His wrath upon the nations that do not acknowledge Him.

**Welcoming Elijah:**

Another reason for opening the door is to welcome **Elijah**, the messenger of the Messianic redemption.

---

### Maharil's Warning - Take It Seriously

**Opening Quote from Part II:**

> "Every one must conduct himself with **awe**..."

> "**This matter should not be trivial in one's eyes!**"

> "There are some things at the Seder which may seem insignificant to man, but let him act prudently to observe them, for **there is nothing vain among them!**"

**Teaching Point:**
- Every detail has meaning
- Even "insignificant" elements matter
- Approach with awe and reverence
- Nothing is random or vain
- Follow sages' instructions carefully

**For Shluchim:**
- Set serious tone at beginning
- Balance joy with reverence
- Details matter (measurements, timing, order)
- Don't skip or minimize anything
- "Nothing vain among them"

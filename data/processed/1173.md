# Matzah Gift Letter - Chabad Lubavitch of Idaho

**Source**: Chabad Lubavitch of Idaho
**Location**: Boise, Idaho
**Date**: 10 Nissan, 5779 (April 15, 2019)
**Authors**: Rabbi Mendel & Esther Lifshitz
**Type**: Personalized matzah gift letter with contemporary relevance and existential themes

## Full Letter Text

10 Nissan, 5779
April 15, 2019

Dear Friend,

Friday night, April 19, 2019 will be the 3331st anniversary of our ancestors' emancipation! When we eat matzah on the first night of Passover, we are connecting to generations of Jews. We are intimately bound with the taste of freedom. And, we are connecting to the very essence of our being and our Creator. Surely this is an opportunity not to miss!

So, this is how we need to celebrate. On April 19 after nightfall, which is at 9:04pm in Boise, sit down and eat 1 oz of the enclosed Shmurah Matzah. That's about ½ of a large size matzah. As you're doing so, envision your great grandparents leaving Egypt 3330 years ago. Envision them eating the exact same handmade matzah (no Manischewitz yet!). Even if you are having an 'early' Seder, save this matzah for Afikoman or dessert and eat it after nightfall, so you can connect with the spirit of Passover.

I have a question for you, though. How can I repeat the same rituals each year and still feel like it's important and inspiring?

The Chassidic Masters explain that the Hebrew word for Egypt, Mitzrayim, means limitations. We too, must transcend the limitations of our own lives; the complacency we have grown accustomed to; the voice of despair in our own head. We too, must leave Egypt every year – and every day. Freedom, it turns out, is a relative term. Yesterday's freedom might be today's enslavement.

The Exodus isn't a story for the history books. It can – and must – be our story as well. On Passover we each can break free from whatever may be hindering us. We too can leave Egypt – our Egypt. As you bite into this traditional matzah, think about why G-d has commanded us to recount and relive the Exodus each year. Think of your current personal bondage and liberation.

Please accept our heartfelt wishes and blessings to you and yours for a happy and kosher Passover. May it be truly liberating!

Sincerely,

Rabbi Mendel & Esther Lifshitz
Chabad Lubavitch of Idaho

P.S. If you need a place for the Seder, need to sell your chametz or any other Passover needs, please call our office and we will be happy to help you, or visit www.jewishidaho.com.

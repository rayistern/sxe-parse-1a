# [Misc > For Writer > _5 Chanukah > General] Electric Public Menorah: All 8 Lights vs. Progressive Lighting

## Major Halachic and Practical Debate Among Shluchim

**Question:** Do you keep all 8 menorah lights on for all 8 days, or turn on additional light each night?

**Forum Topic:** Surprisingly contentious discussion revealing deep philosophical differences

---

## The Original Question

> "I would like to see a consensus from shluchim if they keep all 8 lights on for all the nights of Chanukah or do they make a point of turning an additional light on for each night."

**Simple question, complex answers**

---

## Perspective 1: MUST Light Correct Number Each Night

### The Confusion Argument

**Real-World Problem:**
> "I missed flicking the switch one night, and someone contacted me all confused about which night it was and how many candles to light, because they saw our menorah....hopefully this helps answer your question."

**Impact:**
- People rely on public menorah
- Use it to know which night
- How many to light at home
- Wrong number = confusion

---

### The Educational Role

**Clarity Required:**
> "When you have a Menorah with four lights on the fifth night of Chanukah you are confusing people."

**Responsibility:**
- Public menorah teaches
- Shows correct practice
- Models behavior
- Educational tool

---

### Strong Reactions

**Response to Question:**
> "Personally this question is very surprising."

**Implication:** Should be obvious that correct number each night

---

### The Carter Story

**Referenced Multiple Times:**
> "What the Rebbe told Avremel when President Carter asked him to light all the candles before the last night of Chanukah!!!"

**Rebbe's Position:**
- Asked to light all 8 for Carter
- **Rebbe was upset**
- Even for President
- Even on small menorah
- Not many would see
- **Still wrong**

**Conclusion:** If Rebbe opposed even in that scenario, certainly for public menorahs

## Perspective 2: All 8 Lights Always Acceptable

### The Decoration Distinction

**Different Purpose:**
> "When you have a Menorah which from a week before Chanukah is on the outside ledge of a bank overlooking a big intersection but you cant access it every night of Chanukah, so from a week before it just has all eight light bulbs lit for Pirsumei Nisa no one is getting confused."

**Key Points:**
- Inaccessible locations
- Up before Chanukah starts
- Clearly decoration
- Pirsumei nisa (publicity)
- Context makes it clear

---

### Not Confusing in All Contexts

**Manhattan Example:**
> "Every storefront in Manhattan has it already and no one is lighting Menorah on tes kislev because of it."

**Argument:**
- All 8 lit before Chanukah
- No one confused
- Context matters
- People understand decoration

---

### Practical Limitations

**Shaliach's Challenge:**
> "If you have multiple menorahs around town with a long travel time between them, how do you ensure that every Jew that sees the different Menorahs will be up to date on the correct night?"

**Questions:**
- Multiple locations
- Long travel times
- Can't reach all nightly
- No volunteers available
- No fancy timer system

**Follow-Up:**
> "If you cannot ensure that the lights are up to date on the proper night - and therefore resort to having all eight on for the duration of Chanukah - should you not put up those menorahs?"

**Implication:** Better some pirsumei nisa than none?

---

### The Decoration Theory

**Motti Wilhelm's Suggestion:**
> "Perhaps there is an advantage to lighting all eighth bulbs of a electric menorah to show that it is only for decoration but not a fulfillment of the mitzvah."

**Logic:**
- All 8 lit = clearly not real lighting
- Shows it's symbolic
- No one thinks they're yotzei
- Avoids confusion about obligation

## The "Real Flames Only" Position

### Rabbi Levitansky's Father's Approach

**Strict Standards:**
> "My father A''H was very makpid that ALL of the public Menorahs should have a flame and should be lit with a Brocho every night."

**Implementation:**
- Bank chain agreement
- Special candle lanterns
- Fire proof tape
- Safety lid (extinguishes if tips)
- Real flames only

**Scale:**
> "As of today I believe my siblings already light sixty+ Menorahs throughout Santa Monica, in supermarkets or on street corners etc."

**Logistics Challenge:**
> "As you can imagine it is not easy to run around every night lighting 40/50 Menorahs and we needed a lot of extra bochurim and volunteers from the community"

---

### The Philosophy

**Father's Argument:**
> "His main argument was that especially now when people think you can be 'yotze' with electric we need to show them the correct way to do it."

**Concern:**
- Electric menorahs mislead people
- Think they fulfill obligation
- Need to model real flames
- Educational imperative

---

### The Michshol Concern

**Model Matzah Bakery:**
> "(BTW he felt the same way about the Model Matza bakery because he would get calls from people asking for the recipe etc. and he felt that it a 'michshol')."

**Principle:**
- Educational programs can mislead
- People might do it wrong at home
- Better not to do than to cause michshol
- Applies to electric menorahs too

---

### Continuous Expansion

> "In the spirit of 'malin bakodesh' he would find at least one new place to light a Menorah every year."

**Growth Philosophy:**
- Add locations annually
- Never reduce
- Constantly expanding
- "Malin bakodesh" (ascend in holiness)

---

## Counter-Arguments

**Response to Strict View:**

**Distinction:**
- Wrong number during Chanukah = confusing

## Practical Recommendations

### Decision Framework

**Ask Yourself:**

**1. Can you access daily?**
- Yes → Progressive lighting
- No → Consider all 8 or don't do

**2. Is it main community menorah?**
- Yes → Progressive lighting essential
- No → More flexibility

**3. Do people watch to learn?**
- Yes → Correct number matters
- No → Less critical

**4. Fire safety allows real flames?**
- Yes → Consider real flames
- No → Electric only option

**5. Have volunteers/technology?**
- Yes → Progressive possible
- No → All 8 or automation

---

### Best Practices

**For Progressive Lighting:**
- Visit daily without fail
- Have backup person
- Use automation if available
- Check it's working
- Monitor remotely if possible

**For All-8 Display:**
- Put up before Chanukah
- Clearly decorative
- Don't call it "menorah lighting"
- Maybe add sign: "Chanukah decoration"
- Consider not doing if confusing

**For Real Flames:**
- Recruit volunteers
- Safety equipment
- Insurance coverage
- Fire department notification
- Backup plan for bad weather

**Bottom Line:**
Major unresolved debate among Shluchim about electric public menorahs: progressive lighting (correct number each night) vs. all 8 lights continuously. Progressive camp argues people rely on public menorah to know which night ("someone contacted me all confused"), educational responsibility requires correct display, and Rebbe opposed lighting all 8 for President Carter even on small menorah few would see—if wrong then, certainly wrong for public displays. All-8 camp counters that inaccessible locations (bank window, multiple remote sites) with all lights before Chanukah starts creates decorative context where "no one is lighting menorah on tes kislev"—people understand it's symbolic, and practical reality of multiple locations with long travel times makes daily visits impossible without many volunteers. Technology solutions include Klar menorah automated controller (advances every 24 hours) and WiFi smart switches (control from smartphone anywhere), but power interruptions reset programming. Deeper debate: are electric menorahs legitimate at all? Rabbi Levitansky's father insisted ALL public menorahs use real flames with bracha ("especially now when people think you can be yotzei with electric we need to show correct way"), requiring 60+ nightly visits across Santa Monica with many volunteers. Modern reality: fire codes, insurance, sensitive sprinkler sensors make indoor real flames often impossible. No consensus reached. Decision framework: if daily access feasible and main community menorah where people watch to learn, progressive lighting essential; if remote/inaccessible with clearly decorative context, all-8 more defensible; if fire safety permits, real flames preferable but extremely labor-intensive. Thread tone contentious with some feeling question "out of line even to be discussed," others frustrated by this suppression of dialogue.

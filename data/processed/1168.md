# Shluchim Forum: Community Seder Menu Standards - Machine vs Shmurah Matzah

**Type**: Shluchim Exchange Forum Discussion
**Topic**: Standards for food served at community Seders in Chabad houses
**Date**: March 2015
**Source**: http://www.shluchimexchange.com/en/forum/viewtopic.php?t=478224
**Key Question**: Can/should Chabad houses serve machine matzah and lower food standards at public Seders?

## Original Question - Moshe Stiefel, Almere, Netherlands

**Context**: In past years, the shliach made a community Seder in the shul, serving food "not on our standards" - machine matzah, food not cooked in his own kitchen, gebrokts, etc. (His family ate a different menu.) This year, they've recently opened their Chabad house (the community shul closed down).

**Dilemma**: "In a way I feel that our bais Chabad is like our own house which we of course do not allow machine matzah etc., but perhaps we could allow it, as it isn't our private house...."

**Request**: "What do other shluchim do which arrange a seder in their Chabad houses? Please advise or at least tell me what you do."

**Urgency**: "Of course, this issue needs a quick answer, as we are in the midst of preparations..."

## Response #1: Yechiel S Levitansky, Sumy, Ukraine

**Position**: Maintains high standards despite cost

"Although I know many shluchim here in ukraine who serve machine matzah at the public sedarim etc. 'mekublani mebais avi' [I received from my father's house] that we serve only Shmurah and anything we serve in our CH is what we would serve in our house (at least to the kids)....so yes, it gets expensive and the menu is less colorful but people appreciate it and the Rebbe blaibt nisht kein baal chov! [the Rebbe doesn't remain indebted!]"

**Principle**: Whatever is served in the Chabad House must meet the standards they would maintain in their own home, particularly for their children.

**Acknowledgment**: Admits this approach is expensive and limits menu options ("less colorful")

**Results**: "People appreciate it" and trust in the Rebbe's blessing that the investment will be rewarded

**Contact**: chabadsumy@gmail.com

## Response #2: Yankie Denburg, Coral Springs, FL

**Position**: Received rabbinic guidance against machine matzah

"Several years ago I was told by my Rov not to serve machine matzah in my shul at all - not only not at the Seder but not during any time of pesach..."

**Follow-Up Question**: "In the Rebbe's shitah, if someone ended up eating machine matzah for the seder, are they yotzei the mitzvah, or since the matzos need to be made lshem mitzvas matzah which a machine can't do they would still need to eat shmurah matzah (with another bracha)?"

**Context**: Discussing whether machine matzah can fulfill the mitzvah d'oraysa of matzas mitzvah, given the requirement for "l'shem mitzvas matzah" (intention for the mitzvah) which a machine cannot have.

## Response #3: Levi Brashevitzky, Norfolk, VA

**Position**: Serve only what they personally eat

"We have been doing Seders and all meals during pesach for all that need at the Chabad house and serve only what we eat."

**What They Buy Ready-Made**:
- Prepared kugels (from Crown Heights community - some family members eat, some don't due to machmir standards)
- Oil
- Wine and grape juice
- Salt
- Balsamic vinegar
- Matzah (hand-made shmura only)
- Raw meat
- Raw gefilte fish
- Coffee and tea (which they don't personally use but have available for guests)

**Everything Else**: "All made in our kitchen"

**Standard**: "Anything we buy is what I would eat myself (other than what I buy for the kids which I will serve my kids)"

**Cost Acknowledgment**: "It is very pricey but we fundraise for it"

**Contact**: Cell: 757-513-3663

## Response #4: Shraga Sherman, Merion Station, PA

**Historical Story - The Rebbe Rashab**:

Tells a story (requesting confirmation/correction) that the Joint Distribution sent machine-made matzah (possibly to Rostov) for Pesach. The Yeshiva didn't use it on Pesach but used it after Pesach. Some bochurim wouldn't eat it as "chometz she'avar alav haPesach" (chometz that passed over Pesach). When the Rebbe Rashab heard this, he was upset and said "matza upon which many many (thousands and thousands?) Jews were mekayim mitzvas matzah is not Chometz."

**Interpretation**: "With that said, I understood the Rebbe Rashab's answer as a b'deved b'deved [post-facto, emergency situation] and melamed zechus [finding merit], not at all how we should conduct ourselves, etc."

**The Rebbe's Mivtza**: "I understood the Rebbe's mivtza (the first mivtza?) to be promoting Shmurah Matzah as opposed to a campaign against machine matzah."

**The Rebbe's Statement**: "The Rebbe said (see last week's hiskashrus) that it is better to give children shruya [soaked matzah - which some don't eat] than machine matzah."

## Response #5: Shlomo Uminer, Palm City, FL

**Strong Position on Standards**:

"Dont mix the two things. Being mehader in cooking is nice and you can do it but you can get food from a caterer that does not have every hidur you have."

**But on Shmurah Matzah - No Compromise**:

"Rabbi shtifel you absolutley do not have to mutche [trouble] your wife to cook everthing. shmurah matzah is diffrent its an inyan of the rebbe that the rebbe shturemt [pushed strongly] that we should give all our baalei batim and even not for the seder, there is no place whatsoever fo non shmurah matzah in our chabad house at all no matter what, other then the seder itself better they eat kartofel [potatoes]. This is part of our misssion to give and teach the world about this, its the febbes [Rebbe's] mivtza."

**On Gebrokts**: "Gebroks is not on the same level but its not somthing we should do unless you have shas hadchak [pressing circumstances] circumstances which needs a yishuv hadas [settlement of mind] and a rav."

**Includes Sicha Quote**: Pastes a sicha from Vayakhel-Pekudei 5732 (in Yiddish) about the Rebbe's push for shmurah matzah distribution.

**Translation of Sicha Excerpt**:

The Rebbe emphasized seeing that as many Jews as possible should have shmurah matzah for Pesach - give them matzah for the whole Pesach, or at minimum for the Seder (six matzos as they used to say "a gang matzos"), or at minimum a whole matzah (the middle matzah), or at minimum for the k'zayis matzah.

And this should be done "b'chayus uvemaratz hachi gadol" (with the greatest enthusiasm and energy), seeing that as many Jews as possible should have matzah, reaching another Jew and another Jew, and specifically hand matzah, and specifically round [not square], with all the hiddurim, up to the manner of "ushmartem es hamatzos" (you shall guard the matzos) according to the simple interpretation - guarding the matzos, and also the second interpretation "mamtinim umetzah" - that a Jew looks and waits for when the time will come that he can eat matzah, which is "michla d'asvasa" and "michla d'mehemnusa" (food of healing and food of faith), as stated in the Zohar.

**Contact**: rabbi@chabadmsl.com

**Closing**: "We should see the rebbe immidiatley and celebrate yud alef nissan together. Shlomo uminer"

**Clear Answer**: "Totally incorrect to say that to another Jew and is factually incorrect."

**Implication**: One cannot tell someone they're halachically required to use only shmurah matzah. Machine matzah fulfills the mitzvah d'oraysa, even if shmurah is preferred or the Chabad standard.

## For Shluchim: Navigating Community Standards vs. Halachic Requirements

### The Core Tension

This discussion reveals a fundamental tension Shluchim face: balancing **Chabad standards and the Rebbe's directives** with **halachic requirements**, **community accessibility**, and **resource constraints**.

**Three Positions Emerge**:

1. **Maintain Full Home Standards** (Levitansky, Brashevitzky): Whatever is served in the Chabad House must meet the standards you'd maintain for your own family. Expensive, limited menu, but people appreciate it and trust in the Rebbe's blessings.

2. **Shmurah Matzah Non-Negotiable, Other Hiddurim Flexible** (Uminer, Denburg's Rov): Distinguish between shmurah matzah (which is the Rebbe's mivtza and non-negotiable) versus other chumros like gebrokts or home-cooked food (which can be relaxed with rabbinic consultation in pressing circumstances).

3. **Practical Halachic Approach** (Korf, Saacks): Recognize that machine matzah is kosher and fulfills the mitzvah d'oraysa. While Chabad promotes shmurah, one cannot tell others they're halachically required to use it or haven't fulfilled the mitzvah without it.

### Practical Implementation Guidelines

**On Shmurah Matzah**:
- **Consensus**: Every respondent agrees that Chabad houses should serve only shmurah matzah, not machine matzah, at Seders and throughout Pesach
- **Rationale**: This is "the Rebbe's mivtza," part of Chabad's mission to teach about this
- **Historical Precedent**: The Rebbe instructed Ezras Achim to send only shmurah matzah to Russia
- **The Rebbe's Statement**: Better to give children shruya (which some don't eat due to gebrokts concerns) than machine matzah
- **Sicha Source**: Vayakhel-Pekudei 5732 - extensive push for distributing shmurah matzah with "greatest enthusiasm and energy"

**On Other Food Standards**:
- **Spectrum of Practice**: From everything home-cooked to using caterers/prepared foods with reliable supervision
- **Key Principle** (Uminer): "Being mehader in cooking is nice... but shmurah matzah is different"
- **Gebrokts**: Can potentially be permitted in "shas hadchak" (pressing circumstances) with rabbinic consultation, unlike machine matzah which has "no place whatsoever"
- **Budget Reality**: Maintaining high standards "gets expensive" and requires active fundraising

**On Halachic Communication**:
- **Critical Mistake**: Never tell someone machine matzah doesn't fulfill the mitzvah or is chametz - this is "factually incorrect"
- **Proper Approach**: Promote shmurah matzah as the preferred, more mehudar (enhanced) option without delegitimizing machine matzah
- **The Rebbe Rashab's Teaching**: Matzah upon which thousands of Jews fulfilled mitzvas matzah is not chometz - showing respect for others' observance even while maintaining higher personal standards
- **Nuance**: Machine matzah is "kosher PERIOD" but still "not for us and not for Jews at the Seder" - it's about what we aspire to, not what's minimally acceptable

### For New Chabad Houses

**The Transition Question**: The original questioner faced a common scenario - previously renting space (shul) where lower standards felt acceptable, now in their own Chabad house which "feels like our own house."

**Consensus Guidance**:
1. **Set Standards Early**: The Chabad house sets the tone from the beginning. What you establish initially becomes your known standard.
2. **Communicate Mission**: Frame it as educational - teaching the community about shmurah matzah, not looking down on other practices
3. **Budget Proactively**: Factor the cost of higher standards into fundraising from the start, not as an afterthought
4. **Distinguish Levels**: Shmurah matzah is non-negotiable (Rebbe's mivtza); other hiddurim can be evaluated case-by-case with rabbinic guidance

### Fundraising Messaging

**The "Pricey but Worth It" Approach** (Brashevitzky model):
- Be transparent: "It is very pricey"
- Active solution: "But we fundraise for it"
- Frame as investment in quality Jewish experience
- Trust: "The Rebbe blaibt nisht kein baal chov" - the Rebbe doesn't remain indebted

**Donor Appeal**:
- Sponsor shmurah matzah for community Seder: ~$5-10 per person
- Sponsor full elevated menu: ~$40-60 per person
- "Your support ensures everyone at our Seder experiences Passover the Chabad way - with hand-made shmurah matzah and the highest standards"

### Addressing Pushback

**"I Don't Like the Taste"** (Denburg's scenario):

- Acknowledge: "I know it's different from what many are used to"
- Educate: "This is the traditional way matzah was made for thousands of years - the same way our ancestors ate in Egypt"
- Experience: "Give it another chance - many people find they acquire a taste, and it's only for the Seder itself"
- Benefits: "It's actually more digestible than machine matzah" (anecdotal)
- Mission: "Part of what makes the Chabad Seder special is connecting to this authentic tradition"

**"Why So Strict?"**:

- Frame positively: "We're not being strict - we're being authentic"
- Historical: "This is how the Rebbe asked us to do it"
- Universal: "We want everyone to experience Pesach at the highest level, not just our own family"
- Standards: "Just like we wouldn't serve non-kosher food, we also want to ensure the matzah meets the highest kosher standards"

### The L'shem Mitzvas Matzah Question

**The Unresolved Halachic Issue**:

The discussion leaves open whether machine matzah can truly fulfill "l'shem mitzvas matzah" if the machine itself cannot have intention, even if intention is stated when starting the machine.

**Practical Shliach Response**:
- Don't engage in this debate with community members
- Simply promote: "We use hand-made shmurah matzah because it's made with personal attention and intention at every step"
- If pressed on whether machine is kosher: "Yes, it's kosher. We just choose to enhance the mitzvah with shmurah"

# [Misc > For Writer > _5 Chanukah > General] Key Excerpts from Supreme Court Menorah Ruling

## Ready-to-Use Quotes for Advocacy

**Source:** U.S. Supreme Court ruling regarding public menorah displays

**Case:** County of Allegheny v. American Civil Liberties Union (1989)

**Purpose:** Quotable excerpts for letters, presentations, and advocacy

---

## Excerpt 1: Celebrating the Season

### The Quote

> "In permitting the displays on government property of the menorah and the crèche, the city and county sought to do no more than 'celebrate the season.' Brief for Petitioner County and Allegheny in No. 87-2050, p. 27, and to acknowledge, along with many of their citizens, **the historical background and the religious as well as secular nature of the Chanukah and Christmas holidays**. This interest falls well within the tradition of government accommodation and acknowledgment of religion that has marked our history from the beginning."

---

### What This Means

**Government CAN:**
- "Celebrate the season"
- Acknowledge historical background
- Recognize BOTH religious AND secular nature of holidays
- Accommodate religion

**This Is Traditional:**
- "Marked our history from the beginning"
- Not new or radical
- American practice
- Established tradition

---

### How to Use This

**When Officials Say:** "We can't have religious displays"

**You Respond:**
> "The Supreme Court specifically held that government can acknowledge 'the religious as well as secular nature' of holidays like Chanukah. This falls within our nation's tradition of religious accommodation that has 'marked our history from the beginning.'"

---

## Excerpt 2: Government May Participate in Holiday Celebrations

### The Quote

> "It cannot be disputed that government, if it chooses, **may participate in sharing with its citizens the joy of the holiday season**, by declaring public holidays, installing or permitting festive displays, sponsoring celebrations and parades, and providing holiday vacations for its employees. **All levels of our government do precisely that**. As we said in Lynch, 'Government has long recognized – indeed it has subsidized – holidays with religious significance.' 465 U.S., at 676."

---

### What This Means

**Government MAY:**
- Participate in holiday celebrations
- Share joy with citizens
- Install or permit festive displays
- Sponsor celebrations and parades
- Declare public holidays
- Provide holiday vacations

**This Is Universal:**
- "All levels of our government do precisely that"
- Not theoretical
- Actual practice
- From federal to local

**Long Tradition:**
- Government has "long recognized" religious holidays
- Even "subsidized" them
- Not new concept

---

### How to Use This

**When Officials Say:** "Government should stay out of religious holidays"

**You Respond:**
> "The Supreme Court explicitly stated that government 'may participate in sharing with its citizens the joy of the holiday season' and noted that 'all levels of our government do precisely that.' Government participation in holiday celebrations is routine and constitutional."

---

## Excerpt 3: Recognizing Both Secular and Religious

### The Quote

> "**If government is to participate in its citizens celebration of a holiday that contains both a secular and a religious component, enforced recognition of only the secular aspect would signify the callous indifference toward religious faith** that our cases and traditions do not require; for by commemorating the holiday only as it is celebrated by nonadherents, **the government would be refusing to acknowledge the plain fact, and the historical reality, that many of its citizens celebrate its religious aspects as well**."

---

### What This Means

**Recognizing ONLY Secular = Wrong:**
- "Callous indifference toward religious faith"
- Not required by law
- Actually problematic

**The Reality:**
- Many citizens celebrate religious aspects
- This is "plain fact"
- This is "historical reality"
- Government cannot pretend otherwise

**The Balance:**
- Not imposing religion
- But not ignoring it either
- Acknowledging both dimensions
- Respecting how citizens actually celebrate

---

### How to Use This

**When Officials Say:** "We can only recognize secular aspects of holidays"

**You Respond:**
> "The Supreme Court held that 'enforced recognition of only the secular aspect would signify the callous indifference toward religious faith that our cases and traditions do not require.' Government cannot ignore 'the plain fact, and the historical reality, that many of its citizens celebrate [the] religious aspects as well.'"

**This Is Powerful Because:**
- Turns the argument around
- Excluding religion is ALSO taking a position
- "Callous indifference" is strong language
- Court says balance is required

---

## Excerpt 4: No Coercion, Just Passive Symbols

### The Quote

> "**There is no suggestion here that the government's power to coerce has been used to further the interests of Christianity and Judaism in any way. No one was compelled to observe or participate in any religious ceremony or activity**. Neither the city nor the county contributed significant amounts of tax money to serve the cause of one religious faith. **The crèche and the menorah are purely passive symbols of religious holidays**. Passerby who disagree with the message conveyed to these displays are free to ignore them, or even to turn their backs, just as they are free to do when they disagree with any other form of government speech."

---

### What This Means

**No Coercion:**
- No one compelled to observe
- No one compelled to participate
- No force used
- Purely voluntary

**No Significant Tax Money:**
- Private funding
- Minimal government expense
- Not serving one faith's cause

**Passive, Not Active:**
- "Purely passive symbols"
- Not religious services
- Not proselytization
- Just displays

**Freedom to Ignore:**
- Can turn your back
- Can ignore completely
- Like any government speech
- No one forced to look

---

### How to Use This

**When Officials Say:** "This promotes religion"

**You Respond:**
> "The Supreme Court held that menorah displays are 'purely passive symbols' with no coercion. 'No one was compelled to observe or participate in any religious ceremony or activity.' Passersby 'are free to ignore them, or even to turn their backs, just as they are free to do when they disagree with any other form of government speech.'"

**When Officials Say:** "We'd be using tax money for religion"

**You Respond:**
> "The menorah is privately funded by Chabad. The Court noted that 'neither the city nor the county contributed significant amounts of tax money to serve the cause of one religious faith.' Government's only role is permitting the display, not funding it."

---

## Excerpt 5: No Discrimination Against Jews

### The Quote

> "**Because government may celebrate Christmas as a secular holiday, it follows that government may also acknowledge Chanukah as a secular holiday**. Simply put, **it would be a form of discrimination against Jews to allow Pittsburgh to celebrate Christmas as a cultural tradition while simultaneously disallowing the city's acknowledgment of Chanukah as a contemporaneous cultural tradition**."

---

### What This Means

**Logical Consistency:**
- If Christmas, then also Chanukah
- Can't have one without other
- Must treat similarly

**Discrimination Concern:**
- Allowing one but not other = discrimination
- Against Jews specifically
- Unconstitutional

**Both Are Cultural:**
- Christmas as cultural tradition
- Chanukah as contemporaneous cultural tradition
- Parallel status
- Both acknowledged

---

### How to Use This

**When Officials Say:** "We allow a Christmas tree but not menorah"

**You Respond:**
> "The Supreme Court specifically stated that 'it would be a form of discrimination against Jews to allow Pittsburgh to celebrate Christmas as a cultural tradition while simultaneously disallowing the city's acknowledgment of Chanukah.' If you permit Christmas displays, you must permit Chanukah displays."

**This Is Your Strongest Argument When:**
- They already have Christmas decorations
- They already have tree
- They already celebrate Christmas
- But resist menorah

---

## Excerpt 6: Accommodation Is Part of Our Heritage

### The Quote

> "**Government policies of accommodation, acknowledgment, and support for religion are an accepted part of our political and cultural heritage**, and the Establishment Clause permits government some latitude in recognizing the central role of religion in society. **Any approach less sensitive to our heritage would border on latent hostility to religion**, as it would require government in all its multifaceted roles to acknowledge only the secular, to the exclusion and so to the detriment of the religious."

---

### What This Means

**Accommodation is ACCEPTED:**
- Part of political heritage
- Part of cultural heritage
- Not forbidden
- Actually traditional

**Establishment Clause PERMITS:**
- "Some latitude"
- Recognizing religion's role
- Acknowledging religion
- Supporting religion (in appropriate ways)

**Excluding Religion = Hostility:**
- "Latent hostility"
- Not neutrality
- Harmful to religious citizens
- Not required by Constitution

**Acknowledging Only Secular:**
- Would require government to exclude religious
- "To the detriment of the religious"
- Not what Constitution demands
- Goes too far

---

### How to Use This

**When Officials Say:** "Neutrality means no religion in public"

**You Respond:**
> "The Supreme Court held that 'government policies of accommodation, acknowledgment, and support for religion are an accepted part of our political and cultural heritage.' The Court warned that excluding religion would 'border on latent hostility to religion' and work 'to the detriment of the religious.' True neutrality allows religious expression, not suppresses it."

**This Reframes the Debate:**
- Accommodation is traditional
- Exclusion is hostile
- Neutrality ≠ secularism
- Balance is required

---

## Excerpt 7: Legislative Prayer Comparison

### The Quote

> "**If congress and the state legislators do not run afoul of the Establishment Clause when they begin each day with a state-sponsored prayer for divine guidance offered by a chaplain whose salary is paid at government expense, I cannot comprehend how a menorah or a crèche, displayed in the limited context of the holiday season, can be invalid**."

---

### What This Means

**Legislative Prayer Is Permitted:**
- Congress begins with prayer
- State legislatures begin with prayer
- Chaplain paid by government
- This is constitutional

**Menorah Is LESS Religious:**
- No prayer
- No chaplain
- No government salary
- Just passive display

**The Logic:**
- If active prayer is okay
- Then passive symbol is certainly okay
- Menorah is "limited context"
- Less intrusive than legislative prayer

---

### How to Use This

**When Officials Say:** "Menorah crosses the line"

**You Respond:**
> "A Supreme Court justice noted: 'If congress and the state legislators do not run afoul of the Establishment Clause when they begin each day with a state-sponsored prayer for divine guidance offered by a chaplain whose salary is paid at government expense, I cannot comprehend how a menorah...can be invalid.' If government-paid chaplains are constitutional, a passive menorah display certainly is."

**This Puts It in Perspective:**
- Many MORE religious things are permitted
- Government funds chaplains
- Government sponsors prayers
- Menorah is minor by comparison

## Mitzvot of the Evening

**Four Primary Mitzvot:**
1. Eat Matzah
2. Eat Maror
3. Tell the Story (Haggadah)
4. Drink 4 cups of Wine

---

## The Seder Plate

### What You Need:

**Zeroah:** Roasted Chicken Neck

**Beitza:** Hardboiled Egg

**Maror:** Romaine Lettuce & Horseradish

**Charoset:** A Paste made of: Apples, Pears, Nuts & Wine

**Karpas:** Raw Onion or Cooked Potato

**Additional:** 3 Matzahs, Wine & A Festive Dinner

---

## Seder Outline - 15 Steps

### 1. Kadeish
- Recite Kiddush
- Drink first cup of wine
- Reclining on left side
- **Amount:** 3.5 fl oz

### 2. Urechatz
- Wash hands in the ritual fashion
- **Without a Blessing**

### 3. Karpas
- Dip vegetable in salt water
- Recite Blessing
- **Have Maror in mind**
- Eat vegetable

### 4. Yachatz
- Break middle Matzah
- **Smaller piece:** Back in middle of 2
- **Bigger piece:** Break into 5 for Afikomen

### 5. Magid
- Tell story of the Haggadah
- Children ask the 4 Questions
- **At the end:** Drink second cup of wine with Blessing, reclining on your left side

### 6. Rochtzoh
- Wash hands in the ritual fashion
- **Recite blessing:** Al Netillas Yadayim

### 7. Motzi
- Recite Blessing Hamotzi Lechem
- **Holding all 3 Matzahs in hand**

### 8. Matzah
- Blessing Al Achilas Matzah
- **Holding just 2 top Matzahs**
- Break off piece from the 2 top Matzahs
- Eat Matzah, reclining on your left side
- **Be sure to eat at least one ounce**

### 9. Maror
- Dip Maror in Charoset
- Blessing Al Achilas Maror
- **Do not recline**

### 10. Korech
- Take 3rd Matzah
- Say Kein Assa Hillel
- Eat Matzah and Maror together
- Reclining on your left side

### 11. Shulchan Orech
- Dinner is served
- **Begin with:** Egg dipped in salt water

### 12. Tzafun
- Eat afikoman
- Reclining on your left side
- **Be sure to eat at least one ounce**
- **Do not eat or drink afterwards**

### 13. Beirach
- Birkat Hamazon – Grace After Meals
- Afterwards, drink third cup with Blessing, reclining on your left side
- The Cup of Elijah is filled
- Open door in greeting

### 14. Hallel
- Songs of praise
- Afterwards, drink fourth cup with Blessing, reclining on your left side

### 15. Nirtzah
- Confidence that G-d accepts and will assist our efforts
- **LISHANA HABAH BIYERUSHALAYIM – NEXT YEAR IN JERUSALEM!**

## Talmudic Sources

### Talmud, Pesachim 116a

**Samuel said:**
> "Bread of ['oni] [means] bread over which we recite ['onin] many words."

**It was taught likewise:**
> "'Bread of ['oni]' means bread over which we recite ['onin] many words."

**Another interpretation:**
> "'Bread of ['oni]': 'ani [poverty] is written: just as a beggar generally has a piece, so here too a piece [is taken]."

**Another interpretation:**
> "Just as a poor man fires [the oven] and his wife bakes, so here too, he heats and she bakes."

## Jerusalem Talmud 10:1

### Four Cups Requirement

**Mishnah:**
> "[At the Seder those who serve] should not give him fewer than four cups of wine even if [the funds come] from the charity plate."

### Gemara - Source of Four Cups

**Rabbi Yohanan in name of Rabbi Benaiah:**
> "[They] correspond to the four redemptions [or acts of redemption, mentioned in reference to Egypt]:"

**Biblical Source - Exodus 6:6-7:**
> "Say, therefore, to the Israelite people: I am the Lord. **I will take you out** [from under the burdens of the Egyptians and deliver you from their bondage. **I will deliver you** [from their bondage]. **I will redeem you** with an outstretched arm and through extraordinary chastisements]. **And I will take you** to be my people,' etc."

**The Four Terms:**
1. "I will take out"
2. "I will deliver"
3. "I will redeem"
4. "I will take"

## The Rambam's Laws of Chametz and Matza - Chapter Seven

### Halacha 1: The Mitzvah to Tell the Story

**Positive Commandment:**
> "It is a positive commandment of the Torah to relate the miracles and wonders wrought for our ancestors in Egypt on the night of the fifteenth of Nisan, as [Exodus 13:3] states: 'Remember this day, on which you left Egypt,' just as [Exodus 20:8] states: 'Remember the Sabbath day.'"

**When:**
> "From where [is it derived that this mitzvah is to be fulfilled on] the night of the fifteenth? The Torah teaches [Exodus 13:8]: 'And you shall tell your son on that day, saying: "It is because of this..."' [implying that the mitzvah is to be fulfilled] when matzah and maror are placed before you."

**Universal Obligation:**
> "[The mitzvah applies] even though one does not have a son. Even great Sages are obligated to tell about the Exodus from Egypt. Whoever elaborates concerning the events which occurred and took place is worthy of praise."

---

### Halacha 2: Teaching According to Understanding

**Obligation to Inform Sons:**
> "It is a mitzvah to inform one's sons even though they do not ask, as [Exodus 13:8] states: 'You shall tell your son.'"

**Teaching Method:**
> "A father should teach his son according to the son's knowledge:"

**How Applied:**

**If young or foolish:**
> "He should tell him: 'My son, in Egypt, we were all slaves like this maidservant or this slave. On this night, the Holy One, Blessed be He, redeemed us and took us out to freedom.'"

**If older and wise:**
> "He should inform him what happened to us in Egypt and the miracles wrought for us by Moses, our teacher; everything according to the son's knowledge."

---

### Halacha 3: Making Changes to Provoke Questions

**Purpose:**
> "He should make changes on this night so that the children will see and will [be motivated to] ask: 'Why is this night different from all other nights?' until he replies to them: 'This and this occurred; this and this took place.'"

**What Changes:**
- Give them roasted seeds and nuts
- Table should be taken away before they eat
- Matzots should be snatched from each other
- And the like

**Without a Son:**
> "When a person does not have a son, his wife should ask him. If he does not have a wife, [he and a colleague] should ask each other: 'Why is this night different?' This applies even if they are all wise."

**Alone:**
> "A person who is alone should ask himself: 'Why is this night different?'"

---

### Halacha 4: From Degradation to Praise

**Pattern:**
> "One must begin [the narrative describing our ancestors'] base [roots] and conclude with [their] praise."

**What This Means:**

**Begin:**
> "One begins relating how originally, in the age of Terach, our ancestors denied [God's existence] and strayed after vanity, pursuing idol worship."

**Conclude:**
> "One concludes with the true faith: how the Omnipresent has drawn us close to Him, separated us from the gentiles, and drawn us near to His Oneness."

**Similarly:**
> "One begins by stating that we were slaves to Pharaoh in Egypt and [describing] all the evil done to us, and concludes with the miracles and wonders that were wrought upon us, and our freedom."

**Biblical Template:**
> "This [implies] that one should extrapolate [the passage beginning] from [Deuteronomy 26:5]: 'An Aramean sought to destroy my ancestor...' until one concludes the entire passage."

**Praise for Elaboration:**
> "Whoever adds and extends his extrapolation of this passage is praiseworthy."

---

### Halacha 5: Three Essential Matters

**Obligation:**
> "Whoever does not mention these three matters on the night of the fifteenth has not fulfilled his obligation. They are: the Paschal sacrifice, matzah, and maror."

**The Paschal Sacrifice:**
> "[It is eaten] because the Omnipresent passed over the houses of our ancestors in Egypt as [Exodus 12:27] states: 'And you shall say: "It is the Paschal sacrifice to God."'"

**The Bitter Herbs:**
> "[They are eaten] because the Egyptians embittered the lives of our ancestors in Egypt."

**The Matzah:**
> "[It is eaten] because of the redemption."

**Reference:**
> "These statements are all referred to as the Haggadah."

---

### Halacha 6: As If You Yourself Left Egypt

**Personal Experience:**
> "In each and every generation, a person must present himself as if he, himself, has now left the slavery of Egypt, as [Deuteronomy 6:23] states: 'He took us out from there.'"

**Torah Command:**
> "Regarding this manner, God commanded in the Torah: 'Remember that you were a slave [Deuteronomy 5:15]' - i.e., as if you, yourself, were a slave and went out to freedom and were redeemed."

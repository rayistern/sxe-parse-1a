# Minyanim - Pesach Prayer Services Schedule

**Type**: Synagogue Service Schedule
**Holiday**: Pesach (Passover)
**Dates**: Friday, April 19 - Saturday, April 27
**Format**: Complete daily prayer time listings for all eight days of Pesach

## Key Features of This Schedule

### Erev Pesach Customs

**Fast of the Firstborn**: The morning service accommodates the custom for firstborn males to fast on erev Pesach, commemorating that they were spared during the plague of the firstborn in Egypt.

**Siyum**: A siyum (completion of a Talmudic tractate) is held at 7:30 a.m., allowing firstborn males to participate in a seudat mitzvah (festive meal for a mitzvah), which permits them to eat and not fast the entire day. The tractate is noted as "tba" (to be announced).

**Late Maariv**: The 7:45 p.m. Maariv time is specifically noted as "followed by 1st Seder," indicating the service is scheduled to allow sufficient time after nightfall to begin the Seder promptly.

### Shabbos Accommodations

**First Day Landing on Shabbos**: The schedule shows the first day of Pesach coinciding with Shabbos, requiring special attention to timing and procedures.

**Later Morning Times**: Morning services on Yom Tov days (first, second, seventh, and eighth days) are scheduled at 10:00 a.m., later than the 8:20 a.m. Chol HaMoed times, recognizing the festive nature of Yom Tov and allowing families more preparation time.

### Chol HaMoed Pattern

**Consistent Timing**: All four days of Chol HaMoed maintain identical schedules:
- Shacharis: 8:20 a.m.
- Mincha/Maariv: 7:20 p.m.

This consistency helps congregants plan their intermediate days while maintaining daily prayer commitments.

### Eighth Day Special Features

**Yizkor Service**: Scheduled at 11:30 a.m., allowing latecomers specifically for Yizkor to arrive after Shacharis has begun.

**Seudas Mashiach**: The afternoon service explicitly includes "Seudas Mashiach" (Meal of Moshiach), a Chabad custom of holding a festive meal on the last day of Pesach, initiated by the Baal Shem Tov, to celebrate the future redemption. The timing at 6:45 p.m. positions it between Mincha and Maariv.

**Yom Tov End Time**: Explicitly listed as 8:30 p.m., informing congregants when they may resume weekday activities.

## For Shluchim: Practical Planning and Communication

**Publication Timing**: This schedule should be distributed well in advance - ideally 2-3 weeks before Pesach - through multiple channels: email blasts, shul bulletins, website posting, social media, and printed handouts distributed on the Shabbos before Pesach.

**"TBA" Flexibility**: The siyum tractate listed as "tba" allows flexibility to assign the tractate based on which congregants are actually completing study, or to have the rabbi prepare whichever tractate is most appropriate. Announce the specific tractate once confirmed, at least a week before Pesach.

**Firstborn Outreach**: The siyum is a natural outreach opportunity. Contact firstborn males in the congregation (check your database/records) to personally invite them, explaining the significance and that it exempts them from fasting. This personal touch increases attendance and demonstrates care.

**Seder Timing Communication**: Note that Maariv is "followed by" Seders, not "before" Seders - this signals to congregants they should plan to attend shul first, then go home for the Seder, rather than trying to squeeze in Maariv after starting their Seder.

**Later Morning Times Strategy**: The 10:00 a.m. start for Yom Tov allows families with young children, elderly members, or guests to arrive without rushed morning preparations. However, announce that services will start on time and won't wait for late arrivals, maintaining respect for punctual attendees.

**Chol HaMoed Consistency**: The uniform 8:20 a.m. time throughout Chol HaMoed helps working professionals who may have taken limited vacation time - they know exactly when to plan arrival each day.

**Yizkor Timing Strategy**: Listing Yizkor at 11:30 a.m. (90 minutes after Shacharis start) accounts for Torah reading, Yizkor service preparation, and potential delays. It also allows people who only attend for Yizkor to arrive without sitting through the full service, while not offending those who attend the full service.

**Seudas Mashiach Promotion**: Explicitly listing "Seudas Mashiach" in the schedule elevates it from an optional custom to an official shul program, increasing attendance. Promote it as a unique Chabad custom, explaining its mystical significance and encouraging families to participate before going home.

**End Time Clarity**: Listing "Yom Tov Ends: 8:30 p.m." prevents confusion about when people may drive, turn on electronics, etc. This is especially important for guests or less observant members who may not know how to calculate this themselves.

**Format Considerations**: The clean, simple layout makes this schedule easy to read at a glance. Consider:
  - Printing on colored paper (not the same color as regular Shabbos schedules) so it stands out
  - Making it wallet-sized or fridge-magnet-sized for easy reference
  - Including it in your custom Haggadah if you produce one
  - Creating a digital calendar invite that congregants can import to their phones

**Accessibility Planning**: Morning times at 10:00 a.m. and 11:30 a.m. for Yizkor require planning for those who need assistance (elderly, disabled). Arrange for volunteers to provide rides, assist with accessibility needs, and ensure seating is available for those who arrive specifically for Yizkor.

**Staffing Requirements**: This schedule helps determine when additional volunteers, security, child care, and other resources are needed:
  - Erev Pesach morning: Siyum food/drinks
  - Both Seder nights: Ensure shul is fully cleaned and locked before everyone leaves for Seders
  - Yizkor: Prepare memorial candles, Yizkor prayer cards
  - Seudas Mashiach: Food preparation, table setup, possibly guest speaker

**Weather Contingencies**: For communities where Shabbos/Yom Tov coinciding with Pesach in late April means unpredictable weather, have contingency plans for snow/rain/heat that might affect attendance, parking, or walking distances.

**Guest Accommodations**: The schedule helps guests staying in the area plan their movements between host families and shul. Consider adding a note about approximate service lengths to help hosts plan meals.

**Conflicting Programs**: Review this schedule against any community-wide programs (model Seders, Chol HaMoed trips, children's programs) to ensure no scheduling conflicts and that transitions between shul and programs are feasible.

**Follow-Up**: After Pesach, note actual attendance patterns against this schedule to refine timing for next year. If certain services had very low turnout, consider whether the timing should be adjusted or whether those services should be combined with others.

# [Misc > For Writer > _5 Chanukah > General] "Dancing on Purim" - Purim Parody of "Waltzing Matilda"

## Note: Purim Content (Not Chanukah)

**Original Song:** "Waltzing Matilda" (Australian folk song)

**Parody Title:** "Dancing on Purim"

**Theme:** Humorous account of Purim celebration and drinking culture

**Tone:** Light-hearted, self-deprecating, community humor

---

## Complete Lyrics

### Verse 1: The Reluctant Dancer

```
You came to a party, you sat and talked and sat and ate
But now it's time for dancing on Purim with me
But your back is in pain, and your joints they all are aching
So you aren't dancing on Purim with me

Dancing on Purim, Dancing on Purim
Won't you come dancing on Purim with me
So you drink a L'chaim followed by a many others
Now you are dancing on Purim with me
```

**Theme:** Physical limitations overcome by l'chaim

**Humor:** Back pain and aching joints cured by alcohol

**Truth:** Older adults loosening up at Purim party

---

### Verse 2: The Seated Neighbor

```
You have a neighbor, who is still sitting on his seat
Looking for his seat mate, who's dancing with me
But he won't come a-dancing, because he hasn't wet his beak
So he isn't dancing on Purim with me

Dancing on Purim, Dancing on Purim
Won't you come dancing on Purim with me
So you give him a L'chaim, followed by a many others
Now he is dancing on Purim with me
```

**Theme:** The holdout who needs encouragement

**Humor:** "Wet his beak" (needs to drink first)

**Social dynamic:** Getting everyone involved

**Purim mitzvah:** Giving others to drink

---

### Verse 3: The Angry Wife

```
But his wife isn't happy, she's still sitting at the table
Now her husband's left her, he's dancing with me
You know we're in trouble, when she is heading our way
Her husband's no longer dancing with me

Dancing on Purim, Dancing on Purim
Won't you come dancing on Purim with me
So you give her a L'chaim, followed by a many others
Now she is dancing on Purim with my wife
```

**Theme:** Marital dynamics at Purim party

**Humor:** Wife's anger at husband abandoning her

**Resolution:** Get her to drink too!

**Gender dynamics:** Wives dancing together

**Note:** "with my wife" (not "with me")

---

### Verse 4: The Consequences

```
So I come home, and I can't walk a merry line
And I want to dance, straight into my bed
But my wife, had a whole different plan for me
Let me tell you she, isn't dancing with me

Dancing on Purim, Dancing on Purim
Won't you come dancing on Purim with me
So now I'm hung over, and I have a merry headache
I am never dancing on Purim with you
```

**Theme:** The aftermath/hangover

**Humor:** Can't walk straight, wife is angry

**Reality check:** "Never dancing on Purim with you"

**Irony:** Until next Purim!

**Truth:** Classic Purim morning-after experience

## Purim Drinking Culture Referenced

### The Role of L'chaim

**Social Lubricant:**
- Gets people dancing
- Overcomes physical limitations
- Brings community together
- Part of Purim mitzvah

**Progressive Effect:**
- First person: needs l'chaim to dance
- Second person: needs l'chaim to join
- Third person (wife): needs l'chaim to not be angry
- Fourth consequence: too many l'chaims

---

### Halachic Context

**Ad D'Lo Yada:**
- Mitzvah to drink on Purim
- "Until you don't know..."
- More lenient than other times
- Community celebration

**Reality:**
- People do drink significantly
- Loosens inhibitions
- Creates festive atmosphere
- Sometimes overdo it (hence hangover)

**Bottom Line:**

Humorous Purim parody of "Waltzing Matilda" titled "Dancing on Purim" chronicles progression from reluctant party attendance to drunken dancing to morning-after regret through four verses with repeating chorus. **Verse 1**: Reluctant dancer with back pain and aching joints overcome by "a l'chaim followed by many others" now dancing. **Verse 2**: Seated neighbor "who hasn't wet his beak" convinced to dance after receiving l'chaim. **Verse 3**: Angry wife whose husband abandoned her at table heading their way, resolved by giving her l'chaim so "now she is dancing on Purim with my wife" (not with me—gender-appropriate dancing). **Verse 4**: Consequences arrive—"can't walk a merry line," want to "dance straight into my bed," wife has "whole different plan for me," concluding "now I'm hung over and I have a merry headache, I am never dancing on Purim with you" (until next year, implied). Chorus: "Dancing on Purim, Dancing on Purim, Won't you come dancing on Purim with me." Humor elements include self-deprecating physical ailments, social pressure dynamics, marital negotiations at community events, Purim drinking culture (l'chaim as social lubricant progressing to excess), morning-after reality check. Uses familiar public domain tune ensuring easy audience participation. Performance timing: during Purim seudah after meal, when people are merry, lead singer teaches chorus first then tells story through verses. Audience participation: raise cups on "l'chaim," sway during chorus, point to spouses, nod knowingly at shared experiences. Thematic arc from reluctance → social participation → overindulgence → regret reflects universal Purim experience. Appropriate for adult-oriented events in communities with humor culture, less appropriate for children's parties or very formal settings. Continues traditional Purim spiel custom of musical parodies and humorous performances celebrating community bonds through self-aware comedy.

# [Misc > For Writer > _5 Chanukah > Car Menorah-Parade] Car Menorah Parade - Complete Planning Guide

## Planning Overview

### The Challenge

It's important that it be clear to anyone watching the parade what this is. You may have far more cars than you have menorahs, and while it's wonderful to involve as many people as possible, if you're not careful it can **look like a funeral procession rather than a parade**.

### The Solution

To avoid this—and to ensure the maximum pirsumei nissa—you can decorate every car in the parade, even if they don't have a menorah.

### 4. Music

**CRITICAL: This MUST BE DONE CORRECTLY!**

**The Problem:**
If every car is blasting its own Chanukah tunes, the result is a **cacophony**. And if left to their own devices, many participants may play music unsuitable for a Chabad menorah parade.

**The Solution:**
Get an FM transmitter and share the channel frequency with attendees. This allows everyone to play the same music from their cars—the music you choose.

**Recommended Equipment:**
FM transmitter with 1000-foot range
[Amazon Option](https://www.amazon.com/gp/aw/d/B01D44EP4K?psc=1&ref=yo_pop_mb_pd_title)
- Comes with magnet base
- Antenna connects to roof of parade
- 2.5 mile range
- Broadcasts synchronized music

**How It Works:**
1. Purchase FM transmitter
2. Create Chanukah music playlist
3. Broadcast on specific FM frequency
4. Share frequency with all participants
5. Everyone tunes car radio to that station
6. Synchronized music throughout parade

**Benefits:**
- Professional sound
- Control content
- Synchronized experience
- Impressive coordination

## Police Escort

### Why It's Difficult Without One

Traffic lights and other cars will often break up your formation, leading to confusion and lost cars.

### How to Obtain

**Step 1: Contact Local Police Department**
- Request police escort
- 2-3 months in advance
- Explain community event
- Provide event details

**Typical Escort:**
- One "lead" car (front)
- One "chase" car (rear)
- Sometimes additional vehicles to block intersections

---

### If Police Department is Unwilling

#### Objection: Mixing Church and State

**Their Concern:**
"Don't want to appear to be joining/supporting a religious event"

**Your Response:**
"You don't need them to lead, you need them to secure the parade. They will be protecting your free speech, not engaging in their own."

**Key Point:**
- This is about public safety
- Not religious endorsement
- Protecting free exercise of religion
- Standard escort service

---

#### Solution 1: Hire Off-Duty Officers

**Cost:** $35-$100 per officer per hour

**Process:**
1. Contact police department
2. Request off-duty officer hire
3. Coordinate directly with officers
4. Provide event details
5. Pay officers directly or through department

**Advantages:**
- Actual police officers
- Official vehicles and uniforms
- Professional traffic control
- Security presence

---

#### Solution 2: Apply for Parade Permit

**Process:**
1. Contact city/township clerk
2. Request parade permit application
3. Pay fee (varies by location)
4. Submit required information
5. Wait for approval

**Benefits:**
- Makes event official
- May include police escort
- Traffic control authorized
- Insurance may be easier

**See:** License for Parade section below

---

#### Solution 3: Police-Escorted Motorcade

**Alternative Framing:**
Call it a police-escorted motorcade/procession similar to a funeral.

**Advantage:**
Can get around some permit requirements.

**How It Works:**
- Transit police may have authority
- Can escort procession without permit
- Similar to funeral processions
- Standard procedure

## License for Parade

### When Required

According to many local ordinances, one must obtain a permit before a parade.

### Recommendation

If possible, **instead of trying to get around this requirement, get a permit.**

**Benefits:**
- Helps with safety of parade
- Makes it easier to obtain police escort
- Makes parade much more "official"
- Helps it run smoother
- Shows professionalism

---

### Application Process

**Step 1: Contact City/Township**
- Clerk's office or special events department
- Request parade permit information

**Step 2: Submit Application**
- Complete application form
- Provide event details:
  - Date, time, duration
  - Route with map
  - Expected participants
  - Expected spectators
  - Contact information
  - Insurance information

**Step 3: Pay Fee**
- Varies by municipality
- Typically $50-$500
- Sometimes waived for non-profits

**Step 4: Coordinate**
- Police department
- Public works
- Any street closures
- Traffic control plan

**Step 5: Receive Permit**
- Keep copy with you during event
- Provide copy to police escort
- Have emergency contact info

---

### Timeline

**3 Months Before:**
- Research permit requirements
- Contact appropriate office

**2 Months Before:**
- Submit application
- Pay fees
- Coordinate with police

**1 Month Before:**
- Receive permit
- Finalize route
- Confirm escorts

**1 Week Before:**
- Final coordination
- Confirm all details
- Prepare participant materials

## Success Factors

### Making Your Parade Look Professional

**Essential Elements:**

1. **Police Escort** - Keeps formation together
2. **Music Coordination** - FM transmitter for synchronized sound
3. **Every Car Decorated** - Magnets, flags, balloons minimum
4. **Clear Signage** - What is this parade about?
5. **Route Planning** - Avoid complex intersections
6. **Participant Instructions** - Clear info sheet
7. **Media Coordination** - Invite press, take photos

---

### Maximizing Pirsumei Nisa

**Visibility Strategies:**

**Before Parade:**
- Press releases
- Social media promotion
- Route through high-traffic areas
- Timing during busy hours

**During Parade:**
- Fire truck or large vehicle lead
- Music draws attention
- Waving, engagement with spectators
- Stop at key locations
- Live streaming on social media

**After Parade:**
- Photos and videos
- Media coverage
- Social media posts
- Thank you to participants
- Highlight reel for next year

## Final Thoughts

A successful car menorah parade requires:

1. **Planning** - Start 2-3 months ahead
2. **Permits & Police** - Legal and safe
3. **Visual Impact** - Every car decorated
4. **Coordination** - Synchronized music, clear route
5. **Publicity** - Media coverage, social media
6. **Community** - Involvement builds ownership

The investment in proper equipment and planning pays off in:
- Pirsumei nisa
- Community building
- Annual tradition
- Media coverage
- Children's excitement
- Jewish pride

With proper preparation, your car menorah parade can become the highlight of Chanukah in your community.

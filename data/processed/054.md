# Part I: Yehudit's Story

## Historical Context

**Timeline Uncertainty:**
The story of Yehudit is not clearly dated. First appeared in an ancient book named after the heroine, originally written in Hebrew but the original text was lost. Only a Greek translation remained, and not a very accurate one.

**Possible Dating:**
According to one version, it happened during the time of the Maccabean revolt against Syrian oppression. Yehudit may have been a daughter of Yochanan the high priest, father of the Hasmonean family.

**Enduring Impact:**
> "The heroic deed of Yehudit has inspired faith and courage in the hearts of Jews throughout the ages."

---

## The Siege of Bethulia

### The Enemy Commander

**Holofernes:**
- Mighty Syrian-Greek general
- Head of a huge army
- Notorious for cruelty in suppressing rebellions
- Showed no mercy to men, women and children
- Determined to crush Bethulia's rebellion

**The Conflict:**
- Town of Bethulia in Judea under siege
- Inhabitants refused Syrian rule
- Men fought bravely against superior forces

---

### The Starvation Strategy

**Holofernes's Plan:**
- Couldn't take fortified town by force
- Decided to starve inhabitants into submission
- Cut off food and water supply
- Town brought to verge of surrender

**The Crisis:**
- People gathered in marketplace
- Demanded surrender rather than death by starvation
- Uzziah (defense commander) and elders tried to calm them
- Final plea: "Give us five more days. If no salvation comes by the end of five days, we will surrender. Just five more days..."
- Reluctantly, people agreed

---

## Yehudit Steps Forward

### Who Was Yehudit?

**Background:**
- Noble daughter of Yochanan the high priest
- Young widow
- Lost beloved husband Menashe several years prior
- Devoted time to prayer and acts of charity

**Character:**
- Blessed with extraordinary charm, grace and beauty
- Particularly respected for:
  - Devoutness
  - Modesty
  - Lovingkindness

---

### Her Challenge to the Leaders

**Yehudit's Words:**
> "Why do you test G-d, giving Him only five days in which to send us His help? If you truly have faith in G-d, you must never give up your trust in Him. Besides, don't you know that surrender to Holofernes is worse than death?!"

**Leadership's Response:**
> "You are quite right, daughter, but what can we do? Only a downpour of rain that would fill our empty cisterns could save our people, but it is not the rainy season. We are all suffering the pangs of hunger and thirst. Pray for us, Yehudit, and maybe G-d will accept your prayers..."

**Yehudit's Reply:**
> "We must all continue to pray, and never despair of G-d's help. But I have also thought of a plan. I ask your permission to leave town together with my maid. I want to go to Holofernes..."

---

### The Risky Mission

**Leaders' Shock:**
> "Do you know what you are saying, Yehudit? Would you sacrifice your life and honor on the slim chance that you might soften Holofernes's heart? We cannot allow you to make such a sacrifice for us."

**Yehudit's Biblical Precedent:**
> "It has happened before that G-d sent His salvation through a woman. Yael, the wife of Heber, was her name, as you well know. It was into her hands that G-d delivered the cruel Sisera..."

**Resolution:**
- Uzziah and elders tried to discourage her
- She insisted she be allowed to try
- Finally, they agreed

---

## Entering the Enemy Camp

### The Journey

**Preparation:**
- Dressed in her best clothes (not worn since husband passed away)
- Delicate veil all but hid beautiful face
- Accompanied by faithful maid
- Basket with rolls, cheese and several bottles of old wine
- Sun beginning to set behind green mountains
- Lips whispering prayers to G-d

**Encounter with Sentries:**
> "We have an important message for your commander, the brave Holofernes. Take us to him at once."

---

### Meeting Holofernes

**Her Cover Story:**
> "I am but a plain widow from Bethulia. Yehudit is my name. I came to tell you how to capture the town, in the hope that you will deal mercifully with its inhabitants..."

**The Deception:**
- Claimed life in besieged town unbearable
- Said she bribed watchmen to let her out
- Praised his bravery and mighty deeds
- Described desperate situation in town
- Key claim: "Their faith in G-d remained strong, and so long as they had faith, they would not surrender"
- Critical prediction: When kosher food runs out, they'll eat unkosher food, "and then G-d's anger would be turned against them, and the town would fall"

---

### Winning His Trust

**Holofernes's Question:**
> "But how will I know when the defenders of the citadel will begin to eat unkosher food, as you say, so that I can then storm the walls and capture the city?"

**Yehudit's Solution:**
> "I had thought of that. I have arranged with the watchmen at the city's gates that I would come to the gate every evening to exchange information: I will tell them what's doing here, and they will tell me what's doing there."

**Holofernes's Response:**
- Completely captivated by her
- Promised: "If you are telling me the truth, and will indeed help me capture the city, you will be my wife!"
- Gave orders: Complete freedom for her and maid to walk through camp
- Anyone molesting them would be put to death immediately
- Comfortable tent prepared next to his

---

## The Setup

### Daily Routine Established

**Freedom of Movement:**
- Two veiled women walked leisurely through camp any time of day or evening
- Everyone gave them wide berth (fearful of commander's orders)
- Soon attracted little attention
- Could walk to city gates after dark unnoticed

**Messages to City:**
> "Tell Uzziah that, thank G-d, everything is shaping up according to plan. With G-d's help, we shall prevail over our enemy. Keep your trust strong in G-d; do not lose hope for a moment!"

**Second Evening:**
- Repeated same message
- Added that she'd won Holofernes's complete confidence

---

### The Waiting Game

**Holofernes's Impatience:**
- Spent most time drinking
- When not completely drunk, sent for Yehudit
- She always came with her maid
- By third day, getting impatient

**His Complaint:**
> "Well, gracious Yehudit, what intelligence do you bring me today? My men are getting impatient and demoralized doing nothing; they cannot wait to capture the city and have their fun..."

**Yehudit's Report:**
> "I have very good news, general. There is not a scrap of kosher food left in the city now. In a day or two, famine will drive them to eat their cats and dogs and mules. Then G-d will deliver them into your hands!"

**Holofernes's Invitation:**
> "Wonderful, wonderful! This surely calls for a celebration. Tonight we'll have a party, just you and I. I shall expect you as my honored guest."

---

## The Climactic Night

### The Feast

**Setup:**
- Table laden with various delicacies
- General delighted to welcome her
- Bade her partake of feast

**Yehudit's Strategy:**
- Said she brought her own food and wine
- Prepared especially for the occasion
- "My goat cheese is famous in all of Bethulia, I'm sure you'll like it, General"
- Strong, undiluted wine

**The Execution:**
- She fed him cheese, chunk after chunk
- He washed it down with wine
- Before long: sprawled on ground, dead drunk

---

### Yehudit's Prayer

**Her Words to G-d:**
> "Answer me, O L-rd, as You answered Yael, the wife of Heber the Kenite, when you delivered the wicked general Sisera into her hands. Strengthen me this once, that I may bring Your deliverance to my people whom this cruel man vowed to destroy, and let the nations know that You have not forsaken us..."

---

### The Deed

**The Act:**
- Unsheathed Holofernes's heavy sword
- Took aim at his neck
- Brought sword down with all her might
- Sat for moment to compose herself
- Wrapped general's head in rags
- Concealed under shawl
- Calmly walked out to her own tent

**Escape:**
> "Come quickly, but let's not arouse suspicion."

- Two veiled women walked leisurely as usual
- Reached city gates
- "Take me to Uzziah at once"

---

## Victory

### Presenting the Prize

**Uzziah's Reaction:**
Could not believe his eyes at the gruesome prize.

**Yehudit's Command:**
> "There is no time to lose. Prepare your men for a surprise attack at dawn. The enemy's camp is not prepared for it. When they run to their commander's tent, they will find his headless body, and they will flee for their lives..."

**The Outcome:**
> "This is precisely what happened."

- Enemy fled in confusion and terror
- Left much booty behind
- Wonderful victory
- Saved by G-d-fearing and brave daughter of Yochanan the high priest

---

## Linking Past to Present

**Key Insight:**
Yehudit put her life on the line (both spiritually and physically) to save her community. While fighting the war was traditionally men's work, when necessary, a woman stepped forward to do what needed to be done.

# Part II: Women and Chanukah

## Halachic Sources

### 1. Women's Obligation in Mitzvah

**Source:** Talmud Shabbat 23a

> "Women are obligated to kindle the Chanukah lights, for they too, were included in the miracle."

---

### 2. Custom to Eat Cheese

**Source:** Shulchan Aruch Orach Chaim 670

> "There are those who say that we have the custom to eat cheese on Chanukah to commemorate the miracle when Yehudit fed cheese to the enemy."

---

### 3. Women Refrain from Work

**Source:** Me'Am Loez, Bamidbar, page 98

> "It is customary for women to refrain from work while the candles are burning... because another miracle happened then by virtue of a woman."

---

### 4. Extended Explanation

**Source:** Kitzur Shulchan Aruch, Laws of Chanukah 139:3

**Work Permitted on Chanukah:**
Work is permitted on Chanukah. Women, however, follow the custom of refraining from work during the time the Chanukah candles are burning at home. They should continue this custom without any leniency.

**Why Women's Greater Stringency:**

**Reason 1 - Harsher Decrees:**
> "The Greek's decrees were harsher for the women. They had decreed that any Jewish virgin who is getting married, must first have sexual relations with the Greek governor."

**Reason 2 - Miracle Through a Woman:**
The extended version of Yehudit's story:
- Daughter of Yochanan, the High Priest
- Very beautiful
- Enemy ruler proposed cohabitation
- She told him she would accede
- Fed him dishes of cheese to make him thirsty
- He drank much wine and became intoxicated
- While asleep, she cut off his head
- Brought it to Jerusalem
- Greek leader and army fled when they saw ruler slain

**Commemorative Custom:**
> "To commemorate this miracle, there are some who follow the custom of eating dairy dishes on Chanukah."

# Part III: Chana and Her Seven Sons

## The Story of Ultimate Sacrifice

### The Persecution

**Antiochus's Campaign:**
- Determined to enforce vicious edicts
- Effectively destroying Jewish attachment to Torah
- Forbade all religious laws
- Anyone with Torah would be executed
- Circumcision, kosher food, Shabbat outlawed
- All vestiges of Judaism forbidden

**Phillip's Enforcement:**
- Appointed governor of Judea
- Set out to ruthlessly enforce edicts
- Began with arrest of notable sage Elazar (High Priest)
- Elazar chose martyrdom over submission

---

### The Arrest

**The Family:**
Chana and her seven sons arrested, bound, brought before the king.

---

## The First Son

**Antiochus's Attempt:**
Tried to convince eldest boy to abandon Torah.

**The Youth's Response:**
> "Why do you bother with this long speech, trying to inflict your abominable religion upon us? We are ready to welcome death for the sake of our holy Torah!"

**The King's Reaction:**
- Furious
- Ordered boy's death
- Soldiers tortured the boy
- Forced mother and six brothers to watch
- King sure sight would intimidate prisoners

**Actual Result:**
> "Instead, the martyrdom spurred the family to a deep resolve to accept their fate and to sanctify G-d's name."

---

## The Second Son

**The Plea:**
Even king's retinue begged boy to obey.

**The Response:**
> "Do what you will with me. I am no less than my brother in devotion to G-d."

**His Final Words:**
> "Woe to you, pitiless tyrant! Our souls go to G-d. And when G-d will awaken the dead and His martyred servants, we will live. But you--your soul will dwell in a place of eternal abhorrence!"

---

## The Third, Fourth, Fifth, and Sixth Sons

**Third Brother:**
Unflinchingly suffered same fate.

**Fourth Brother:**
Echoed his brothers' exhortations, faced brutal death with firm resolve.

**Fifth Brother's Words:**
> "Don't suppose that G-d has handed us over to you to exalt you or because He hates us. It is because He loves us and has granted us this honor. G-d will take His vengeance upon you and your progeny."

**Sixth Brother:**
Same end as brothers who preceded him. Words bespoke deep faith that G-d would ultimately requite the suffering of His servants.

**Chana's Role:**
> "Throughout this horrible sequence Chana stood by her sons, giving them strength and encouragement."

---

## The Seventh Son

### The King's Final Attempt

**The Offer:**
- Only youngest child remained
- King offered gold and silver
- Seven-year-old displayed same courage
- Taunted king to carry out threats

**The King's Disbelief:**
Couldn't believe such words from mere child. Called out to Chana.

---

### Chana's Counsel to Her Son

**The Pretense:**
- Stood before murderer of her children
- King: "Woman, have compassion upon this child. Persuade him to do my will so that you will have at least one surviving child and you too will live."
- She pretended to agree
- Asked to speak with son

**Her True Message:**
> "My son, I carried you in my body for nine months, I nursed you for two years and I have fed you until today. I have taught you to fear G-d and uphold His Torah. See the heaven and the earth, the sea and the land, fire, water, wind and every other creation. Know that they were all created by G-d's word. He created man to serve Him and He will reward man for his deeds."

**About the King:**
> "The king knows he is condemned before G-d. He thinks that if he convinces you, G-d will have mercy on him."

**About Life and Death:**
> "G-d controls your life's breath and can take your soul whenever He desires. If only I could see the greatness of your glorious place where we would be illuminated with G-d's light and rejoice and exult together."

**To the King:**
> "I was unable to prevail upon him."

---

### The Child's Response

**His Words:**
> "Whom are you seeking to overpower with your words and enticements? I laugh at your foolishness. I believe in the Torah and in G-d Whom you blaspheme. You will remain an abomination upon all mankind, loathsome and far from G-d."

**The King's Final Test:**
According to the Talmud, Antiochus gave boy chance to save himself by ostensibly bowing down to retrieve his signet ring. The boy refused.

---

### Chana's Final Moments

**The Last Kiss:**
As they removed him, Chana begged to kiss him one last time.

**Her Words:**
> "My children, tell your ancestor Abraham, 'You bound only one son upon an altar, but I bound seven.'"

**The Execution:**
Antiochus ordered child be tortured even more than his brothers.

**Chana's Death:**
- Left surrounded by bodies of her sons
- Prayer exalting G-d on her lips
- Threw herself from a roof
- Rested beside her martyred sons

---

# Discussion Points for Class

## Yehudit's Story

**Themes:**
1. **Faith and Action** - "We must all continue to pray, and never despair... But I have also thought of a plan"
2. **Personal Risk for Community** - Put life and honor on line
3. **Strategic Thinking** - Carefully planned approach
4. **Precedent** - Referenced Yael as biblical model
5. **Divine Partnership** - Prayed at critical moment, then acted

**Questions:**
- What enabled Yehudit to take such a risk?
- How did she balance trust in G-d with practical action?
- What can we learn about community responsibility?

---

## Chana's Story

**Themes:**
1. **Spiritual Courage** - Not just physical bravery
2. **Mother's Role** - Strengthening children's resolve
3. **Kiddush Hashem** - Sanctifying G-d's name
4. **Comparison to Akeidah** - "You bound one, I bound seven"
5. **Eternal Perspective** - Focus on world to come

**Questions:**
- How could Chana encourage her children toward death?
- What does this teach about priorities in life?
- How do we apply this to our challenges (obviously much smaller)?

---

## Women's Role in Chanukah

**Themes:**
1. **Equal Obligation** - Women obligated in mitzvah
2. **Recognition** - Special customs commemorating women's role
3. **Stringency** - Women take on extra observance
4. **Historical Memory** - Keeping story alive through practice

**Questions:**
- Why are women's Chanukah customs stricter?
- What does this teach about women's spiritual power?
- How can we honor these heroines today?

---

## Universal Applications

**For Everyone:**
1. How far would you go for your community?
2. What role does faith play in courageous action?
3. How do we teach our children proper priorities?
4. What would we be willing to sacrifice for Judaism?
5. How do we maintain identity under pressure?

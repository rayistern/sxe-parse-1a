## Key Takeaways

### Different Approaches for Different Situations

**Large Jewish Communities:**
- More established opposition
- But also more support available
- Fight can generate publicity
- May be worth standing firm

**Small/New Communities:**
- More vulnerable position
- Less community backup
- May need to wait for support
- Consider gradual approach

---

### The Rebbe's Guidance - Critical Understanding

**What the Rebbe Said About Machlokes:**

**From Teaneck Situation:**
- Rebbe wrote: "Bitchila kosavti lo shelo lehadlik eish hamachlokes" (At the very outset, I wrote to him not to ignite the fire of Machlokes)
- When Senator told them to back off: Rebbe wrote "V'harei boze hatzedek itoy" (For in this, he is correct)
- They backed off that year, worked it out the next year

**From Mayor Koch Situation:**
- Rabbi Butman wanted to stand strong for specific location
- Rebbe wrote back: Don't start a Machlokes with him
- Ended up getting different public space

**Clear Principle:**
> "While the Rebbe defends the right to put up a public Menorah, **he doesn't want us to pick fights about them.**"

---

### Strategies That Have Worked

**1. Media Pressure:**
- Inform media of event before opposition
- Make cancellation more difficult
- Negative publicity for mall/city

**2. Play Committed:**
- Act as if plans are irreversible
- "Already invited important people"
- "Too late to cancel"

**3. Build Relationships First:**
- Personal meeting with opposing clergy
- Build community support
- Wait until you have backup

**4. Compromise:**
- One-night ceremony instead of 8 nights displayed
- Different location
- Scaled-down version

**5. Corporate Precedent:**
- Show list of other Simon malls with menorahs
- Demonstrate this is standard practice
- Corporate consistency argument

**6. Long Game:**
- Stand firm initially
- Relationships evolve
- Even opponents often come around

## Important Warnings

### For New Shluchim

**Do NOT:**
- Apply one Shliach's approach to your situation
- Assume what worked in LA works in small town
- Start machlokes without community support
- Act like Rabbi Cunin unless the Rebbe told YOU to

**DO:**
- Assess your specific situation
- Consider your vulnerability
- Build support first
- Consult with experienced Shluchim
- Consider Rebbe's guidance on avoiding machlokes
- Be strategic, not just bold

---

### The Nuance

**Two Truths Simultaneously:**

1. **The Rebbe values public menorahs** and defended Chabad's right to them
2. **The Rebbe doesn't want machlokes** and told Shluchim to avoid fights

**Resolution:**
Work strategically, build support, choose battles wisely, play the long game.

---

## Practical Decision Framework

### Before Taking Strong Stand, Ask:

**1. Community Assessment:**
- Do I have any community support?
- How long have I been here?
- Are there balebatim backing me?

**2. Opposition Assessment:**
- Is this just one rabbi or communal consensus?
- Can I build relationships?
- Is compromise possible?

**3. Strategic Assessment:**
- Will this fight help or hurt long-term goals?
- Do I have resources to win?
- What's my fallback position?

**4. Guidance Assessment:**
- What would Rabbi Yudel Krinsky say?
- What did the Rebbe say in similar situations?
- What do experienced Shluchim advise?

## Final Thoughts

The forum shows a spectrum of approaches, from aggressive to cautious, all from experienced Shluchim. The common wisdom:

**Key Principles:**
1. **There is no one-size-fits-all approach**
2. **New Shluchim in small towns: Be extra cautious**
3. **The Rebbe values menorahs AND avoiding machlokes**
4. **Build support before taking stands**
5. **Relationships often improve over time**
6. **Corporate precedent can be powerful**
7. **Media pressure can work but has risks**
8. **Sometimes compromises lead to bigger wins later**

Most importantly: **Get guidance from experienced Shluchim who know YOUR situation, not just general advice from the forum.**

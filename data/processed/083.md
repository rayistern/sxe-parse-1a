# [Misc > For Writer > _5 Chanukah > General] Press Release Template: Borough Approves Public Menorah

## Sample Press Release for Municipal Approval

**Example:** Bernardsville, NJ - Borough Council approval

**Tone:** Professional, celebratory, community-focused

**Format:** Standard press release structure

---

## The Press Release

### Header

```
PRESS RELEASE
```

---

### Headline

```
Light Up Your Life:
CHANUKAH IN BERNARDSVILLE CENTER
```

**Elements:**
- Catchy phrase ("Light Up Your Life")
- Location (Bernardsville Center)
- Holiday name (Chanukah)
- Active, inviting

---

### Dateline and Opening Paragraph

```
[Bernardsville] - Bernardsville's Chanukah celebrations will receive a significant boost this year as a nine-foot Menorah is erected in the center of the town's Olcott Square. A special celebration - with local dignitaries, including Mayor Hugh Fenwick, participating - will be held at the Menorah on Tuesday December 3rd at 6:30 p.m.
```

**Key Elements:**

**The What:**
- Nine-foot menorah
- Erected in Olcott Square (town center)
- "Significant boost" to celebrations

**The When:**
- Tuesday, December 3rd
- 6:30 p.m.
- Specific date and time

**The Who:**
- Local dignitaries participating
- **Mayor Hugh Fenwick** named specifically
- Public invited (implied)

---

### Approval Paragraph

```
Bernardsville's Borough Council approved the request, put forth by longtime local resident Sid Sussman and Chabad of Greater Somerset County, at its meeting on Monday evening, December 7th.
```

**Key Elements:**

**The Approval:**
- Borough Council approved
- Official government action
- Establishes legitimacy

**The Requestors:**
- **Longtime local resident** (not just outsider rabbi)
- Plus Chabad organization
- Partnership model

**The When:**
- Monday evening, December 7th
- Recent approval
- Fresh news

---

### Rabbi Quote

```
"Chanukah's a time for celebrating the freedom to openly celebrate our values; this Menorah proclaims that message to the world", said Rabbi Mendel Herson, Director of the Basking Ridge based Chabad, "we appreciate the town's eagerness to bring a comprehensive Holiday spirit to the town. They displayed a marvelous sense of leadership and understanding."
```

**Quote Analysis:**

**First Part - The Message:**
- "Freedom to openly celebrate our values"
- **Universal theme** (not just Jewish)
- "Proclaims that message to the world"
- Bigger than one community

**Second Part - Gratitude:**
- "Appreciate the town's eagerness"
- "Comprehensive Holiday spirit"
- "Marvelous sense of leadership and understanding"
- **Praises the officials** (they love this)

**Tone:**
- Grateful, not entitled
- Emphasizes universal values
- Makes officials look good
- Inclusive language

---

### Event Details

```
Light refreshments and chocolate Chanukah 'gelt' will be available on Tuesday evening. The entire community is invited for this momentous event. For more information please call Rabbi Yossi Lazaroff at 908-604-8844
```

**Elements:**

**Refreshments:**
- Light refreshments (food!)
- Chocolate Chanukah gelt (specific, traditional)
- Free (implied)

**The Invitation:**
- "Entire community invited"
- "Momentous event"
- Open to all

**Contact:**
- Rabbi Yossi Lazaroff
- Phone: 908-604-8844
- Clear point of contact

---

### Media Notice

```
Media Notice: the event is open to all reporters, journalists and photographers; your coverage is invited. Rabbi Mendel Herson will be on hand to answer questions.
```

**Purpose:**
- Explicitly invites media
- Makes coverage easy
- Offers spokesperson
- Welcomes questions

**Why Include:**
- Reporters may not assume they're invited
- Makes it clear: this is newsworthy
- Available spokesperson identified
- Professional media relations

## Why This Press Release Works

### Structure Analysis

**Inverted Pyramid:**
1. Most important: what, when, where, who
2. Background: approval, partnership
3. Quote: adds human element, praises officials
4. Details: food, contact, media

**If Editor Cuts:**
- First paragraph stands alone
- Has all essential information
- Rest adds color and detail

---

### Strategic Elements

**1. Local Partnership**
> "Longtime local resident Sid Sussman and Chabad..."

**Why This Matters:**
- Not just "outside rabbi"
- Community member requested it
- Chabad partnered with local
- More legitimate, less "them vs. us"

**Lesson:**
- Find local partner when possible
- Jewish community member
- Prominent resident
- Makes it community initiative

---

**2. Official Endorsement**
> "Borough Council approved..."

**Why This Matters:**
- Government blessing
- Legitimate, not controversial
- Official action, not fringe activity
- Safe for others to support

**Lesson:**
- Lead with approval
- Shows government support
- Makes participation comfortable
- Establishes credibility

---

**3. Mayor by Name**
> "Including Mayor Hugh Fenwick, participating"

**Why This Matters:**
- Name recognition
- Adds importance
- Personal connection
- Mayor's office will likely promote

**Lesson:**
- Name officials specifically
- Gives them credit
- They'll share/promote
- Personal stake in success

---

**4. Universal Framing**
> "Freedom to openly celebrate our values"

**Why This Matters:**
- Not "Jewish values" but "our values"
- Freedom theme (American)
- Inclusive language
- Transcends single faith

**Lesson:**
- Universal language
- Freedom, celebration, community
- Not sectarian
- Everyone can relate

---

**5. Praising Officials**
> "Marvelous sense of leadership and understanding"

**Why This Matters:**
- Officials love being praised
- Reinforces their decision
- Harder to reverse
- Encourages future support

**Lesson:**
- Thank profusely
- Make them heroes
- Public recognition
- Builds relationship

## Distribution Strategy

**Local Media:**

- Local radio stations

### When to Send

**Timing:**
- Immediately after approval
- 1-2 weeks before event
- Not too early (people forget)
- Not too late (can't plan)
- Enough time for media to schedule

**Example:**
- Approval: December 7
- Event: December 3 (wait, that's backwards in the example!)
- Send: Shortly after approval

**Note:** The example has date inconsistency (approval Dec 7, event Dec 3), but principle is clear

## Variations for Different Situations

### First-Time Approval

**Add:**
> "This is the first time [town name] will host a public menorah display, joining hundreds of cities nationwide in celebrating religious diversity during the holiday season."

**Why:**
- Emphasizes historic nature
- Shows nationwide context
- Makes it newsworthy

---

### Annual Event

**Add:**
> "This marks the [X]th year that [town name] has hosted the public menorah lighting, which has become a beloved community tradition."

**Why:**
- Shows staying power
- Tradition angle
- Community acceptance

---

### After Initial Resistance

**Soften or Omit:**
- Don't mention previous denials
- Don't reference opposition
- Focus on current approval
- Positive only

**Why:**
- No need to remind of controversy
- Officials prefer positive framing
- Past is past
- Look forward

---

### Multiple Sponsors

**Add All Partners:**
> "Approved request put forth by [Organization 1], [Organization 2], and Chabad of [Area]..."

**Why:**
- Share credit
- Broader coalition
- More community buy-in
- Others will promote too

**Bottom Line:**
This Bernardsville press release exemplifies effective municipal menorah announcement: lead with government approval (Borough Council voted yes) and official participation (Mayor Hugh Fenwick by name), emphasize local partnership (longtime resident Sid Sussman plus Chabad—not just outside rabbi), use universal framing ("freedom to openly celebrate our values"), and lavish praise on officials ("marvelous sense of leadership and understanding"). Structure follows inverted pyramid (key facts first, details later), explicitly invites media coverage, and keeps tone celebratory rather than controversial. Key strategies: name officials specifically (they'll promote it), partner with local resident (community legitimacy), frame as "comprehensive Holiday spirit" (not sectarian), provide all logistics (date, time, location, contact), and make coverage easy for journalists. The phrase "freedom to openly celebrate our values" is masterful—simultaneously Jewish and universal, religious and American. Adapt this template by filling in your town, officials, dates, and partners, but keep the structure and tone: government blessing, official participation, universal values, grateful not entitled, and invitation to all.

# [Misc > For Writer > _5 Chanukah > General] Dignitary Email Example

## Brief Email Template for Inviting Officials

**Forum Source:** http://www.shluchimexchange.com/en/forum/viewtopic.php?t=542984

**Format:** Actual email sent and confirmed successful

---

## The Email

### Subject Line
*(Not specified in document, suggest: "Invitation: Public Menorah Lighting with Mayor Breen")*

---

### Email Body

> "Hi Mr. X,
>
> I hope this e-mail finds you well.
>
> I'm rabbi Chanan Chernitsky, director of Chabad of Newfoundland, based in St. John's.
>
> In celebration of the Jewish holiday of Chanukah we will once again be having a public Menorah lighting ceremony at Bannerman park on Sunday December 22nd at 4 PM.
>
> Mayor Breen has confirmed his attendance and will be speaking as well.
>
> We would be honoured if you could please join us for the event.
>
> All the best,
>
> Rabbi Chernitsky"

---

## Why This Email Works

### Structure Analysis

**1. Friendly Opening**
- "Hi Mr. X" (appropriate formality)
- "I hope this e-mail finds you well" (warm, professional)

**2. Self-Introduction**
- Name + title
- Organization
- Location
- Establishes credibility quickly

**3. The Event**
- "Once again" (implies tradition/track record)
- Specific holiday context
- Clear location + date + time
- Public ceremony (open to all)

**4. The Hook**
- **"Mayor Breen has confirmed his attendance and will be speaking"**
- Creates social proof
- Implies importance/legitimacy
- Makes it appealing to attend

**5. The Ask**
- Simple, clear invitation
- "Honoured" (respectful)
- "Please join us" (direct request)

**6. Friendly Closing**
- "All the best" (warm)
- Just first name + title

---

### Length
**Perfect:** Short enough to read in 30 seconds, complete enough to respond

---

## Key Success Factors

### 1. Social Proof

**The Power of "Mayor Confirmed":**
- If mayor is attending, event must be significant
- Creates FOMO (fear of missing out)
- Shows bipartisan/universal appeal
- Makes saying "yes" easier (not alone)

**Order Matters:**
- Secure one big name first
- Use that to attract others
- Each confirmation makes next easier

---

### 2. Specificity

**All Key Details Included:**
- ✓ What: Public Menorah lighting
- ✓ When: Sunday December 22nd at 4 PM
- ✓ Where: Bannerman park
- ✓ Why: Chanukah celebration
- ✓ Who else: Mayor Breen

**No Need to Ask Questions:**
Dignitary can say yes immediately without requesting more info

---

### 3. Tone

**Balance Achieved:**
- Professional but not stiff
- Respectful but not obsequious
- Friendly but not overly familiar
- Clear but not demanding

---

### 4. Low Pressure

**Soft Ask:**
- "We would be honoured if you could please join us"
- Not "We need you" or "You must"
- Easy to decline gracefully if needed
- But clear invitation nonetheless

---

## Template for Your Use

### Basic Structure

```
Subject: Invitation: [Event Name] on [Date]

Hi [Title] [Last Name],

I hope this email finds you well.

I'm [Your Name], [Your Title] of [Your Organization], based in [Location].

In celebration of [holiday/occasion], we will [once again/for the first time] be hosting a [event description] at [location] on [day, date] at [time].

[Notable person] has confirmed [his/her] attendance and will be [speaking/lighting/participating].

We would be honored if you could please join us for the event [and offer brief remarks/light the menorah/etc.].

All the best,
[Your First Name]
[Your Title]
[Organization]
[Phone]
```

## When To Send

### Timing Strategy

**Initial Outreach:**
- 6-8 weeks before event
- Gives time for calendar coordination
- Shows professional planning

**Follow-Up (if no response):**
- 1 week after initial email
- Brief: "Just following up on invitation below..."
- 2-3 weeks before event

**Final Reminder (if confirmed):**
- 1 week before
- Include parking/logistics
- Reconfirm timing

---

## Additional Tips

### Before You Send

**Secure First "Name":**
- Don't send to everyone simultaneously
- Get one key person confirmed
- Then use that as leverage for others
- "Mayor already confirmed" is powerful

**Research:**
- Know who you're inviting
- Correct title and spelling
- Their connection to community
- What they care about

---

### Subject Line Options

**Simple:**
- "Invitation: Public Menorah Lighting Dec 22"
- "Chanukah Celebration - Your Attendance Requested"

**With Hook:**
- "Join Mayor Breen at Our Chanukah Celebration"
- "Public Menorah Lighting - Mayor Confirmed"

**Formal:**
- "Formal Invitation: Annual Chanukah Ceremony"
- "Request for Your Participation: Chanukah Event"

---

### Follow-Up Responses

**If They Say Yes:**
```
Thank you so much! We're honored you'll join us. I'll send details closer to the date. Please let me know if you'd like to offer brief remarks (2-3 minutes). Looking forward to seeing you there!
```

**If They Say No:**
```
Thank you for considering it. We understand scheduling conflicts. We'd love to have you at a future event. Wishing you a wonderful holiday season!
```

**If No Response:**
*(After 1 week)*
```
Hi [Name], just following up on my email below regarding our Menorah lighting on Dec 22. Would love to have you join us along with Mayor Breen. Let me know if you need any additional information. Thanks!
```

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

**Don't:**
- ✗ Send long, detailed email (save for follow-up)
- ✗ Make multiple requests in one email
- ✗ Use overly formal or stiff language
- ✗ Forget to mention other dignitaries
- ✗ Be vague about date/time/location
- ✗ Send without proofreading
- ✗ Forget contact information
- ✗ Make them ask for details

**Do:**
- ✓ Keep it brief
- ✓ One clear ask
- ✓ Conversational but professional
- ✓ Name-drop confirmed attendees
- ✓ Include all key info
- ✓ Proofread carefully
- ✓ Sign with full contact info
- ✓ Provide everything needed to say yes

---

**Bottom Line:**
This email succeeds through brevity, clarity, and strategic use of social proof. The key phrase "Mayor Breen has confirmed his attendance" transforms it from a request into an opportunity - the dignitary isn't just being asked to attend, they're being invited to join other community leaders at a significant event. Keep the tone warm but professional, include all necessary details, and make saying "yes" as easy as possible. The entire email can be read in 30 seconds, yet provides everything needed for a decision. That's the sweet spot for busy officials.

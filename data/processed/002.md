## Full Response Article by Shlomo Uminer (Palm City, FL)

*Posted: Mon Dec 22, 2008 21:02 PM ET*

> Here is an article that I published in the paper in response to "minor holiday" and "not public." I chose not to address the minor part. Maybe it will help.

### Jewish Identity and Public Expression

As we approach the holiday of Chanukah, the Festival of Lights, let us reflect upon the timely message of the Menorah, the Holiday's primary symbol: **Jewish identity and public expression thereof.**

#### The Public Menorah

Jewish law calls for the Menorah to be placed at the outside entrance of one's home or by the window facing the street.

**Source:** The Talmud and codifiers of Jewish law set the time to kindle the menorah after nightfall and "before the streets are common to be empty of passersby."

Thus, kindling the menorah in public view is not only a preferred component but rather **an essential element** in the Mitzvah of lighting the Menorah.

**Personal Memory:** During my rabbinical training in Israel, I remember the most warming experience of the sights of families kindling the menorah on the courtyard or porch outside their homes.

#### Why Public?

Why the emphasis on the public visibility of the Menorah and sharing the beauty and richness of its lights with others? Why not just in the tranquility and privacy of one's home?

A review of the history of the Chanukah holiday and its story will shed light in understanding how this tradition corresponds to Chanukah's central theme.

---

### The Historical Context

#### The Syrian-Greek Persecution (2100+ Years Ago)

The Jewish people residing in the land of Israel under the rule of the Syrian Greeks were banned from practicing their religion.

**King Antiochus IV's Decrees:**
- Looking to achieve total religious uniformity
- Decreed the death penalty for any Jew abiding by Torah laws and customs
- Desecrated the Temple
- Placed an idol of Zeus on the holy altar
- Forced Jews to bow before it under penalty of death

**Two Paths:**
1. Teachers of the Torah were tortured and killed publicly for trying to perpetuate the forbidden Jewish religion
2. Those willing to abandon the Jewish faith could continue living their lives unimpeded; many were even awarded positions in the government

**The Greeks' Goal:** Not seeking the Jews' physical annihilation but spiritual destruction, denying their freedom to practice their religion.

#### The Maccabees' Victory

It was only through the bravery and devotion of Mattityahu, his five sons (the Maccabees), and those who joined them, who would not yield on their convictions and fought to keep their religion, that the spirit of freedom prevailed over swords of oppression, and the beleaguered Jewish nation defeated the mighty Syrian Greek Empire.

#### The Miracle

Victorious, the Maccabees returned to the holy Temple in Jerusalem to clean and rededicate it. The lights of the Menorah were once again kindled, with the one remaining pure jug containing enough oil to last one day miraculously burnt for eight days, when fresh oil became available to replenish it.

---

### The Universal Message

The menorah commemorating the victory of the Jews from oppression and its symbol of Jewish pride and identity as a nation free once more also bears the **universal message of religious freedom for people of all faiths**.

The ability to celebrate the holiday of Chanukah and to kindle a Menorah publicly in freedom reflects the victory the Maccabees fought so fiercely for.

#### Historical Perspective

This freedom of expression was not always enjoyed. As the light of their freedom dimmed again and Jews were forced to live in lands of bigotry and persecution, the public Menorah lights, cowering from the dangers of religious hatred, were forced into the privacy of the home.

---

### Freedom in America

Now, in a land that vigorously protects the right of every man to practice his or her religion free from all forms of restraint and hatred, we are once again lighting the menorah publicly and freely.

#### America's Founding Principles

Many of America's first settlers and pioneers were fleeing from religious oppression in their homeland. Men and women endured danger and hardship to find the freedom to practice their religion in peace.

**First Amendment:**
> "Congress shall make no law respecting the establishment of religion, or prohibiting the free exercise thereof."

This first amendment to the Constitution:
- Guarantees individuals the right to practice their religion without any fear
- Prevents the government from favoring or demanding the observance of any particular religion

#### Public Celebrations Today

This expression of freedom is warmly embraced as governors and mayors join dignitaries across the country. In city after city, Jews and non-Jews gather in the spirit of common fellowship to light this beacon of freedom in public squares, thereby recognizing the great contribution of the various thriving ethnic cultures which have enriched American culture and life.

**To the Jewish Population:** These Menorahs are a source of great inspiration, invoking in them a renewed sense of pride and identity with the Jewish Nation.

#### Presidential Recognition

**Historic Moment (First Night of Chanukah, 1979):**
I recall as a child when the President of the United States stepped out of the White House, walked across the street to Lafayette Park, and in the glare of national television personally joined in the kindling celebration of a giant 28-foot Menorah.

# Matzah Gift Letter Template - Shmurah Matzah Distribution

**Type**: Sample Letter/Template
**Purpose**: Accompany shmurah matzah gift packages sent to community members
**Tone**: Educational, warm, mitzvah-focused
**Recipient**: [REDACTED-NAME] (placeholder for personalization)

## Letter Text

Dear [REDACTED-NAME],

On the threshold of the joyous Holiday of Passover, we are pleased to share with you and your family with Shmura Matzot.

Shmura Matzah is quite unique. The word "Shmura" means watched and is an apt description of this Matzah. From the moment of harvest, through the milling, sifting and packaging, continuous supervision guarantees that no water comes in contact with the grain and flour. Any such contact would cause leavening and disqualify it from use on Pesach. The Matzah is then kneaded, shaped and baked completely by hand, guaranteeing that it will never fall beyond immediate personal supervision.

The message of exodus is ever present in today's modern world, the Hebrew word for Egypt can also be translated as limitations, the flat Matzah bread represents humility, and by being humble one can lift himself from his limitations to unseen heights.

We are cognizant of the relative unavailability of Shmura Matzot to the general public, and deem it a privilege to present them to you. It's our honor and pleasure to share the spirit of the holiday with you and your family. Please enjoy them Monday nught April 10th and Tuesday night April 11th after sunset to fulfill the special Mitzvah of eating Matzah on Passover.

With best wishes for a happy, healthy and joyous Pesach to you and your entire family.

[Signature line not specified]

## Letter Analysis

### Opening

**"On the threshold of the joyous Holiday of Passover, we are pleased to share with you and your family with Shmura Matzot."**

- Sets joyful, anticipatory tone
- "Threshold" creates sense of special moment approaching
- "Share with you and your family" emphasizes community and inclusion
- Uses proper term "Shmura Matzot" (plural of Shmurah Matzah) immediately establishing what's being gifted

**Note**: Grammar issue in original ("share with you and your family with") likely should be "share with you and your family" - may want to correct in actual use

### Educational Section - What is Shmurah Matzah?

**Three-Part Explanation**:

1. **Etymology**: "The word 'Shmura' means watched and is an apt description"
   - Accessible Hebrew word definition
   - "Apt description" implies it's not just a name but accurately describes the process

2. **Production Process**: "From the moment of harvest, through the milling, sifting and packaging, continuous supervision guarantees that no water comes in contact with the grain and flour."
   - Complete timeline: harvest → milling → sifting → packaging
   - Key requirement: continuous supervision
   - Critical standard: no water contact (prevents leavening)
   - Consequence: "Any such contact would cause leavening and disqualify it from use on Pesach"

3. **Handmade Element**: "The Matzah is then kneaded, shaped and baked completely by hand, guaranteeing that it will never fall beyond immediate personal supervision."
   - Entirely manual process
   - Rationale: ensures constant personal oversight
   - Contrast to machine-made (implied, not stated negatively)

### Spiritual/Philosophical Message

**Modern Relevance**:
"The message of exodus is ever present in today's modern world, the Hebrew word for Egypt can also be translated as limitations, the flat Matzah bread represents humility, and by being humble one can lift himself from his limitations to unseen heights."

**Three Connected Ideas**:

1. **Exodus is Contemporary**: Not ancient history but relevant today
2. **Egypt = Mitzrayim = Limitations**: Classic Chassidic interpretation making Exodus personally applicable
3. **Matzah = Humility**: Flat bread (no rising/ego) → Humility → Transcending limitations

**Pedagogy**: Transforms ritual food into spiritual practice with contemporary application

### Positioning the Gift

**Addressing Availability**:
"We are cognizant of the relative unavailability of Shmura Matzot to the general public, and deem it a privilege to present them to you."

- Acknowledges shmurah matzah isn't commonly available in regular stores
- "Cognizant" = we understand your challenge in obtaining this
- "Deem it a privilege" = elevates the giving as honor, not charity
- Creates value: This is special/hard to get, and we're providing it

**Framing as Sharing**:
"It's our honor and pleasure to share the spirit of the holiday with you and your family."

- "Honor and pleasure" = reciprocal gift, not one-way charity
- "Share the spirit" = not just physical matzah but spiritual experience
- Maintains dignity of recipients

### Specific Usage Instructions

**Date-Specific Guidance**:
"Please enjoy them Monday nught April 10th and Tuesday night April 11th after sunset to fulfill the special Mitzvah of eating Matzah on Passover."

**Elements**:
- **When**: Specific dates and times (Monday night April 10th, Tuesday night April 11th)
- **Timing**: "After sunset" = precise halachic requirement
- **Purpose**: "To fulfill the special Mitzvah of eating Matzah on Passover"
- **Verb**: "Enjoy" = makes mitzvah pleasurable, not burdensome

**Note**: "nught" appears to be typo for "night" in original

**Customization Needed**: Dates must be updated annually for current year's Pesach

### Closing

**"With best wishes for a happy, healthy and joyous Pesach to you and your entire family."**

- Triple blessing: happy, healthy, joyous
- "Entire family" = inclusive, multigenerational
- Uses "Pesach" (Hebrew) assuming some Jewish literacy, though letter otherwise uses "Passover" (English)

## For Shluchim: Implementation and Customization

### Personalization Requirements

**Essential Updates**:

**Mail Merge Capability**:
- Letter structure allows easy mail merge for 500+ recipients
- Keep greeting format consistent: "Dear [First Name]," for warmth
- Consider "Dear [First Name] and [Spouse Name]," if both names known

### Tone and Messaging Strengths

**Educational Without Condescending**:
- Explains "Shmura" meaning without assuming ignorance
- Provides enough detail to appreciate the gift without overwhelming

**Accessible Spirituality**:
- Egypt/Mitzrayim/limitations teaching is accessible Chassidic concept
- Humility → transcendence is universal message beyond denominational lines

**Gift Framing**:
- "Honor and pleasure" + "privilege" language prevents charity stigma
- Positions Chabad as giving something valuable/rare
- Creates gratitude rather than obligation

### Areas for Enhancement

**Grammatical Corrections Needed**:
- "share with you and your family with" → "share with you and your family"
- "nught" → "night"

**Potential Additions**:
- Quantity specification: "Enclosed you will find [2 or 6] hand-made Shmurah Matzos"
- Storage instructions: "Please keep in a dry place until use"
- Amount guidance: "Each person should eat at least 1 oz (approximately half of one matzah) at the Seder"
- Invitation: "If you need a place for the Seder or have Pesach questions, please contact us at [phone/email]"
- Website reference: "Learn more about Passover at [website]"

**Alternative Spiritual Messages**:
- Could emphasize matzah as "bread of faith" and "bread of healing" (Zohar)
- Could mention matzah our ancestors ate leaving Egypt (connecting to tradition)
- Could include brief story or teaching

### Production Considerations

**Printing**:
- Quality paper stock reflects gift value
- Letter-size on letterhead more formal than note card
- Consider Chabad house logo/branding at top
- Leave space for signature (handwritten or printed)

**Timing**:
- Print/stuff 2-4 weeks before Pesach depending on distribution method
- If hand-delivering, can print closer to holiday
- If mailing, factor in shipping time (see file 1170 for shipping logistics)

### Inclusion with Matzah Package

**Folding/Placement**:
- Tri-fold fits well in bubble mailer with matzah
- Place on top so visible when package opened
- Consider clear protective sleeve if matzah might shed crumbs on letter

**Additional Enclosures**:
- Pesach guide/brochure (but watch weight limits for shipping - see file 1170)
- Chabad house event calendar
- Donation envelope (subtle, optional)
- Recipe card for charoset or other Pesach dish

### Alternative Versions for Different Audiences

**For Less Affiliated**:
- Add more background on Passover story
- Explain Seder briefly
- Include invitation to community Seder more prominently

**For More Knowledgeable**:
- Can assume more Hebrew/Jewish literacy
- Could reference specific sources (Zohar, etc.)
- Might include deeper Chassidic teaching

**For Major Donors**:
- More personal handwritten note in addition to printed letter
- Perhaps reference their support: "As a valued supporter of [Chabad house], we wanted to ensure..."
- Upgrade to premium presentation

### Follow-Up Strategy

**After Pesach**:
- Thank you note/email: "We hope you enjoyed the Shmurah Matzah we sent"
- Feedback request: "Did you use it at your Seder?"
- Connection maintenance: "Looking forward to seeing you at [next event]"
- Story sharing: "We distributed [X] matzos to [Y] families this year thanks to supporters like you"

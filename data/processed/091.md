### Material Differences

**Menorah.net:**
- Material: Aluminum
- Lighter weight
- More compact
- Easier to move around

**PublicMenorahs.com:**
- Material: Steel
- Stronger construction
- Heavier
- More difficult to move
- Tiki torch compatible

> "Menorah.net is aluminum and publicmenorahs.com is steel"

---

### Feature Comparison

**Menorah.net Advantages:**
- Lighter weight
- More portable
- Compact storage
- **Menorah Controller** (automatic/manual control)
  - Can manually set number of bulbs lit
  - Auto function available
  - Set time, next bulb turns on after 24 hours
- Tiki torch conversion kit available

**PublicMenorahs.com Advantages:**
- Stronger construction
- More durable
- Tiki torch compatible (direct)
- Lower price point
- BUT: 6ft model **doesn't have individual switches** for each night
  - Can manually add new bulb each night

**Corporate Menorah:**
- **9 switches** (full individual control)
- One switch per candle + shamash
- Maximum flexibility

## Power Source Options

### Overview

**Four Main Solutions:**
1. LED/Solar (rechargeable)
2. Battery (car batteries)
3. Generator
4. Street lamps (existing infrastructure)

---

## Option 1: LED Rechargeable or Solar

### Modern Solution

**Types:**
- LED chargeable bulbs
- TomCare solar lights
- Solar generator system

**Availability:**
> "Shlichus market will be selling"

**Advantages:**
- No fuel needed
- Environmentally friendly
- "Green menorah" marketing angle
- No noise
- Low maintenance

**Marketing:**
> "A solar generator is also cool and you can promote that is a green Menorah"

**Pitch:**
> "It's an expense tho, but if you have a green baalhabotim can be a good pitch :)"

**Considerations:**
- Initial expense higher
- Solar requires sun exposure
- Rechargeable needs daily charging
- Weather dependent (solar)

---

## Option 2: Car Batteries

### Popular Choice

**Why Car Batteries:**
> "Car batteries sound the most shayech"

**Requirements:**

**A. Low-Watt LED Bulbs**
- Get from Walmart
- 4W LED bulbs available
- Essential for battery life

**B. Voltage Conversion**
- Car batteries: 12V
- Most menorahs: 120V
- **Need an inverter**

**C. Professional Installation**
> "Get an electrician to do it"

**Electrical Work Needed:**
- Merge cables
- Ensure outdoor-safe
- Proper weatherproofing
- Safety considerations

**Maintenance:**
> "You might need to switch out the battery a few times over Chanukah"

**Planning:**
- Multiple batteries
- Charging rotation
- Replacement schedule
- Monitoring system

---

## Option 3: Generator

### Reliable But Noisy

**Advantages:**
> "A generator is heavy but it is worth it's weight in gold in some places"

---

### Sizing the Generator

**Calculate Wattage:**
- 9 bulbs at 75 watts each = 675 watts
- Add any spotlights
- Get generator with **significantly more capacity**
- Advertised number = peak, not idle
- Build in safety margin

**Example:**
- 675 watts for bulbs
- 200 watts for spotlights
- Total: 875 watts
- **Get 1500-2000 watt generator** (safety margin)

---

### Noise Management

**The Problem:**
> "It makes a lot of noise"

**Solution:**
- Place far from event area
- Use long extension cord
- At least during actual ceremony
- Distance reduces noise

**Alternative:**
> "You can get electric generators that are almost inaudible"

**Investment Worth It:**
- Quieter models more expensive
- But worth it for events
- Professional atmosphere
- Can hear speakers/music

---

### Security

**Critical:**
> "Make sure to chain it to something that won't move"

**Why:**
- Expensive equipment
- Theft target
- Public location
- Overnight exposure

**How:**
- Heavy chain
- Lock to immovable object
- Visible security
- Consider enclosure

---

### Fuel Management

**Buy Adequate Gas Cans:**
> "Buy gas cans big enough to fill it (you'll probably/certainly need to refill each night)"

**Planning:**
- Check tank capacity
- Calculate burn rate
- Buy sufficient cans
- Know nearby gas stations
- Schedule refueling
- Never let it run out

**Storage:**
- Safe fuel storage
- Away from flames
- Proper containers
- Fire code compliance

---

## Option 4: Street Lamps

### Hidden Power Source

**Surprising Discovery:**
> "In several places I've been there have been outlets in the street lamps, either exposed, or under a screwed panel."

**Where to Look:**
- Street lamp bases
- Exposed outlets
- Screwed panels
- Maintenance access points

---

### How to Use

**The Approach:**
> "I don't know if this will help you, but just putting it out there, look around to see if it's there."

**If You Find It:**
> "If you see it and use it, usually no one will ask questions"

**If Under Panel:**
- Someone working there nearby
- Ask with "breitkeit" (casualness)
- "Are you the one who knows how to open this?"
- "We need to use it for our menorah display"
- "Uchedomeh" (and so on - friendly approach)

---

### Legitimacy

**When on Public Property:**
- City/county menorah
- Public display
- Minimal electricity use
- Community benefit
- Usually okay

**Best Practice:**
- Ask permission if possible
- Know who to contact
- Have approval in writing
- Offer to pay minimal fee

## Power Source Decision Matrix

### Choose Based On:

**For Portable/Temporary:**
- Battery or solar (lightweight)
- Move between locations
- No infrastructure

**For Established Location:**
- Generator (reliable)
- Can secure properly
- Don't mind noise
- Or find street lamp outlet

**For Professional Events:**
- Quiet generator
- Or street lamp if available
- Battery backup
- Professional appearance

**For "Green" Marketing:**
- Solar generator
- Environmentally friendly
- Appeals to certain donors
- Modern image

**For Budget-Conscious:**
- Car batteries + inverter
- Street lamp (if available)
- Initial investment lower
- DIY approach

## Pro Tips from Forum

### Individual Switches Matter

**If No Individual Switches:**
> "The 6ft from public menorahs doesn't have individual switches for each night...You could have someone add a new bulb each night if you want though"

**Considerations:**
- Manual bulb changes
- Access to menorah
- Daily maintenance
- Consistency challenge

**Alternative:**
- Get model with switches
- Or automated controller
- Worth extra cost if remote location

---

### Weight vs. Strength Trade-Off

**Experience:**
- Aluminum: easier setup/takedown
- Steel: better for permanent/semi-permanent
- If moving daily: aluminum
- If staying put: steel's strength worth it

---

### Controller Technology

**Menorah.net Controller:**
- Auto-advance feature
- Set it and forget it
- 24-hour timer
- Reduces daily visits
- Worth investment for remote locations

**WiFi Smart Switches:**
> "If there is wifi available, I think Home Depot has now lights that you can turn on and off from your smart phone anywhere"

**Modern Solution:**
- Remote control
- From anywhere
- Monitor status
- Troubleshoot remotely
- Multiple locations manageable

**Bottom Line:**
Four main menorah vendors serve different needs: Menorah.net offers lightweight aluminum with automated controller (double price but premium features), PublicMenorahs.com provides durable steel construction at lower cost (but 6ft model lacks individual switches), Corporate Menorah has 9 individual switches for maximum control, and Light Up Menorah specializes in aluminum holder + tiki torch combinations. Power solutions include: (1) LED rechargeable/solar for green marketing and quiet operation ("if you have green baalhabotim can be good pitch"); (2) car batteries with inverter (12V to 120V conversion, use 4W LED bulbs from Walmart, need electrician for outdoor-safe installation, swap batteries periodically); (3) generator for reliability (calculate 675W for 9x75W bulbs plus spotlights, get significantly higher capacity, chain to immovable object, buy large gas cans for nightly refills, place far away due to noise or invest in "almost inaudible" models); (4) street lamp outlets as hidden power source ("usually no one will ask questions," look for exposed or panel-covered outlets, ask worker "are you the one who knows how to open this?"). Material trade-off: aluminum lighter/more portable but steel stronger/more durable. Key features: Menorah.net controller auto-advances bulbs every 24 hours (worth it for remote locations), WiFi smart switches enable smartphone control from anywhere, individual night switches critical unless manually changing bulbs daily. Budget range $350 (DIY + car battery) to $6,100 (solar generator system). Choose based on portability needs, noise sensitivity, budget, technical capability, and environmental messaging goals.

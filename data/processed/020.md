## Police Escort Issues

### Challenge: Church and State Separation

**Question:**
"My local Police department just called me to notify me that they cannot join me (lead) the parade (it's our first time doing this) as we had previously made up. They told me that the village manager said to them that this is mixing church and state - and that they had many problems in the past in this area. Anyone ever dealt with this successfully? Preferably in NY?"

---

### Solutions

#### Solution 1: Reframe the Request

**Response:**
> "Tell them you don't need them to lead, you need them to secure the parade."

**Key Talking Points:**
- This is about public safety, not religious endorsement
- Police protect free speech and assembly
- They're securing the parade, not participating religiously
- Standard traffic control service

---

#### Solution 2: Hire Off-Duty Officers

**Response:**
> "Or hire them off duty for 35 dollars an hour."

**Note:** In some locales it's closer to $100 per hour, per police officer. Try other ideas first.

**Process:**
1. Contact police department
2. Request to hire off-duty officers
3. Coordinate directly with officers
4. Negotiate rate
5. Handle payment

---

#### Solution 3: Apply for Parade Permit

**Response:**
> "You can also apply and pay the fee for a parade permit."

**Benefits:**
- Official approval
- May include police escort as part of permit
- Legal framework
- Professional process

---

#### Solution 4: Conversation with Village Manager

**From:** [REDACTED-NAME]

> "Do they have a shmanta/holiday parade? Maybe having a sit-down conversation with the manager."

**Strategy:**
- Point out other holiday parades
- Request equal treatment
- Face-to-face meeting
- Explain community value
- Address concerns directly

### Roof-Mounted Speakers

**Forum:** http://www.shluchimexchange.com/en/forum/viewtopic.php?t=492291

**Question:**
"We are making a menorah parade and want the front car to have music from the roof. What's the easiest way to do that. What would I need to buy to make it happen?"

---

#### Option 1: YOEC Equipment Rental

**Response:**
> "YOEC Zal Shluchim should know of a place. They used to- could be still do - rent 3 portable speakers and connect an aux cord..."

---

#### Option 2: Radio Shack System

**Response:**
> "Buy Radio Shack speakers horn and amplifier system with aux to your phone music whatever. Amplifier sits in car obviously while horn tied well on roof or via window being shut. You will need extra wire to connect from speaker to amp while aux cable goes into music and amp. Sound is great I use it year after year and good quality."

**Components:**
- Speaker horn (for roof)
- Amplifier (in car)
- Aux cable (connects phone/music to amplifier)
- Extra wire (connects speaker to amplifier)
- Mounting system (tie to roof or secure via window)

**Advantages:**
- Reusable year after year
- Good quality sound
- Relatively affordable

**Considerations:**
- Music isn't heard after several cars

---

#### Option 3: Pickup Truck with Generator and Powerful Speakers

**Response:**
> "We did that for a year or two but music isn't heard after several cars. We now rent a pickup truck and rent a generator with powerful speakers to go in the back of the truck. Costs about $300 for everything."

**Setup:**
- Rent pickup truck
- Rent generator
- Rent powerful speakers
- Set up in truck bed

**Cost:** About $300 total

**Advantages:**
- Much more powerful
- Heard throughout parade
- Professional sound
- Mobile DJ setup

**Best Use:**
- Larger parades
- When budget allows
- Maximum sound coverage

## Fire Truck Menorah Parade

### Detailed How-To Guide

**Interview with:** Rabbi [REDACTED-NAME], Gaithersburg, Maryland
**Contact:** [REDACTED-PHONE], [REDACTED-EMAIL]

---

### Background

**Origin Story:**
> "Our local fire departments go through the neighborhoods at the end of December giving out candy canes. There are a couple of Jewish guys in the fire department whom I know, who suggested that we do something similar in honor of Chanukah. With that, the Fire Truck Menorah Parade was born, and has been a hit ever since."

---

### Timing

**When:**
Choose one night of Chanukah for the program.

---

### Fire Trucks Setup

**Lead Truck:**
- One large car Menorah mounted on front of fire truck
- Purchased heavy duty Menorah from Rabbi Traxler in Texas
- Cost: Approximately $800
- Heavier piece, uses regular bulbs
- Far more effective for parade than standard menorah
- Mounted on front bumper (fire trucks have large bumpers)

**Additional Trucks:**
Other trucks from the station join the parade

---

### Parade Route

**Designated Stops:**
- Make stops in designated areas
- Mostly residential neighborhoods (not business areas)
- This works well for engaging community

**Notification:**
- Notify people who live in each area
- Give approximate time of arrival
- Build anticipation

**Timing:**
- About 2 hours with fire trucks
- Plan timing and stops carefully
- Usually make approximately 6 stops

**Special Stop:**
- One stop at location of Chanukah Wonderland
- Works well for cold weather areas
- People can stay indoors until parade arrives

---

### Music

**Critical Element:**
> "Loud music is important for this parade as it gives it energy."

**Setup:**
- DJ with large speakers in minivan (NOT car speakers)
- **OR** Loudspeaker mounted atop a car

**Purpose:**
- Creates energy
- Announces arrival
- Festive atmosphere

---

### At Each Stop

**Arrival:**
- Fire trucks with lights blazing
- Music blasting

**Giveaways:**
- Chocolate Chanukah gelt for all kids
- Dreidels

**Mini Program:**
- Bochurim juggling fire
- Fire juggling works especially well (dark outside)
- Challenging for many other types of entertainment at this hour

**Optional:**
- Someone singing Chanukah songs
- Music track playing for live singer

---

### Coordination

**Designated Coordinator:**
- One coordinator at each stop
- In contact with parade
- Ensures readiness

**Advance Car:**
- Arrives few minutes before parade
- Gets people ready for arrival
- Ensures crowd is gathered

---

### Finale

**Location:** Chabad House

**Program:**
- Public lighting of large Menorah outside Chabad House
- Politician present for lighting (if possible)
- Food after the lighting
- Optional: Entertainer performance

**Note:**
Most people who come out for parade's stop in their area will not attend the finale.

---

### Alternative Options

**For Communities Without Fire Trucks:**

> "I only have 2 hours on one night with the fire trucks and can only get to a limited number of stops. Technically, you could line up car menorahs with music, singing, and a juggling act and do the same. It wouldn't be the same as with the fire trucks, but for a community that has never done it before, they wouldn't know the difference."

**Equivalent Setup:**
- Car menorahs (instead of fire truck menorah)
- Music system
- Singing
- Juggling act or entertainment
- Same stop format

**Advantage:**
For first-time event, creates same impact and excitement.

## Summary of Best Practices

### Essential Elements

**1. Permits:**
- Get official parade permit when possible
- Or frame as police-escorted motorcade
- Legal compliance and safety

**2. Police Escort:**
- Critical for keeping parade together
- Can be hired off-duty if necessary
- Frame as security, not religious participation

**3. Visual Impact:**
- Decorate every car (magnets, flags, balloons)
- Fire truck if possible
- Giant menorahs on pickup trucks
- Inflatable dreidels

**4. Music:**
- FM transmitter for synchronized music (best option)
- Or powerful speakers on truck
- Coordination is key

**5. Entertainment:**
- Fire juggling at stops
- Treats for children
- Singing and activities
- Make it memorable

**6. Finale:**
- Grand menorah lighting
- Politician or community leader
- Food and celebration
- Chabad House location

## Final Thoughts

Car menorah parades create tremendous pirsumei nisa and community excitement. Key success factors:

1. **Legal Framework** - Permits and police coordination
2. **Visual Impact** - Every car decorated, fire trucks, inflatables
3. **Audio** - Synchronized music via FM transmitter
4. **Entertainment** - Stops with activities, treats, engagement
5. **Professional Execution** - Planning, coordination, follow-through

The investment in equipment and planning pays off in:
- Community participation
- Media coverage
- Children's excitement
- Jewish pride
- Annual tradition
- Pirsumei nisa

With proper preparation and attention to these details, your car menorah parade will become a highlight of Chanukah in your community!

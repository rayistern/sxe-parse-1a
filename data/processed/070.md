# [Misc > For Writer > _5 Chanukah > General] Formal Letter Template to Mayor for Menorah Lighting

## Complete Template for Municipal Officials

**Format:** Formal letter requesting dignitary participation and proclamation

**Use:** First-time or official invitation to mayor and city council

---

## The Template

### Letterhead/Header

```
[Your Organization Letterhead]

[Date]

The Honorable [Name], Mayor
Honorable City Council members
[Street Address]
[City, State ZIP]
```

---

### Salutation

```
Honorable Mayor and Honorable City Council members:
```

**Note:** Formal address for both mayor and council

---

### Opening Paragraph: The Invitation

```
I would like to take this opportunity to formally invite you to the upcoming public Chanukah Menorah Kindling Ceremony, a project of [mosad name here].
```

**Key Elements:**
- "Formally invite" (official tone)
- "Upcoming" (creates anticipation)
- "Public" (open to all, civic nature)
- "Project of [organization]" (establishes ownership)

---

### Event Details Paragraph

```
This year, we will be holding our Kindling Ceremony at [location, date, and time]. The event will feature the kindling of the city's largest Menorah as well as music, dancing, latkes, gifts, and non-sectarian holiday greetings. We are projecting a crowd of [number] people at the program, as this is the largest public celebration in the area. Several local news teams are expected to cover this unique event.
```

**Include:**
- **Location** (specific venue)
- **Date** (full date)
- **Time** (exact time)
- **Features:**
  - Menorah (superlative: "city's largest")
  - Music
  - Dancing
  - Latkes (food)
  - Gifts
  - "Non-sectarian holiday greetings" (key phrase!)
- **Projected attendance** (shows significance)
- **Superlative:** "largest public celebration in area"
- **Media coverage** expected (photo op!)

---

### The Philosophy/Request Paragraph

```
As the Chanukah holiday is a celebration of religious freedom, we feel it would be appropriate for the elected representatives of our great country to participate in its celebration. It would be an honor if you would join us and give your greetings at this joyous event.
```

**Key Themes:**
- **Religious freedom** (universal American value)
- **Appropriate** for elected officials
- **"Our great country"** (patriotic)
- **Honor** (respectful request)
- **"Give your greetings"** (clear ask)
- **"Joyous event"** (positive framing)

---

### Proclamation Request Paragraph

```
In addition, we respectfully request a proclamation from the city of [name] attesting to the significance of the celebration and the universal message of the Chanukah lights. Enclosed please find a proposed text for such a proclamation. If you have any questions or confirmation of appearances, please call us at [number here].
```

**Elements:**
- **"Respectfully request"** (polite)
- **Proclamation** (official recognition)
- **"Universal message"** (not just for Jews)
- **Proposed text enclosed** (make it easy!)
- **Clear contact info**

---

### Closing

```
Looking forward to working with you in the future, I remain,

Respectfully yours,

Rabbi Chabad Lubavitcher
[Title]
[Organization]
[Contact Information]
```

**Note:**
- "Looking forward to working... in the future" (ongoing relationship)
- "Respectfully yours" (formal but warm)
- Include full title and contact info

## Why This Template Works

### Formal Tone Appropriate For:

**Official Requests:**
- First-time invitation
- Proclamation request
- Going "on record"
- Creating paper trail

**Governmental Communication:**
- Addresses all council, not just mayor
- Uses official titles throughout
- Formal opening and closing
- Professional language

---

### Strategic Elements

**1. Religious Freedom Frame**
- Not just "Jewish holiday"
- American civic value
- Appropriate for government participation
- First Amendment celebration

**2. Non-Sectarian Language**
- "Non-sectarian holiday greetings"
- Shows inclusivity
- Makes participation comfortable
- Reduces church-state concerns

**3. Makes It Easy**
- All details in one letter
- Proclamation text provided
- One phone number for response
- Clear asks (attend + proclaim)

**4. Shows Significance**
- Largest menorah
- Projected crowd size
- Media coverage
- Largest celebration in area

**5. Multiple Hooks**
- Religious freedom
- Constituent engagement
- Media opportunity
- Community unity
- Photo op

## Sample Proclamation Text

### To Enclose With Letter

```
PROCLAMATION

WHEREAS, Chanukah, also known as the Festival of Lights, commemorates the rededication of the Holy Temple in Jerusalem over 2,000 years ago following the victory of a small band of Jews over the mighty Greek army; and

WHEREAS, the miracle of Chanukah occurred when a day's supply of oil miraculously burned for eight days in the Temple's Menorah, symbolizing the triumph of light over darkness and freedom over oppression; and

WHEREAS, the Chanukah Menorah has become a universal symbol of religious liberty and serves as a reminder that freedom of religion is a cornerstone of our democracy; and

WHEREAS, [Organization Name] will conduct a public Menorah lighting ceremony on [Date] at [Location] to share the light and message of Chanukah with the entire community; and

WHEREAS, this celebration promotes unity, understanding, and the shared values that strengthen our community and our nation;

NOW, THEREFORE, I, [Mayor Name], Mayor of the City of [City Name], and the City Council of [City Name], do hereby proclaim the dates of [Date Range] as

CHANUKAH

in the City of [City Name], and encourage all citizens to learn about this meaningful holiday and its timeless message of hope, freedom, and light.

IN WITNESS WHEREOF, I have hereunto set my hand and caused the Seal of the City of [City Name] to be affixed this [Day] day of [Month], [Year].

_______________________________
[Mayor Name], Mayor

ATTEST:

_______________________________
[City Clerk Name], City Clerk
```

## Timing Strategy

### When to Send

**First Letter:**
- **8 weeks before Chanukah** (initial request)
- Gives time for council meetings/approvals
- Shows professional planning

**Follow-Up Call:**
- **1 week after sending**
- "Just checking if you received letter..."
- Offer to answer questions

**Second Follow-Up:**
- **4 weeks before**
- "Following up on invitation..."
- Confirm logistics if yes

**Final Confirmation:**
- **1 week before**
- Event details
- Parking, timing, program flow

## Common Questions

### Do I Need to Send Hard Copy or Email?

**Best Approach:**
- **Initial contact:** Email (faster, easier)
- **Formal invitation:** Mail hard copy with signature
- **Follow-up:** Email or phone
- **Proclamation:** May require hard copy for official records

---

### What if They Can't Attend?

**Options:**
- Offer alternative night (if 8-day celebration)
- Ask if another official can represent
- Still request proclamation
- Invite to future event

---

### Should I Ask for Money/Sponsorship?

**NO** in this letter.

**This letter is for:**
- Participation
- Proclamation
- Civic recognition

**Separate conversation for:**
- Sponsorship
- Funding
- Donations

Keep them separate to avoid confusion about motives.

**Bottom Line:**
This formal letter template works for official first contact with mayors and city councils. It uses appropriate governmental language, frames Chanukah as celebration of religious freedom (universal American value), provides all necessary details, and makes two clear asks: attend and proclaim. The key phrase "non-sectarian holiday greetings" addresses church-state concerns, while enclosing proposed proclamation text makes approval easy. Use this for first formal contact, then follow up with more casual communication once relationship established. The proclamation becomes valuable for future invitations ("Last year Mayor X proclaimed...") and shows city's official recognition of event's significance.

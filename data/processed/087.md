## Section 1: What to Use to Light a Public Menorah

### Overview

**For Outdoor:** Tiki torches
**For Indoor:** Candle conversion kit
**Critical:** Make sure wicks are pulled out enough

---

### Best Lighting Tool

**Recommended:**
> "Best to use a Blow torch or butane torch taped to a stick or pole"

**Why:**
- Reliable ignition
- Works in wind
- Easy to control
- Can reach high

**Avoid:**
> "Very difficult to use a tiki torch to light the other candles"

**Problems with Using Tiki to Light:**
- Tiki cover gets in the way
- Tilting makes fuel spill
- Takes 60 seconds per candle
- Oil spills everywhere
- Inefficient

---

### Option 1: Tiki Torch

**Advantages:**
- Pretty powerful
- Works well in wind
- Good flame
- Traditional look

**Fuel Options:**

**1. Tiki Brand Torch Fuel**
- Available at Home Depot/Lowe's
- Standard product
- Easy to find

**2. Paraffin**
- Alternative fuel
- Bring insert to Home Depot
- Find best fit for your torch
- Works well

**Wick Configuration:**
- Only has 1 wick (big, but just 1)
- Good for menorah
- Not good for Havdalah (need multiple wicks)

**Setup:**
- Unless you have different type
- Standard tiki has single wick
- Check your specific model

---

### Option 2: Candle Conversion Kit

**Components:**
- Paraffin fuel
- Glass chimney
- Professional look

**Best For:**
- Indoor menorahs
- Klarr menorah
- When tiki conversion not available

**Alternative Method:**
> "I did tape and big candles"

**When No Kit Available:**
- Use tape
- Secure big candles
- Workable solution

## Section 6: Important Sicha About Public Lighting

### Not Yotzei with Public Menorah

**Critical Information:**
> "I saw this in a sicha today, where the Rebbe says that an announcement should be made by the public lighting, that one is not yotze!"

**Halachic Requirement:**
- Announce at public lighting
- People NOT yotzei with public menorah
- Must light at home
- Public is in addition, not replacement
- Important to clarify

**Why Announce:**
- People might think they fulfilled obligation
- Need to light at home still
- Public is pirsumei nisa (publicizing miracle)
- Personal obligation separate
- Educational moment

## Section 7: City Wants to Copy Event

### Time-Sensitive Issue

**The Situation:**
> "We are planning the first public menorah lighting in our city. We confirmed the place we will be doing it and got the Mayor's confirmation that he will be joining. Then someone from the Mayor's office contacted me and told me that the Mayor is only available on a different day and that the city likes the idea (of a public menorah lighting) so much that they are planning to do it (with our menorah in the place that we arranged) every day that we are not doing it."

**The Problem:**
- City wants to take over event
- Use your menorah
- Different days
- Mayor at their event, not yours
- Their own publicity
- Replacing your event

---

### Advice from Forum

**Response #1: Combine Efforts**
> "You might want to point out that you will bring your community and it is more effective to combine efforts for one big night"

**Plus:**
> "We in chabad are good marketers and can bring ppl that wouldn't come otherwise plus you'll make it more fun with party kids entertainment etc which they won't do"

**Argument:**
- Better to combine
- One big event better than multiple small
- You bring community
- You bring marketing
- You bring entertainment
- More successful together

---

**Response #2: Community Connection**
> "U can tell then The reason you want this to be one coordination is because in a small town it is important that people get to know other people in the Jewish community so the more the merrier"

> "It would be silly to have 2 parties for a smallish Jewish community"

**Framing:**
- Small Jewish community
- Unity important
- Meeting each other matters
- Splitting attendance silly
- Stronger together

---

**Response #3: Alternative for Mayor**
> "If the mayor cant be there on that day have him send other city representative on his behalf -it works just fine"

**Solution:**
- Deputy mayor
- City council member
- Other official
- Mayor sends greetings
- Not worth splitting event

---

**Response #4: Your Value-Add**
> "Bottom line : with you involved it will be on a much grander scale and they will look strange doing they're own thing (besides that your party will be better etc)"

> "Maybe bring another community member with u to share their perspective like yours"

**Strategy:**
- Emphasize your expertise
- City doing it alone looks strange
- Your event will be better
- Bring community member to meeting
- Show community support

**Response #5: You're Lighting Every Night Anyway**

### Synthesis: When to Fight, When to Wait

**Fight (or Push Hard) When:**
- You have community support
- Public property (stronger rights)
- Larger community
- Established presence
- Bad to back down now
- Rebbe's specific guidance to "charge ahead"

**Wait (or Build First) When:**
- New to community (6 weeks!)
- No community backing
- Private property (weaker case)
- Small community
- More to lose than gain
- Vulnerable position

**Strategy for Mall Situation:**
- Media threat (already public)

- "Too far along to stop"
- But also...
- Meet Reform rabbi personally

## Section 10: Halacha of Public Menorah

### 15 Halachic Guidelines

**Source:** Compiled halachic guidelines for public menorah lightings

---

**1. Location**
> "It is a great Mitzvah to light Chanukah lights in the thoroughfares of the city 'in the most public and central location'."

---

**2. Sheva Mitzvos Bnai Noach**
> "The occasion of the public Menorah lighting should be utilized to also highlight the Sheva Mitzvos Bnai Noach."

**Educational opportunity for universal values**

---

**3. Announcement About Personal Obligation**
> "It is important to inform those present that the public Menorah lighting does not fulfill one's personal obligation to light Chanukah lights and that it is incumbent upon each individual to light Chanukah lights at home."

**Critical announcement - must make clear**

---

**4. Bracha Despite Not Being Yotzei**
> "Nevertheless, we recite a Brocho at the public lighting, similar to the Halacha regarding the lighting of Chanukah lights in a Shul etc."

**Bracha is appropriate even though not fulfilling personal obligation**

---

**5. Tznius - Women Don't Make Bracha**
> "For reason of Tznius we do not honor a woman with making the Brocho."

**Women can light, but man makes bracha**

---

**6. Multiple Honorees**
> "A number of individuals may be honored to each kindle a Chanukah light. However, when doing so all the announcements of the names etc, should be made prior to the Brocho so as not to make a Hefsek between the Brocho and kindling of all the lights."

**Process:**
1. Announce all names first
2. Make bracha
3. Then light all candles
4. No interruption between bracha and lighting

---

**7. Non-Jews and the Shamash**
> "A non-Jew should not be honored to light a Chanukah light. Instead, many will honor a non-Jew with kindling the Shamash – which is acceptable."

**Compromise:**
- Non-Jew shouldn't light actual candles
- But can light shamash
- Honors them appropriately
- Halachically sound

---

**8. Height Restriction**
> "The height of the Menorah should not exceed twenty cubits from the ground, i.e. the lowest part of the lights should be within twenty cubits of the ground."

**20 cubits = approximately 30 feet**

**Measure from:**
- Lowest part of flames
- To ground
- Not top of menorah

---

**9. Real Flames Preferred**
> "Whenever it is possible, one should use real flames for the public Chanukah lights, rather than using electric lights - as a Brocho cannot be recited on electric lights."

**Priority:**
1. Real flames (can make bracha)
2. Electric lights (no bracha)

---

**10. No Bracha on Gas or Butane**
> "Likewise, a Brocho should not be recited on lights which are fueled by gas or butane."

**Fuel types:**
- Kerosene: Yes, bracha
- Gas: No bracha
- Butane: No bracha
- Oil: Yes, bracha

---

**11. Kerosene Lights**
> "Many kindle the public menorah using kerosene lights, and there's sufficient Halachic basis for that."

**Kerosene:**
- Widely used
- Halachically sound
- Bracha permitted
- Practical for outdoors

**12. Minyan Preferred**
> "Effort should be made to kindle the public Menorah in the presence of at least ten people. However, when that is not achieved, a Brocho may nevertheless be recited."

**Ideal:** 10 people (minyan)
**Minimum:** Can do with less, still make bracha

**13. Timing**
> "The earliest time that the Menorah may be lit is Plag Hamincha, but there should be sufficient fuel for the lights to last at least 30 minutes past nightfall."

**Earliest:** Plag HaMincha
**Fuel requirement:** Lasts 30 minutes past nightfall (tzeis)

**14. Gender Separation**
> "As with all public events, effort should be made to achieve gender separation."

**Mechitza:**
- Try to implement
- Seating arrangements
- Separate areas
- As feasible

**15. Daily Lighting**
> "With the menorah it should be let/ turn on every day, and not all at once"

**Important:**
- Light each day's candles on that day
- Not all at once
- Follow actual Chanukah progression
- Educational value
